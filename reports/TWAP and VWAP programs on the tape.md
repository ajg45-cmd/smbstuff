# Programs leave footprints, not readable signatures

Institutional VWAP and TWAP programs are real, slow, mostly passive and mechanically scheduled, and the research proves they leave statistical traces. Nothing in the research shows that a trader watching a public tape can identify one in real time, measure its size, or know when it ends. The typical algorithmic parent order in the best available sample is small and patient (about $287,000 worked over 84 minutes at 4.8% of volume, with fewer than 0.4% of its child orders sent as market orders), about half of US volume prints off-exchange with no side attached, and "VWAP as support" has never been tested in any study the notes could find. Four things are documented well enough to trade around. Flow that persists on one side for minutes to hours is mostly one participant splitting an order. A finished order gives back about a third of its impact by the same day's close. About 30% of institutional parent orders run more than one day and tend to reappear at the same half-hour. Closing-auction price moves revert overnight, while late continuous-session moves mostly do not. The trader's own records agree with this picture. His turn trades fail when a seller is still grinding and work after capitulation. Holding full size to VWAP beat his actual exits by about 0.2R per trade over 77 turns. In low floats, a stock below VWAP has roughly a 1-in-20 chance of another leg. His records contain no logged overnight trade at all, so overnight conviction cannot come from his own data yet. His evidence supports one firm conclusion: his documented leak is selling winners before VWAP and oversizing losers, not overstaying.

**How to read the evidence labels.** This report uses four labels throughout. **Documented** means peer-reviewed research, primary rule text, exchange documentation, or large-sample data. **Practice** means a rule a named trader or firm teaches, with no published test. **Unverified** means folklore or a claim the research could not source. **Own data** means the trader's Notion records, which come from a simulated forward-test account ("acct 204080 (ForwardTest, simulated)") and from replays on one-minute bars, often with small samples ([Monthly Review Oct 2026](https://app.notion.com/p/40b821abbd81810ea134e1120162d42a)).

**Source access limits.** Several sites could not be opened during research and were read only through search-result excerpts. Citations marked † are excerpt-only. This covers all the broker documentation, survey, NBER and SEC/eCFR material on algo mechanics; everything from smbtraining.com, alphatrends.net, and the Zarattini/Aziz/Barbon paper pages; and nyse.com and sec.gov pages on auction rules. The SMB and Brian Shannon books were not read. Rule text for 10b-18 should be checked against eCFR before anyone relies on the exact wording.

## A typical program is small, slow and passive, and its size is invisible

### The schedule is built before the day starts

A VWAP algorithm applies the stock's historical intraday volume curve to the parent order between its start and end time, then works each time bucket mostly with passive limit orders and turns aggressive only when it falls behind. Broker documentation confirms the user-set parameters: start and end time, a maximum percentage of volume, a limit price, whether to keep working past the end time, and whether to join the auctions ([IBKR VWAP](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/index.php?f=1124)†, [IBKR TWS guide](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/software/tws.bak/usersguidebook/algos/vwap.htm)†). The benchmark is often interval VWAP from order arrival to the close, not full-day VWAP, so the session VWAP line on a chart is not necessarily the line the algorithm is measured against ([IBKR VWAP](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/index.php?f=1124)†).

TWAP ignores volume and trades by the clock: "on a 2-hour order the system is 25% done after 30 minutes, 50% done after an hour" and it "does not speed up/slow down based on projected volume or price moves" ([IBKR algo descriptions](https://interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/software/tws.bak/ibalgostop.htm)†). Percent-of-volume algorithms have no schedule and trade a set fraction of whatever prints, with settings from 1% to 50% ([IBKR Percent of Volume](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/trading/orders/percent-of-volume.php)†). Arrival-price algorithms are benchmarked to the mid-quote at submission and front-load according to an urgency setting ([IBKR Arrival Price](https://interactivebrokers.ie/en/trading/orders/arrival-price.php)†). All of this is **Documented** as to mechanism. The exact catch-up rules, tracking-band widths and bucket lengths are proprietary and were not found.

Three inferences follow from the documented parameters, and they are inferences only. A VWAP program is heaviest near the open and into the close, whatever price does. A percent-of-volume program grows exactly when volume surges. A program with a limit price stops lifting when price trades through the limit, which is a plausible reason intraday levels hold until an order is amended. No source measured any of these on a public tape.

### Measured orders are far smaller than desk lore suggests

The largest dataset in the notes covers 2.3 million parent orders and over 300 million child orders from 2012 to 2016. **The average parent tried to trade $287,000 over 84 minutes, equal to 4.80% of volume over its life. Fewer than 0.40% of child orders were market orders and 81.5% were limit orders** ([NBER conference paper](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f145256/f145256.pdf)†). That works out to about 130 child orders per parent. Other institutional samples put average participation at 2.5% and 3.6% ([Management Science supplement](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/mnsc.2023.4871/suppl_file/mnsc.2023.4871.sm1.pdf)†).

This matters for tape reading. The average program does not lift offers; it sits on the bid and waits. The "5 to 20% of volume" figure quoted on desks describes settings chosen for large orders and could not be sourced, so it stays **Unverified**. The survey statistic that 83% of hedge funds use VWAP algorithms counts respondents who use the type at all; it is not a share of flow ([The TRADE HF survey 2026](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Algo-Survey-HF-2026.pdf)†). No source gives the share of institutional volume executed by each algorithm type.

### Impact is concave, and a third of it comes back the same day

Price impact during a large order grows roughly with the square root of its size, so the first part of a program moves price most per share ([Zarinelli et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.2152), [Almgren et al.](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/costestim.pdf)). When the order ends, the price relaxes at once. In more than 8 million US metaorders, **impact at the end of the same day averaged about two-thirds of its peak, and it decayed to about one-third of peak over roughly 50 days** ([Bucci et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.05332)). The same paper found that order direction is autocorrelated across days: a buyer today tends to be followed by buying on later days. This is the strongest **Documented** expectation in the whole subject: when a buyer finishes, expect a partial give-back, not a round trip. These are averages over millions of orders and single-order dispersion is large.

Other participants react in two stages. In European data with identified firms, high-frequency traders first leaned against large institutional orders and later traded with them on the most informed orders ([van Kervel and Menkveld](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:74:y:2019:i:3:p:1091-1137)). That matches the desk story of absorption followed by a grind, but the data identified the participants, which the public tape does not.

### Programs often run more than one day

Reconstructed institutional orders show that **multi-day orders are about 30% of parent orders, and the average parent lasts about 1.38 trading days** ([AEA working paper](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/ibSfSdEb)). A separate, well-replicated result shows that a stock's return in a given half-hour predicts its return in the same half-hour on following days, for about 40 trading days, with volume and order imbalance showing the same rhythm ([Heston, Korajczyk and Sadka](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.3535.pdf)). The authors' reading, that institutions repeat a schedule day after day, is an interpretation. The effect is a small cross-sectional tilt, not a single-stock setup.

### Corporate buybacks are capped, passive and absent at the open and close

The Rule 10b-18 safe harbor shapes buyback flow. A safe-harbor purchase cannot be the opening trade, and issuers of liquid stocks cannot buy in the last 10 minutes of the session (the last 30 minutes for everything else) ([Federal Register, SEC Release 33-8335](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2003/11/17/03-28593/purchases-of-certain-equity-securities-by-the-issuer-and-others)). Daily purchases are capped at 25% of four-week average volume, and bids cannot exceed the highest independent bid or last independent trade ([eCFR 17 CFR 240.10b-18](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-240/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFRbda83517ce4377f/section-240.10b-18)†). Brokers ship dedicated buyback algorithms that enforce these limits ([IBKR API, Jefferies algos](https://interactivebrokers.github.io/tws-api/jefferies.html)†). The rule is **Documented**. The tape consequence is an inference: a buyback is a dip-supporting bid that never initiates an uptick, lives in the body of the day, and is gone by 15:50. Accelerated repurchases are priced at average daily VWAP over the term less a discount, so the bank's covering is itself a multi-week VWAP buy ([Wilson Sonsini](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/accelerated-share-repurchase-asr-programs-to-be-impacted-by-proposed-changes-to-rule-10b5-1-and-share-repurchase-disclosure-rules.html)†). Buyback plans can keep running through earnings blackouts under 10b5-1 instructions, and one practitioner study found no return difference for heavy-buyback firms in blackout periods ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2019/01/buyback-blackout-periods-do-not-negatively-impact-market-performance/)). "Buyback blackout means weakness" is **Unverified**.

## Half the tape is dark, and the visible half proves less than it seems

### What is established and what is not

The table sets each signature a tape reader might use against what the research supports.

| Signature | What is established | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Same-side aggressor flow persisting for minutes to hours | Trade signs have long memory; under a few hours the persistence is "overwhelmingly due to splitting rather than herding" ([Tóth et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.1632v1)) | Documented that it exists. No public-tape detector has been validated |
| Partial give-back when the buyer finishes | About one-third of peak impact reverts by the same close ([Bucci et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.05332)) | Documented |
| Prints at regular clock intervals | Some algorithms cycle in wall-clock time ([Hasbrouck and Saar](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1695460)†). VWAP child orders are measurably predictable in broker data ([Order anticipation paper](https://www.scribd.com/document/462910636/SSRN-id2828363-pdf)) | Documented with broker data. Not shown on public data |
| Odd-lot slicing | Real, but odd lots were 64.4% of all trades in February 2025 ([MEMX](https://memx.com/insights/equity-adv-option-contracts-and-odd-lot-trades-all-up-year-over-year)) | Documented, and useless as a discriminator |
| Reloading or iceberg bid | Reserve orders exist. Display size can be randomised and each refill goes to the back of the queue ([Nasdaq RASH spec](https://m.nasdaqtrader.com/content/technicalsupport/specifications/Tradingproducts/rash_sb_ETMF.pdf), [SEC 34-91109](https://www.sec.gov/file/34-91109)) | Mechanism documented. Detection is unreliable |
| Clean held bid above a key level | SMB desk pattern, described by Bellafiore as rare ([SMB](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/the-clean-held-bids-matter-most)†) | Practice. No hit rate published |
| Price holding above VWAP means a buy program, or VWAP acts as support | Follows from arithmetic. No empirical test was found | Unverified as a level. Plausible as a mechanism |
| TICK beyond ±1000 means a program | Mechanically shows many stocks last-printing on one side. Thresholds untested ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/tick-index/)) | Unverified as a predictor |
| Delta, or buy versus sell volume | Classification rules get 76% to 81% of trades right and fail inside the quote ([Ellis, Michaely and O'Hara](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/accuracy-of-trade-classification-rulesevidence-from-nasdaq/A2CAD2206B562784527548F5283A9ECB)) | Documented as a limitation |

The closest thing to direct evidence of detectability is the study of more than 20,000 VWAP parent orders, which built predictability signals from child-order statistics and stated that the patterns "can be even discerned by human traders" ([Order anticipation paper](https://www.scribd.com/document/462910636/SSRN-id2828363-pdf)). That study used the broker's own child-order records and measured the cost of being predictable. It did not measure a public-tape reader's hit rate. No study in the notes reports the precision of any program detector built on public data.

The VWAP point deserves plain statement. With persistent net buying and rising impact, each later print sits above most earlier prints, so last price sits above the cumulative average. **Price above a rising VWAP is a consequence of persistent buying. It is not evidence that buyers defend the VWAP line**, and any steady drift produces the same picture. The only tested VWAP rules found are author-run backtests on index ETFs. The long-above, short-below rule on QQQ reported a Sharpe ratio of 2.1 for 2018 to 2023 but assumed no slippage ([SSRN 4631351](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4631351)†), and an independent review found it breaks even at one tick of slippage ([Telonics](https://telonicstrading.com/research-vwap.html)†). No credible test of VWAP standard-deviation bands was found.

### What cannot be detected from the public tape

Off-exchange trading was **50.6% of consolidated volume in 2025** ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/2025-u-s-equities-year-in-review)). Those trades print up to 10 seconds after execution ([FINRA Notice 13-19](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/13-19)) with no displayed quote beforehand. The dark share is highest in the middle of the day ([Nasdaq](https://nasdaq.com/articles/its-darkest-middle-day)†) and falls sharply in the final minutes ([IEX](https://www.iex.io/article/closing-time-you-dont-have-to-go-home-but-you-might-want-to-trade-here-end-of-day-trading)†). A print between the bid and ask is consistent with either side initiating.

The following cannot be determined from public data: the identity or type of the participant; the total size or the quantity remaining; whether the flow is one parent order or several; whether the benchmark is VWAP, TWAP, percent-of-volume or arrival price; the end time; and whether a held level is one order or many unrelated ones. The lit-tape footprint of a program is a biased lower bound on its activity. A large-cap stock can also show "buy program" tape that is index basket flow and nothing stock-specific.

## The day runs on two closing clocks and a growing auction

### Volume has moved to the close

The closing auction was 3.1% of daily US dollar volume in 2010 and 7.5% in 2018, and the last five minutes of continuous trading held another 6.96% ([Bogousslavsky and Muravyev](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7)). By December 2025 the auction was 9.74% of total volume on one measure ([Rosenblatt](https://www.rblt.com/market-structure-reports/us-securities-volumes-2025-year-in-review)) and 15.39% on a narrower one, with a further 33.85% in the last two continuous hours ([SSGA, citing Instinet](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/institutional/insights/how-passive-investing-reshaping-microstructure)). The two auction figures differ by denominator, and about 10% of consolidated volume is the working number. On index-event days the auction share is about 20%, and above 25% on Russell reconstitution ([IBKR podcast](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/podcasts/ibkr-podcasts/is-your-stock-about-to-russell-up/)). No reliable recent table of first-30-minute, midday and last-30-minute volume shares was found. The blog figures in circulation conflict and give no method.

Two forces oppose each other into the bell. Hedging flow pushes with the day's move at the index level, while de-risking and auction demand create pressure that reverts. Direction into the close is therefore more reliable for the index than for a single stock, and a stock-level study reports end-of-day reversal for individual names ([Notre Dame working paper](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/EOD.pdf)).

### The auction print reverts; the last five minutes mostly do not

The average closing-auction price deviates from the pre-close midpoint by 8.1 basis points, and on 1% of stock-days by more than 0.63% ([Bogousslavsky and Muravyev](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7)). **Those deviations revert by half shortly after the close and fully overnight**, and price moves of similar size in the last five minutes of continuous trading reverse about five times less ([Bogousslavsky and Muravyev](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v66y2023ics1386418123000502.html)). The late NYSE D Order trend means the 15:50 imbalance is a progressively less complete picture. One calendar item needs checking: Nasdaq's extended-hours schedule was expected to start on 6 December 2026, and its status was not confirmed ([Arnold & Porter](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2026/04/29/arnold-porter-discusses-sec-approval-of-nasdaq-proposal-to-expand-trading-hours/)).

### Overnight belongs to a different crowd

Across 14 strategies, profits accrue either entirely overnight or entirely intraday, usually with opposite signs, and a stock's intraday returns continue into later intraday returns while overnight returns continue into overnight returns ([Lou, Polk and Skouras](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/87481)). Combined with the same-half-hour result, the supported expectation is "program buying today means more intraday buying tomorrow at the same clock times". It is not "strong close means gap up". **No rigorous study was found showing that a close near the high on heavy volume predicts next-day continuation.** That claim stays **Unverified**.

## His own records show the same split: capitulation pays, grinds punish

### How programs appear in his vocabulary and data

VWAP plays three roles in his book: the target of the turn trade, the floor and trail of continuation and relative-strength trades, and the reclaim trigger in low floats ([Turn Trade](https://app.notion.com/p/3f8821abbd818198bb2bca504f2b60e9), [November Playbook](https://app.notion.com/p/40b821abbd81819da8bad63827ca710e)). He already uses program language, such as names "holding the 9 EMA like a sell program (PEP, NEM) = no size" and DDOG being "kinda just passive sell program". His evidence base is one-minute bars and discretionary tape reads. No page models participation rates or schedules, and his IBM case study flags that Level 2, print imbalance and the exact VWAP line "were not pulled" ([IBM 7/16 case study](https://app.notion.com/p/3a0821abbd8181a8a9c2e892053e7cb8)). Every "program" in his records is therefore inferred from bar shape, which is as far as the public-tape research says anyone can go.

### The turn trade works after the seller finishes

His turn rules line up with the documented mechanics. A turn is a bet that a flow has ended and that its temporary impact will partly revert. His losing condition is the documented signature of an order still working.

His 2-year backtest shows a clean run that never tags the 2-minute 9 EMA at +0.24R against −0.29R for moves riding the 9 EMA ([Turn Grading study](https://app.notion.com/p/401821abbd81810aa834c7c408c2bfc0)). In his 95-turn sample, relative volume below 1.2 produced a 35% win rate and −0.38R, and only 9% of those turns reached VWAP ([RVOL study](https://app.notion.com/p/418821abbd8181f3a591d2d696db240e), [Hold to VWAP study](https://app.notion.com/p/418821abbd8181e9ab6ff7b5b9adb397)). Counter-trend turns on relative volume of 1.2 or more went 59% and +0.49R over 38 trades. SMB's own threshold says a stock with relative volume under 1 is not in play ([SMB RVOL](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/relative-volume-rvol-defined-and-how-we-use-it)†), which is **Practice** agreeing with his **Own data**. His page marks the RVOL result as directional only (p ≈ 0.12).

AMZN is not an isolated case. **Of the 33 turns that closed beyond VWAP, 20 stopped him out first** ([Hold to VWAP study](https://app.notion.com/p/418821abbd8181e9ab6ff7b5b9adb397)). The documented fact that order direction persists for hours explains why the first flush extreme often breaks before the real turn. His rule "stopped but not invalidated = re-enter" is the correct response to that, as long as size allows a second attempt. His SVXY lesson says the same: "you cant really risk A+ of your stop on one clip have to allow yourself reentry."

One tension in his own data needs a recheck. His checklist asks for a waterfall of 1.5 ATR or more from VWAP, yet in the 95-turn sample moves of 1 to 1.5 ATR won 75% (+0.26R, n=20) and moves above 1.5 ATR won 39% (−0.27R, n=23). Slow slopes beat steep ones, and his backtest marks "more stretched = better" as not backed, with longs at 3 or more standard deviations from VWAP the worst bucket ([RVOL study](https://app.notion.com/p/418821abbd8181f3a591d2d696db240e), [Turn Grading study](https://app.notion.com/p/401821abbd81810aa834c7c408c2bfc0)). The two measures differ (size of the run versus distance from VWAP), so this is a flag, not a contradiction.

### Fast snaps fade; the slow-grind rule rests on four trades

His hold-to-VWAP study is the direct answer to his own question about when to hold. Over 77 turns, his actual exits averaged +0.02R. Selling everything at VWAP averaged +0.21R. Holding everything to the close averaged +0.05R with a 24% win rate ([Hold to VWAP study](https://app.notion.com/p/418821abbd8181e9ab6ff7b5b9adb397)). The gain is concentrated: model turns made +1.75R held to VWAP against +0.85R on his exits, and low-quality turns lost either way.

| Time taken to reach VWAP | Trades | All out at VWAP | Half at VWAP, piece to close | A piece helped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 minutes or less | 17 | +1.83R | +1.62R | 24% of the time |
| 30 to 90 minutes | 4 | +3.04R | +3.72R | 75% |
| More than 90 minutes | 10 | +3.58R | +3.28R | 30% |

The fast-snap result is consistent with the documented finding that temporary imbalances lasting under an hour reverse. The rule "a grind to VWAP means the day is reversing, keep a piece" rests on four trades, and the bucket above 90 minutes does not support it. Treat it as a hypothesis until the planned recheck at about 150 turns. The study page says the same.

### One-sided VWAP days exist, and VWAP is the wrong trail on some of them

His recurring fingerprint for a one-way day is nearly every bar closing on one side of VWAP: MRNA 9/1 (73 of 74 five-minute bars above after 09:50), SPCX 7/28 (353 of 390), DKS 8/26 (388 of 390), and MSFT 7/20 (no minute below after the 10:11 reclaim) ([MRNA 9/1](https://app.notion.com/p/3ce821abbd8181638d9ad0bc6a4440fb), [Trend day](https://app.notion.com/p/38d821abbd818181b17acc0e3a4ea4af), [Day 2](https://app.notion.com/p/38d821abbd8181cfbdb8ff94ac1483e9)). The arithmetic above explains why that fingerprint is honest but late: it confirms persistent buying has already happened. It does not say VWAP will hold.

His trail simulations show the consequence. On MRNA 9/1, holding above VWAP captured 95.4% of the move while every EMA trail lost money. On CRWV 9/8, VWAP captured 43% and the 5-minute 21 EMA captured 77%, because price ran so far above VWAP that the stop gave back most of the trade. On CRM 8/28, 86.2% of the range printed in the first 30 minutes and holding to the close lost 2.10R. Each is a single day, and his pages say so.

His study of 336 relative-strength leader days found that the VWAP pullback-and-hold "does NOT predict the HOD close" (AUC 0.51), while distance above VWAP (0.72) and position in the day's range (0.73) at 1:00 PM do ([RS-Leader template](https://app.notion.com/p/387821abbd8181ed822dc28022fcedfc)). That is his own data confirming the general finding: the level is not the signal, the persistence is.

IBM 7/16 is his clearest "buy program" day. It showed a capitulation open, a "relentless, one-directional VWAP-anchored grind", relative volume of about 3.2 times, IBM up 3.8% against a Nasdaq down 1.5%, and 2.57 million shares in the last five minutes and the close ([IBM 7/16 case study](https://app.notion.com/p/3a0821abbd8181a8a9c2e892053e7cb8)). The bar shape is consistent with a full-day parent order. Whether it was one buyer, a buyback, or short covering is unknowable from his data. He has no fill of his own on that day, and several of his best trend-day cases (IBM, SPCX 7/28, MSFT 7/20) were missed or abandoned. On MSFT he was flat 18 minutes before the VWAP reclaim and took 8.2% of a 12.99R move.

### Low floats: VWAP is a state, not an entry

Across 133 sessions, a low-float stock sitting more than one standard deviation above VWAP with under 37% retraced made a new high within 30 minutes about 60% of the time, against a base rate near 20%. A stock below VWAP did so 7% to 8% of the time ([Low-float VWAP study](https://app.notion.com/p/3fb821abbd8181a1b2abfad24c21d8d2)). Yet every mechanical long built on the hold was negative in expectation, and adding back after a flush lost in every variant (−0.07R to −0.24R). The study's conclusion is the tiering rule: "Use the hold read to stay in or size up a winner you already have, not as an entry signal." His CLRO 11/2 loss, "adding under vwap and not on right side of V", is that rule broken ([11/2 notes](https://app.notion.com/p/40c821abbd81802f9d88d940e6d56806)). His low-float trades on days with $500 million or more of dollar volume were the only profitable bucket (33 trades, profit factor 2.65) ([Best Ops Review](https://app.notion.com/p/40a821abbd8180178d1ed97814d9988e)).

### His reviews point at conviction and size, not at overstaying

October's 297 round trips lost $9,182 with a profit factor of 0.53. Trades held 20 minutes or more made money in July, September and October, while trades closed within 20 minutes lost about $10,500 each month. His ten biggest losers carried 2.6 times the notional of his ten biggest winners ([Monthly Review Oct 2026](https://app.notion.com/p/40b821abbd81810ea134e1120162d42a)). Entries before 09:45 lost in all three months, and 11:30 to 14:00 was October's only green window. Turns entered after 11:30 averaged +0.80R on his exits (n=17). That runs against SMB's published tiering advice of most size on the open and least at midday ([SMB Tier Size](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/tier-size)†). On this question his own data should outrank the general practice.

### Overnight: he has no evidence of his own

His records contain no dated overnight position with fills and a result. The turn is "intraday scalp only", and October's after-hours entries went 1 for 9 and lost $812 ([November Playbook](https://app.notion.com/p/40b821abbd81819da8bad63827ca710e)). Neither is tested.

## An evidence-rated framework by time of day

### How to follow a program without pretending to see it

Following a program means acting on what persistence implies, and on nothing more. While one-sided flow persists, do not fade it; the turn comes after capitulation, which is both documented (splitting persists for hours) and his own data. Join in the direction of the flow only on proof: a higher low or reclaim that holds, never an add below VWAP on a long. Expect a give-back of about a third of the move when the flow stops, so a volume fade with a weaker bid is an exit cue and not a dip to buy. Treat a held bid as an entry with a two-stage exit, which is SMB's practice: the dropped bid is the exit, and a fast rebid at the same price is the re-entry ([SMB PlayBook post](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/the-smb-playbook-compiling-our-best-trades)†). Size goes up as proof accumulates, which SMB teaches as tiers 1 to 4 with VWAP as a guide ([SMB tip](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/smbu-trading-tip-of-the-week-wwe)†) and which his own notes state as "size follows proof, not hope".

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. He cannot know that a program is present, how large it is, or when it ends, and no practitioner source offers a tested way to do so. He can know which state he is in. Persistent one-sided flow is documented to continue for hours and to come partly back when it stops, so conviction should attach to observable transitions: capitulation volume followed by a confirmed 2-minute turn, a reclaim that holds for the next bar, or a volume fade after a long grind. His own numbers already rank those transitions, and they say the cheapest improvement is holding model turns to VWAP at full size while refusing size on low-volume grinds.

Two gaps are worth closing with his own data, because the literature will not close them. The slow-grind rule needs the 150-turn recheck before it carries size. Overnight needs a deliberate small-size log, since neither the research nor his records support a strong-close rule today. A third test is cheap and directly targets the best-documented multi-day effect: for names that showed a one-sided VWAP day, record whether the same half-hour buckets are bid again the next session.
