## 1. IPO lock-ups: terms, early releases, SPAC/direct listing variants, how much unlocks

### Takeaway
The 180-day lock-up binding essentially all pre-IPO holders is still the norm, but it is a contract, not a rule, and since 2019-2021 many larger deals carry early-release clauses (earnings/blackout pull-forwards, price triggers, staggered tranches), so the calendar date on a lock-up site is frequently wrong and the real date often lands right after an earnings report. The amount unlocking is commonly several times the trading float (reported cases: ~82-90% of shares outstanding).

### Cited Findings
- [practice] Typical lock-up is "usually 180 days after the IPO"; most newly public companies set a single expiration date; if the date falls on a weekend/holiday, the agreement's definition of the effective trading day governs (search summary of several explainer pages) — [StockAnalysis lockup calendar](https://stockanalysis.com/ipos/lockups/), [JPMorgan Private Bank](https://privatebank.jpmorgan.com/nam/en/o/ipo-timeline-and-lock-up-period-what-you-should-know)
- [practice] Fenwick survey (mostly 2019 IPOs): 14% of respondents' lock-ups allowed early release when the expiration fell inside a trading blackout; price-based releases were rarer, with the usual trigger a stock price about 33% above the IPO price — [NASPP blog summarising Fenwick](https://www.naspp.com/blog/Are-IPO-Lockup-Periods-Negotiable-Maybe-25506)
- [practice] Mayer Brown "Market Trends 2024/2025: Lock-Up Agreements": the 2020-2021 IPO boom brought more flexible lock-ups (performance-based releases, staggered schedules, shorter terms for non-executive employees); those features became less common afterwards and "could return as conditions improve" — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/market-trends-2024-2025-lock-up-agreements)
- [unverified] Equibles review of 440 IPO prospectuses 2024-2026: six months still the norm, but the largest deals increasingly release shares early (statistic detail not retrieved) — [Equibles](https://equibles.com/research/who-is-actually-locked-up-after-an-ipo)
- [practice] Structures in current use: staggered releases spreading insider sales across the first six months, and blackout pull-forwards letting a lock-up expire early when it would otherwise land inside a blackout — [Lexology, "Early Lock-Up Releases: Overview and Trends"](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=994f3a80-3071-48e3-bdfb-5def2fd7108b)
- [documented] Brav & Gompers (2003, RFS), 2,794 IPOs: lock-up length supports a commitment-device explanation (more moral-hazard-prone firms lock up longer); insiders of firms with larger excess returns, VC backing or high-quality underwriters are more likely to be released early — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v16y2003i1p1-29.html)
- [documented] Kelly, Lv & Twite (Univ. of Melbourne WP 2024-05) study insider sales during the lock-up and contractual provisions allowing automatic early release; early releases tend to occur after information asymmetry is resolved — [working paper](https://fbe.unimelb.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/5175618/UoMFinanceWP_2024_05.pdf)
- [practice] CoreWeave (IPO 28 Mar 2025): lock-up ended 14 Aug 2025, about six weeks before the scheduled 24 Sep date (earnings-linked release); 83% of Class A shares became tradeable — [EquiLend](https://equilend.com/?p=51466)
- [practice] Circle (IPO June 2025): J.P. Morgan released 11.5 million shares as of 13 Aug 2025 under an early-release provision after the stock traded 15% above the IPO price; the remaining insider shares stayed under the standard 180-day lock-up — [EquiLend](https://equilend.com/?p=51466)
- [practice] Figma (IPO July 2025): company disclosed with its first earnings report that an early release of 25% of eligible securities held by certain employees/service providers was likely to be triggered that week, tradeable 5 Sep 2025 — [Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/markets/figma-plunges-after-first-earnings-since-ipo-lock-up-expires-some-shareholders/)
- [practice] Arm (IPO Sept 2023): only 9.5% of shares outstanding had been available to trade since the IPO; roughly 90% owned by SoftBank; lock-up expired 12 Mar 2024 — [Reuters via Yahoo](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/arms-shares-rise-wall-street-194944362.html)
- [practice] Reddit (IPO Mar 2024): an analyst put ~82% of shares (~133M) as subject to the 9 Aug 2024 lock-up expiration — [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/27548628/why-reddit-stock-looks-too-risky-for-one-analyst-ahead-of-august-9)
- [practice] SPAC sponsor founder shares: typical lock-up is one year from de-SPAC closing, with early termination if the stock trades above a fixed price (usually $12.00) for 20 of 30 trading days beginning 150 days after closing — [Harvard Law School Forum, "SPACs: An Introduction"](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2018/07/06/special-purpose-acquisition-companies-an-introduction/), [LexisNexis market trends: de-SPAC](https://www.lexisnexis.com/community/insights/legal/practical-guidance-journal/b/pa/posts/market-trends-de-spac-transactions)
- [practice] De-SPAC PIPE shares: the company must file a resale shelf on Form S-1/F-1 because it cannot use Form S-3 for 12 months after its Form 10 information is filed; PIPE holders cannot sell publicly until that registration is effective — [Orrick, June 2025](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/06/SPAC-Re-Attack-Top-Considerations-for-Issuers-Considering-a-De-SPAC-Transaction)
- [documented] PIPE investors in de-SPACs lose roughly 45% within two years (Avci, Schipani & Seyhun, Iowa Law Review 2026) — [Iowa Law Review](https://ilr.law.uiowa.edu/volume-111-issue-2/need-regulation-private-equity-evidence-de-spac-transactions)

### Inferences
- With earnings-linked releases, the "unlock day" and the "first earnings reaction" increasingly coincide (CoreWeave, Figma, Circle), so the price move on that date cannot be cleanly attributed to supply. Any backtest of "unlock day" returns on 2020s large IPOs is contaminated by earnings.
- For a de-SPAC, three separate supply dates matter: (a) PIPE resale S-1 effectiveness (often weeks after closing), (b) non-sponsor holder lock-up (often ~180 days), (c) sponsor lock-up (1 year or the $12 trigger). The first is the earliest and is not on most lock-up calendars.
- Float multiplier, not the percent drop quoted in old studies, is the more useful sizing variable: Arm (9.5% float) and Reddit (~18% float) both had 5-10x the float become eligible on one day.

### Gaps
- No 2021-specific or 2024-2026 statistic for the share of IPOs with early-release provisions was retrieved (Mayer Brown and Equibles pages could not be opened).
- Direct listings: no source retrieved. Recollection, not verified: direct listings have no underwriter-imposed lock-up by default (Spotify 2018, Slack 2019, Coinbase 2021 had none), while Palantir (2020) voluntarily imposed a partial lock-up allowing only ~20% of holdings to be sold until after its first full-year results.
- Underwriter waivers: no source retrieved. Recollection, not verified: FINRA Rule 5131 requires the book-runner to announce a waiver/release for officers and directors by press release or news service at least two business days before it takes effect, so discretionary releases for insiders are visible in advance; releases for non-insiders need not be announced.
- Follow-on lock-ups (re-lock of insiders for 30-90 days after a secondary) not sourced this session.
- Typical percentage of shares locked: academic average not retrieved. Recollection, not verified: Field & Hanka report that the IPO floats a minority of shares and most of the remainder is locked.

## 2. Measured price effect around lock-up expiration

### Takeaway
The best-documented number is still Field & Hanka's roughly -1.5% three-day abnormal return and a permanent ~40% rise in volume (1988-1997 sample), concentrated in VC-backed firms; later samples through 2022 still show a negative average for VC-backed names but weaker or insignificant effects elsewhere, and some studies place the decline in the days before the date. It is an average of a wide distribution and is small relative to the daily range of a recent IPO, with high and spiking borrow costs standing between the statistic and a trading profit.

### Cited Findings
- [documented] Field & Hanka (2001, Journal of Finance 56(2):471-500): at lock-up expiration, a permanent 40% increase in average trading volume and a statistically prominent three-day abnormal return of -1.5%; effects are much larger for VC-financed firms; VCs sell more aggressively than executives and other holders; limited support for several explanations, no complete one — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v56y2001i2p471-500.html)
- [documented] Field & Hanka sample: 1,948 firms, 1988-1997 (per later literature review); the abnormal return is described as permanent with no rebound in subsequent weeks; the abnormal return is significantly negative even when no insider sales are reported — [Nowak 2004 review](https://folia.unifr.ch/documents/317901/files/nowak_AFE_2004.pdf)
- [documented] Venture-backed concentration: "Venture Capital and IPO Lockup Expiration" (Bradley, Jordan, Roten, Yi 2001) finds the losses concentrated in firms with VC backing (exact CAR not retrieved) — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/271626975_VENTURE_CAPITAL_AND_IPO_LOCKUP_EXPIRATION_AN_EMPIRICAL_ANALYSIS)
- [documented] Ofek & Richardson NYU working paper on lock-up expiration: treats it as a known event and asks why the decline is not arbitraged away; larger declines in stocks that are harder to short and with wider bid-ask spreads — [NYU archive](https://archive.nyu.edu/bitstream/2451/27072/2/wpa99054.pdf)
- [documented] Flynn & Tashjian-style sample of 882 lock-ups, 2000-2006: permanent volume increase and negative abnormal return, but the effect was weaker after 2001 — [Univ. of Utah thesis](https://collections.lib.utah.edu/details?id=417378)
- [documented, thesis-level] Aalto thesis, 2,428 US issuers 2003-2022: significant negative abnormal returns and higher volume around expiration; stronger for VC-backed; no similar effect for growth-capital backing; a significant *positive* abnormal return for buyout-backed issuers — [Aalto](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/05aae052-991d-4947-8363-6b2a5df9fb89)
- [documented] One US study found no significant drop on the expiration date itself but significant cumulative abnormal returns in the days leading up to it — [Emerald, "Market reaction to the expiration of IPO lockup provisions"](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/03074350410768859/full/html)
- [documented] Gibbs & Hao (2018, Journal of Banking & Finance): first daily short-selling study around lock-up expirations; short selling rises before expiration and falls afterwards, higher for VC- and PE-backed IPOs; in VC/PE-backed firms pre-expiration shorting predicts ownership reduction but **not** later returns; in other IPOs it does predict post-expiration returns — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v88y2018icp30-43.html)
- [documented] A related short-selling paper reports daily abnormal trading *volume* is not significantly positive until the expiration date itself — [SWFA 2014 submission](https://mediacast.blob.core.windows.net/production/Faculty/StoweConf/submissions/swfa2014_submission_184.pdf)
- [documented] Early-release announcements: Keasler (2001) finds significant negative abnormal returns at the early-release announcement, and the usual scheduled-expiration drop is reduced for firms that announced early release — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02744524)
- [documented] Managers show positive abnormal accruals in the quarter before and the quarter of lock-up expiration (earnings managed up into the unlock) — [NYU Stern, Ertimur et al.](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/ERTIMUR.pdf)
- [practice] Borrow: new-borrow fees briefly exceeded 70% (Peloton) and 95% (SmileDirectClub) ahead of early-2020 expiries; Peloton fell 17% on expiry day but shorts who paid those fees roughly broke even at best; Tilray rose more than 24% into its expiry as shorts covered — [S&P Global Market Intelligence](https://spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/mi/research-analysis/borrow-costs-spike-ahead-of-preipo-lockup-expiries.html), [S&P Global, short selling recent IPOs](https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/mi/research-analysis/short-selling-recent-ipos.html)
- [practice] Arm, 12 Mar 2024: stock **rose** 2.1% to $129.50 on expiry day; it had slid the prior day (11 Mar) on anticipation — [Reuters via Yahoo](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/arms-shares-rise-wall-street-194944362.html), [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2024/03/11/why-arm-holdings-stock-was-sliding-today)
- [practice] Rivian, lock-up ended weekend of 7-8 May 2022: stock fell ~17-22% on Monday 9 May (sources differ by time of measurement) after CNBC reported Ford would sell; Ford sold 8 million shares at $26.90 — [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2022/05/09/why-rivian-automotive-stock-crashed-monday), [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.in/stock-market/news/rivian-plunges-19-as-ford-and-other-investors-to-sell-millions-of-shares-as-lock-up-period-expires/articleshow/91444175.cms)
- [practice] CoreWeave, Aug 2025: 35% two-day decline as the lock-up lifted, also attributed to weak quarterly results; insiders sold more than $1 billion afterwards, with JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley handling several block sales — [EquiLend](https://equilend.com/?p=51466), [Bloomberg](https://news.bgov.com/capital-markets/coreweave-insiders-sold-1-billion-post-ipo-as-ai-rich-cash-out), [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/coreweave-post-ipo-lockup-share-liquidations-begin-as-jpmorgan-goldman-sachs-and-morgan-stanley-handle-several-block-sales/)
- [practice] Figma, 4 Sep 2025: shares down as much as 16% after first earnings plus the early-release notice; weak guidance also cited — [Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/markets/figma-plunges-after-first-earnings-since-ipo-lock-up-expires-some-shareholders/)
- [practice, conflicting] Circle: mid-November 2025 articles say the lock-up was to end two days after Q3 earnings (earnings on 14 Nov); another outlet gives 5 Dec 2025. Stock was already ~68% below its June peak (about $82 vs $298) before the expiry — [Nasdaq/Motley Fool](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/why-circle-internet-group-stock-sank-20-week), [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/circle-stock-faces-pressure-as-rate-cuts-lock-up-expiry-loom/)

### Inferences
- "Does the drop happen before, on or after?" The academic answer is mixed: Field & Hanka centre it on a three-day window at the date with no rebound; at least one US study and the short-selling evidence put the price pressure in the run-up, with volume only jumping on the date. A reasonable reading is that price moves ahead of and into the date while the actual share flow starts on the date and persists.
- The permanent +40% volume means the event is a regime change in liquidity, not a one-day spike. There is no documented systematic "bounce after".
- Tradeability: -1.5% over three days is well inside one day's range for a six-month-old IPO, borrow fees spike exactly when the trade is wanted, and Gibbs & Hao find shorts in VC/PE-backed names did not earn subsequent returns. The effect is real on average and not a reliable standalone trade.
- Big recent cases cut both ways (Arm up, Rivian/CoreWeave/Figma down hard), and the large drops all had a second cause (named holder selling a block, or earnings). Named-seller headlines and same-week earnings look more informative than the expiry date itself.
- Buyout-backed IPOs showing positive abnormal returns suggests backing type should be checked before assuming direction.

### Gaps
- Exact CARs from Brav & Gompers (2003), Bradley et al. (2001) and Ofek & Richardson (2000) were not retrievable. Recollection, not verified: Brav & Gompers and Ofek & Richardson report drops in the 1-3% range around expiry and Bradley et al. roughly -1.6% overall with the loss concentrated in VC-backed, high-tech, high run-up names.
- No peer-reviewed study retrieved that directly tests decay of the effect through the 2010s-2020s, or that isolates earnings-triggered / price-triggered early unlocks in the 2020-2021 cohort.
- No Renaissance Capital or Nasdaq quantitative piece on recent unlock returns was found.
- How many days volume stays abnormally high beyond "permanent" (day-by-day profile) not retrieved.

## 3. How unlocked holders actually sell

### Takeaway
The legal plumbing is well documented (Rule 144 volume caps and Form 144 for affiliates, 10b5-1 cooling-off, sell-to-cover carve-out); the execution style (VWAP/TWAP over N days) is not documented in anything retrieved. What the evidence does show is that the largest holders use bank-run blocks and VC distributions-in-kind rather than dribbling shares on the tape, and that VC distributions are followed by negative returns for months.

### Cited Findings
- [documented] Rule 144 volume limit for affiliates: sales in any three-month period may not exceed the greater of 1% of the class outstanding or the average weekly trading volume over the four weeks preceding the Form 144 filing; affiliates must also meet current-public-information, manner-of-sale and notice conditions — [ABA Business Lawyer, Rule 144 Opinions Report, Winter 2026](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-lawyer/2026-winter/rule-144-opinions-report)
- [documented] Form 144 is required when an affiliate's proposed Rule 144 sales in a three-month period exceed 5,000 shares or $50,000 (threshold set by the 2007 amendments, Release 33-8869) — [SEC small-entity guide](https://www.sec.gov/info/smallbus/secg/rules144-145-secg.htm), [Federal Register 2007](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2007/12/17/07-6013/revisions-to-rules-144-and-145)
- [documented] Until 2023 Form 144 was filed on paper; it must now be filed electronically on EDGAR for reporting issuers, so the notices are searchable same-day — [ABA Business Lawyer](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-lawyer/2026-winter/rule-144-opinions-report)
- [documented] 10b5-1 amendments (adopted Dec 2022, effective 2023): directors and officers may not trade under a new or modified plan until the later of 90 days after adoption or two business days after the 10-Q/10-K for the quarter of adoption, capped at 120 days; other persons (not the issuer) 30 days; a plan amendment counts as a new plan and restarts the clock; no cooling-off for issuers; no hardship exception; D&Os must certify no MNPI — [SEC Release 33-11138](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf), [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/sec-adopts-amendments-rule-10b5-1), [Simpson Thacher](https://stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2022/12/16/sec-amends-rules-relating-to-rule-10b5-1-trading-plans-and-imposes-additional-disclosure-requirements), [Cravath](https://cravath.com/a/web/3LHUAAAxr6hd91zX1MZj9b/7Lfikf/the_secs_new_10b5-1_rules.pdf)
- [practice] Simpson Thacher: when plans are adopted in normal quarterly windows the cooling-off will generally be 90 days — [Simpson Thacher](https://stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2022/12/16/sec-amends-rules-relating-to-rule-10b5-1-trading-plans-and-imposes-additional-disclosure-requirements)
- [documented] Sell-to-cover: plans used solely to sell shares to cover tax withholding on RSU/restricted-stock vesting are exempt from the overlapping-plan and single-trade-plan limits; the cooling-off period still applies; SEC staff read "necessary to satisfy tax withholding" as not limited to minimum withholding — [NASPP](https://naspp.com/blog/Rule-10b5-1-Amendments), [BCLP on SEC staff interpretations](https://bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/sec-staff-updates-rule-10b5-1-interpretations.html)
- [practice] RSU mechanics: award agreements may delay settlement until a trading window opens if net-share withholding is not used; many employers require Section 16 officers to elect standing sell-to-cover for all RSU grants; some issuers write mandatory automatic open-market sell-to-cover into the award terms — [DLA Piper, Mar 2025](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2025/03/tax-considerations-for-public-company-equity-incentive-awards)
- [documented] Gompers & Lerner (1998, Journal of Finance), VC distributions to LPs: distributions follow large run-ups, produce a substantial price reaction immediately around the event, and returns continue to be negative in the months after; more negative for lower-quality underwriters and less seasoned firms; consistent with VCs timing distributions on inside information — [abstract listing](https://econbiz.de/Record/venture-capital-distributions-short-run-and-long-run-reactions-gompers-paul/10005691887)
- [practice] Funds usually mark distributed shares at the trading price on the distribution date or a trailing/centered average (one LPA uses a ten-day window centered on the distribution date); carry is computed at distribution value so LPs bear the post-distribution decline — [Hirschler](https://www.hirschlerlaw.com/newsroom-publications-stock-distribution-pricing-timing-is-everything), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2012/10/19/solving-venture-capitals-distribution-dilemma/)
- [practice] Facebook's first lock-up expiry, Aug 2012: stock down 6.27% to $19.87 on the day — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2012/08/17/the-facebook-distribution-dilemma)
- [practice] Block route in practice: Ford's 8M-share Rivian block at $26.90 immediately after expiry; CoreWeave holders used several bank-run blocks — [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2022/05/09/why-rivian-automotive-stock-crashed-monday), [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/coreweave-post-ipo-lockup-share-liquidations-begin-as-jpmorgan-goldman-sachs-and-morgan-stanley-handle-several-block-sales/)
- [practice] Individual insider selling shows up in Form 4s within days: a Reddit director sold 64,000 Class A shares between 9 and 13 Aug 2024, starting on expiry day — [Investing.com](https://investing.com/news/company-news/reddit-director-gale-mary-porter-sells-over-34-million-in-company-stock-93CH-3570456)

### Inferences
- Rule 144's cap is not binding for a hot recent IPO: "greater of 1% of outstanding or one average week of volume" per three months, per affiliate, is a large number when weekly volume is high. Non-affiliates (most employees and many early investors) are outside the cap altogether. The cap matters mainly in illiquid small caps.
- Because a D&O 10b5-1 plan now needs ~90 days of cooling-off, a plan intended to sell at the unlock must be adopted roughly a quarter earlier; plan adoptions are disclosed in the 10-Q/10-K, giving advance notice of programmatic insider selling.
- VC distribution-in-kind converts one large holder into hundreds of LPs who mostly sell on receipt with no coordination. That fits Gompers & Lerner's months-long negative drift better than a one-day event.
- Sell-to-cover is non-discretionary, price-insensitive selling on or just after quarterly vest dates; for a company six months public it adds to supply in the same window as the unlock.

### Gaps
- No source found on whether unlocked holders or their brokers use VWAP/TWAP/POV algorithms or over how many days. This remains **[unverified] practitioner lore**. The retrieved evidence only supports "blocks for the largest holders, Form 144/Form 4 visible sales for affiliates".
- Exact Gompers & Lerner event-window CAR not retrieved. Recollection, not verified: roughly -2% in the three days around distribution.
- No study retrieved measuring price pressure on RSU vest dates, or documenting the common "window opens two full trading days after earnings" convention (recollection, not verified: this is standard insider-trading-policy language).
- Non-affiliate Rule 144 holding-period conditions (six months for reporting issuers) not sourced this session.

## 4. Follow-on supply: secondaries, blocks, ATMs, shelves, converts, PIPE resale

### Takeaway
Academic averages for SEO discounts are about 2-3% to the prior close (1980s-1990s, rising over time), most US deals since 2009 are announced and priced overnight, and reported 2025 deals cluster around 3-10%. ATMs are now a large share of issuance but are sold invisibly under issuer-set daily caps and price floors, and are only disclosed after the fact. Convertible issuance is accompanied by arbitrage short selling that accounts for most of the larger post-2000 announcement drop.

### Cited Findings
- [documented] Corwin (2003, JF): seasoned offers underpriced on average 2.2% in the 1980s-1990s, with the discount rising substantially over time — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v58y2003i5p2249-2279.html)
- [documented] Mola & Loughran (2004, JFQA): 4,814 SEOs 1986-1999, average offer priced at a 3% discount to the prior-day close, with discounts rising steadily — [Cambridge/JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/discounting-and-clustering-in-seasoned-equity-offering-prices/CC91F820199DA02A6A341A832CAFF0AC)
- [documented] Altinkilic & Hansen (2003): average SEO discount 3.2% over the 1990s (as cited in later work) — [NBER WP 26344](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w26344/w26344.pdf)
- [documented] SEO announcement effect: prior literature documents an average two-day abnormal return of about -3% — [SciELO review, 2026](https://www.scielo.cl/pdf/ede/v53n1/0718-5286-ede-53-1-00004.pdf)
- [documented] Gustafson (2018, JFQA): 75% of SEOs 2009-2014 were announced and issued overnight vs 27% in 2000-2008; non-overnight issuers suffer a 2.5% pre-issue price decline that reverses within 7 days, with the reversal larger for bigger offers and larger pre-issue declines; returns after overnight offerings are less positive and unrelated to offer size — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v53y2018i02p837-866_00.html)
- [practice] 2025 single-deal discounts to last close: Rapport Therapeutics 7.2%; MBX marketed follow-on 10%; Monte Rosa 5.1%; Primo Brands $489M block 3.4% (stock slid afterwards). An education blog puts winning block bids at about 5% — [iTiger/Reuters BUZZ](https://www.itiger.com/news/2566167722), [IFR MBX](https://www.ifre.com/equities/2318855/mbx-secures-us200m-from-marketed-follow-on), [IFR Primo](https://www.ifre.com/equities/2467102/primo-brands-slides-after-us489m-block), [IB interview blog](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/follow-on-offerings-block-trades-explained)
- [documented] Billett, Floros & Garfinkel, "At-the-Market Offerings" (JFQA 54(3), 2019): ATMs are sold directly into the secondary market and "dribbled out" over time; in 2016 ATM incidence and total proceeds were 63% and 26% of those of SEOs; ATM users tend to have lower leverage, higher market-to-book and larger cash balances; ATM-vs-SEO choice consistent with costly certification — [Cambridge/JFQA](https://core-cms.cambridgecore.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/atthemarket-offerings/BE5876A62A1E14B5C285F63D67C7FDE8), [CFA Institute Digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2020/06/dig-v50-n6-2)
- [documented, filing text] ATM mechanics from filed sales agreements: the issuer sends the agent a placement notice that can set a maximum number of shares per day and a minimum price below which the agent may not sell; the agent may accept, counter or decline; the agent uses commercially reasonable efforts with no obligation to buy; sales must be Rule 415 "at the market" (on exchange or to a market maker); agreements bar sales that would be a Rule 10b-18 "block" or a Regulation M "distribution" without written consent, and Regulation M compliance applies while a notice is live — [Gladstone Investment 8-K Ex. 1.1 (2024)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1321741/000119312524138399/d838923dex11.htm), [Sonida Senior Living 8-K Ex. 1.1 (2024)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043000/000119312524083578/d823516dex11.htm)
- [documented] Convertible offerings: average announcement abnormal return about -1.69% for 1984-1999 vs about -4.59% for 2000-2008, a difference that disappears after controlling for arbitrage-induced short selling — [Univ. of Glasgow WP](https://www.gla.ac.uk/media/media_199412_en.pdf)
- [documented] Choi, Getmansky & Tookes: changes in short interest around convertible issuance (1993-2006) show issuance triggers arbitrage-driven short selling — [FDIC archive copy](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090825081723/http:/www.fdic.gov/bank/analytical/cfr/Choi_Getmansky_Tookes.pdf)
- [documented] Issue-date returns are more favourable where short selling is restricted, so post-2000 studies overstate the "information" content of convert announcements — [Financial Analysts Journal 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v68y2012i5p70-88.html)
- [documented] Related 144A financing event study: average 3.53% decline relative to market near announcement with a 2.35% recovery in the following month — [CFA Institute Digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2017/04/short-selling-pressure-and-last-resort-debt-finance-evidence-from-144a-high-yield-risk-adjust)

### Inferences
- Liquid mid/large caps: the overnight deal reprices the stock to roughly the offer price at the next open; the academic evidence of reversal applies to the older marketed (non-overnight) format, and Gustafson finds post-offer returns after overnight deals are less positive. "Buy the secondary for the bounce" is therefore weaker for the format that now dominates.
- ATMs have no announcement event on selling days. The only real-time tells are indirect (persistent offer-side supply near a price floor, stock unable to hold spikes); confirmation comes later in the 10-Q/10-K or a prospectus supplement. Price floors mean ATM supply tends to switch on into strength and off into weakness.
- Convertible deals: the day-of drop is largely mechanical hedge shorting that is typically executed around pricing, and the partial recovery evidence suggests it is not all information. Many issuers pair converts with buybacks or capped calls, which changes the net flow (not sourced here).

### Gaps
- Billett-Floros-Garfinkel's announcement-return and time-to-sell numbers could not be retrieved (author PDF host blocked). Recollection, not verified: ATM program announcement returns are negative but smaller than for SEOs.
- No systematic 2020s US average for overnight follow-on or block discounts found; only single deals.
- ATM commission level and any "percent of daily volume" convention not sourced. Recollection, not verified: commissions typically up to ~3% of gross, and issuers commonly instruct agents to stay within a modest share of daily volume; neither is a regulatory limit.
- Shelf (S-3) mechanics, the "baby shelf" one-third-of-float limit for sub-$75M-float issuers (General Instruction I.B.6), warrant exercise flows and PIPE resale returns outside de-SPACs were not sourced this session.

## 5. Low-float / small-cap specifics

### Takeaway
In micro-cap momentum names the evidence is anecdotal but consistent: offerings are priced at very deep discounts (25% to 80%+ in reported cases), often with warrants, and the stock typically gaps to or below the deal price on the headline. No systematic study of intraday behaviour was found.

### Cited Findings
- [practice] Comstock: $2.25 offering, a 25.5% discount to prior close — [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/47084294)
- [practice] Scorpius Holdings: priced at $1 against a $5.63 prior close; stock down 79% — [InvestorPlace](https://investorplace.com/?p=3156229)
- [practice] Auddia: fell more than 62% to an all-time low after pricing a $12M offering at a deep discount — [Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/auud-stock-hits-52-week-low-dilution-fear/cZBhImpRecf)
- [practice] Rubico: fell 51.4% on a roughly $7.5M offering with about 5.3M shares outstanding — [Investing.com](https://ng.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/rubico-stock-plummets-after-pricing-dilutive-public-offering-93CH-2187845)
- [practice] Other same-pattern cases: NRx Pharmaceuticals -29%, Neptune Wellness -42%, Dragonfly Energy plunge on a $5.5M offering, TeraWulf -30.1% (Apr 2022) — [InvestorPlace NRXP](https://investorplace.com/?p=2834126), [InvestorPlace NEPT](https://investorplace.com/?p=2368407), [Investing.com DFLI](https://ng.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/dragonfly-energy-stock-plunges-after-55-million-public-offering-93CH-2034256), [Motley Fool WULF](https://www.fool.com/investing/2022/04/12/why-terawulfs-shares-plunged-301-on-tuesday)
- [practice] Float context: Innovation Beverage Group cited with a public float of about 614,337 shares — [Longbridge](https://longbridge.com/news/279072672)
- [documented] The deep discounts above compare with a ~2-3% academic average for all SEOs (Section 4), so micro-cap deals are an order of magnitude more dilutive — [Mola & Loughran](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/discounting-and-clustering-in-seasoned-equity-offering-prices/CC91F820199DA02A6A341A832CAFF0AC)

### Inferences
- The offer price (plus warrant strike) becomes the reference level: deal buyers who received warrant coverage can sell stock at or just above the deal price and still hold free optionality, which is the usual explanation for prices pinning near or under the deal price for days. This is logic, not a measured result.
- A company with an effective S-3 and an existing ATM or recent 424B5 can sell into any spike without a new headline; checking for that before trading a low-float runner long is cheap risk control.

- Registered-direct and warrant-inducement mechanics not sourced.

## 6. Where traders find the dates

### Takeaway
Calendar sites take the date from the prospectus and miss early-release triggers, so the 424B4 (and later 8-K/10-Q updates) is the only authoritative source; affiliate selling is visible via Form 144 (electronic since 2023) and Form 4, and ATM terms via the filed sales agreement.

### Cited Findings
- [practice] StockAnalysis publishes an IPO lock-up expiration calendar, states dates are taken from each company's prospectus, and warns that early-release provisions can unlock shares before the stated date — [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/ipos/lockups/)
- [documented] Form 144 notices are filed electronically on EDGAR since 2023 for reporting issuers — [ABA Business Lawyer](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-lawyer/2026-winter/rule-144-opinions-report)
- [documented] ATM sales agreements (daily share cap and floor-price mechanics) are filed as Exhibit 1.1 to an 8-K — [Gladstone Investment 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1321741/000119312524138399/d838923dex11.htm)
- [practice] Early releases are announced in company filings/earnings materials (Figma disclosed its trigger with earnings) — [Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/markets/figma-plunges-after-first-earnings-since-ipo-lock-up-expires-some-shareholders/)
- [practice] De-SPAC PIPE resale shelves are filed on Form S-1 — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/06/SPAC-Re-Attack-Top-Considerations-for-Issuers-Considering-a-De-SPAC-Transaction)
- [documented] 10b5-1 plan adoptions/terminations by officers and directors are subject to the disclosure requirements added in the 2022 amendments — [SEC Release 33-11138](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11138.pdf)

### Inferences
- Workflow implied by the above: (1) calendar site for a first pass; (2) open the 424B4 and read the lock-up language for earnings/price/blackout triggers and tranche sizes; (3) recompute the date after the first earnings date is announced; (4) watch Form 144 the morning of and after; (5) Form 4 two business days later.

### Gaps
- MarketBeat, IPOScoop, Renaissance Capital and Nasdaq lock-up calendars were not surfaced by search and could not be opened; their existence and coverage are recollection, not verified.
- Not sourced this session (recollection, not verified): the 424B4 sections "Shares Eligible for Future Sale" and "Underwriting" carry the lock-up terms; a new ATM appears as a 424B5 prospectus supplement under an effective S-3; a priced offering appears as an 8-K plus 424B5; resale S-1 effectiveness shows as an EDGAR "EFFECT" notice.

## 7. Index and fund-driven scheduled flows that can offset unlock selling

### Takeaway
FTSE Russell excludes locked-up shares from free float and adds them back only at the next quarterly review after expiry, so passive demand from the float increase arrives weeks to months after the unlock, not on the day.

### Cited Findings
- [documented] FTSE Russell: shares subject to lock-up are removed from free float; changes from lock-up expiry are implemented at the next quarterly review, provided the expiry falls on or before the share/float information cut-off date — [FTSE Russell free float restrictions](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/ftse-russell/en_us/documents/policy-documents/free-float-restrictions.pdf)
- [documented] FTSE Russell FAQ: warrant exercises and lock-up expiries are deferred to the next quarterly review rather than applied intra-quarter — [Shares and free float maintenance FAQ](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/ftse-russell/en_us/documents/policy-documents/shares-and-free-float-maintenance-faq.pdf)
- [documented] FTSE fast-entry rule: IPOs need at least 5% free float and voting rights in the market within 12 months of inclusion; IPOs below 5% at entry may be considered if lock-up expirations within 12 months are expected to lift float to 5% — [FTSE IPO fast-entry consultation results](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/ftse-russell/en_us/documents/policy-documents/ftse-faq-document-ipo-fast-entry-market-consultation-results.pdf)
- [practice] Russell Investments (June 2026) on SpaceX: as more shares become publicly available and free float expands, benchmark weight is expected to rise, and passive funds must rebalance, creating elevated trading around the rebalance — [Russell Investments](https://russellinvestments.com/content/ri/us/en/insights/russell-research/2026/06/ftse-russell-reconstitution-signals-benchmark-evolution.html)

### Inferences
- The offset is real but mistimed for an intraday trader: unlock supply hits on day 0, index float-up buying hits at the close of the next quarterly rebalance date. It supports "supply absorbed over the quarter", not "buy the unlock day".
- For mega-IPOs with tiny initial floats, the float-up demand at successive quarterly reviews can be large relative to ADV and is a scheduled demand event in its own right.

### Gaps
- S&P and MSCI float-adjustment rules, and any measured price effect of float-up events, were not retrieved.
- Index inclusion/deletion effects are outside what I sourced here.

## 8. Practitioner guidance on trading unlock days, and whether it is evidenced

### Takeaway
"Short into the unlock" has weak empirical backing once borrow cost and variance are counted; "buy the bounce after" has none in the academic record. The better-supported observations are that price pressure starts before the date, that volume steps up permanently, and that the violent moves coincide with earnings or a named large seller.

### Cited Findings
- [documented] No rebound in the weeks after expiry in Field & Hanka's sample, contradicting a systematic post-unlock bounce — [Nowak 2004 review](https://folia.unifr.ch/documents/317901/files/nowak_AFE_2004.pdf)
- [documented] Pre-expiration short selling in VC/PE-backed IPOs does not predict later returns — [Gibbs & Hao 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v88y2018icp30-43.html)
- [documented] Declines are larger where shorting is harder and spreads wider, i.e. where the trade is least implementable — [Ofek & Richardson, NYU](https://archive.nyu.edu/bitstream/2451/27072/2/wpa99054.pdf)
- [practice] Peloton: -17% on expiry day yet shorts paying 70%+ fees roughly broke even; Tilray: +24% squeeze into expiry — [S&P Global](https://spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/mi/research-analysis/borrow-costs-spike-ahead-of-preipo-lockup-expiries.html), [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/mi/research-analysis/short-selling-recent-ipos.html)
- [practice] Arm rose on expiry day after sliding the day before — [Reuters via Yahoo](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/arms-shares-rise-wall-street-194944362.html)
- [practice] Sell-side framing before Reddit's expiry was "increased volatility heading into and around" the date, not a directional call — [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/analyst-ratings/analyst-color/24/08/40219818/reddit-posts-strong-q2-results-but-jpmorgan-warns-of-volatility-ahead)
- [documented] Early-release announcements themselves carry a negative reaction and reduce the later scheduled-date drop — [Keasler 2001](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02744524)
- [unverified] A Claremont thesis on 2017-2021 finds post-expiration insider trading does not indicate short- to medium-term returns — [Claremont](https://scholarship.claremont.edu/cmc_theses/3085/)

### Inferences
- Claims ranked by support: (1) volume steps up at expiry and stays up — documented; (2) average small negative drift into/at expiry, strongest for VC-backed — documented, small; (3) crowded shorts get squeezed into expiry when the stock has already sold off — case evidence only; (4) reliable bounce after expiry — not evidenced; (5) holders work orders by VWAP over several days — not evidenced either way.
- For intraday expectations the conditioning variables with some support are: VC-backed vs buyout-backed, unlock size relative to float, whether earnings fall in the same week, whether a specific holder has signalled a sale (block headline, Form 144), prior run-up (Gompers-Lerner, Brav-Gompers both tie selling to run-ups), and borrow cost.

### Gaps
- No practitioner playbook from a prop firm or trading desk was retrieved; nothing here documents intraday (open/VWAP/close) patterns on unlock days.
- No evidence found on options-implied moves or skew into unlock dates.
