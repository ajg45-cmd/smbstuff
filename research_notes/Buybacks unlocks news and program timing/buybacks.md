## 1. Execution methods and their mix; how a broker's buyback desk works the order

### Takeaway
Open-market repurchases (OMRs) are the dominant channel by a wide margin (commonly cited as roughly 95% of US repurchases, weakly sourced); ASRs and 10b5-1 preset plans are the main structured variants and tenders are now rare. A broker works an OMR as a slow, schedule/VWAP-style agency order constrained by the 10b-18 price/time/volume limits, and 10b5-1 instructions are usually price-contingent formulas the issuer cannot touch once set.

### Cited Findings
- **[U]** Open-market buybacks are "almost 95%" of US repurchases (Wikipedia; underlying citation not seen). — [Wikipedia: Share repurchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_repurchase)
- **[D]** The safe harbor covers only open-market purchases effected for the issuer on an agency or riskless-principal basis; privately negotiated (off-market) repurchases are not covered by Rule 10b-18 and are not counted in the daily volume limit. — [SEC Division of Trading and Markets 10b-18 FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/marketreg/r10b18faq0504.htm); [Cravath, "Structuring Share Repurchases" (July 2025)](https://www.cravath.com/a/web/iyzUHjtbyLYCG9UtcuXW7L/auAstB/structuring-share-repurchases-rule-10b-18-and-rule-10b5-1.pdf)
- **[D]** Rule 10b5-1 preset repurchase plans: versus plain OMRs they give "an expanded repurchase window and increased legal cover" at the cost of flexibility and the option to time; adopters are more often firms constrained by blackout windows and firms with a history of poor repurchase timing; 10b5-1 repurchase announcements are associated with greater and faster completion rates and more positive market reactions than OMRs (Bonaimé, Harford, Moore, Management Science 2020, sample of 10b5-1 and ASR announcements 2001-2014). — [Management Science / INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3322); [Bonaimé research page](https://alicebonaime.com/research)
- **[D]** 10b5-1 preset repurchase plans are "more than twice as popular" as ASRs (University of Kentucky dissertation on preset plans; 2001-2014 era data). — [UKnowledge dissertation](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1006&context=finance_etds)
- **[D]** The December 2022 Rule 10b5-1 amendments did **not** impose a cooling-off period, the overlapping-plan limit or the single-trade-plan limit on issuer repurchase plans (those apply to persons other than the issuer: 90 days for officers/directors, 30 days for others). — [Skadden, Dec 2022](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2022/12/sec-amends-rules-for-rule-10b51-trading-plans-and-adds-new-disclosure-requirement); [Federal Register 87 FR (Dec 29, 2022)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2022-12-29/html/2022-27675.htm)
- **[P]** Many issuers nonetheless self-impose a cooling-off on their 10b5-1 buyback plans, "typically 30 days or more from entry into the plan or until the opening of the next trading window." — [White & Case on the 10b5-1 amendments](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/sec-adopts-amendments-rule-10b5-1)
- **[P]** 10b5-1 instruction formats seen in filed plans: the daily amount "determined by a formula specified in the plans, based on the market price" (Tech Data 10-K); plans can trade only within minimum/maximum prices or at prices that move over time, with the issuer ceding timing control to the broker (Mayer Brown); a plan can be an irrevocable limit order with broker time-and-price discretion subject to the instructions (Fulgent, BPW exhibits). — [Tech Data 10-K FY2011](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000790703/000119312511075076/d10k.htm); [Mayer Brown / Bloomberg Law 2019](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/publications/2019/09/bloomberg-law-professional-perspectiverule10b51mayer-brown.pdf); [Fulgent 10b5-1 plan exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1674930/000095017023005178/flgt-ex10_46.htm)
- **[P]** Broker guidance to corporates: buy more aggressively when the price is weak and allow the share count at each price point to flex roughly 10-20% around the algorithm's volume targets so the trader can manage achieved price (Raymond James corporate share-repurchase paper). This is the closest sourced description of a "grid / matrix" that buys more as price falls. — [Raymond James, share repurchase paper](https://www.raymondjames.com/-/media/rj/dotcom/files/corporations-and-institutions/equity-capital-markets/ces_share_repurchase.pdf)
- **[P]** "The company's broker often uses strategies similar to VWAP execution or schedule-based execution to spread the purchases through the day" (Guzman & Company practitioner's guide, Oct 2025). — [Guzman practitioner's guide](https://www.guzman.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Share-Buybacks-for-US-Corporations-Practitioners-Guide-v-LinkedIn.pdf)
- **[P]** Self-reported broker performance: Guzman says its buyback executions 2017-2022 beat daily VWAP by an average $0.016/share and beat VWAP on 56% of trades (vendor marketing; not audited). — [Guzman Buyback Report, Nov 2022](https://www.guzman.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Guzman-Buyback-Report-1122.pdf)
- **[D/P]** ASR mechanics: issuer prepays a notional to a bank, receives most of the shares up front (bank borrows them from stock lenders), and the bank buys in the open market over the term to cover; final share count = notional / (average daily VWAP over the term, "typically three- to six-months", less a negotiated discount), with a true-up at maturity; floors/caps (collars) are negotiated. — [Columbia CLS Blue Sky, Aug 12 2026](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2026/08/12/accelerated-share-repurchases-corporate-governance-and-market-integrity/); [WSGR on ASRs](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/accelerated-share-repurchase-asr-programs-to-be-impacted-by-proposed-changes-to-rule-10b5-1-and-share-repurchase-disclosure-rules.html); [Deloitte DART 8.4](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/presentation/asc260-10/roadmap-earnings-per-share/chapter-8-other-considerations/8-4-accelerated-share-repurchase-agreements)
- **[P]** ASRs typically price off "10b-18 VWAP for every trading day during the purchase period, minus an agreed discount"; "the large majority of ASRs are now based on straightforward VWAP minus structure" (Mayer Brown, 2018). — [Mayer Brown share buybacks deck, July 2018](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/events/2018/07/west-legaledcenters-share-buybacks/files/presentation-materials/fileattachment/share-buybacks-7-24-18.pdf)
- **[D]** Scale example: General Motors' November 2023 ASR was $10 billion; banks borrowed and delivered about 215 million shares up front, roughly 16% of shares outstanding. — [Columbia CLS Blue Sky, Aug 12 2026](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2026/08/12/accelerated-share-repurchases-corporate-governance-and-market-integrity/)
- **[D]** Critique of VWAP-minus products: an academic study ("The Great Deception", 2023) argues that "VWAP-minus" guaranteed-discount buyback products reference a benchmark defined as the arithmetic average of daily VWAPs over the period, which a broker with timing discretion can beat mechanically; a typical quoted guarantee is VWAP minus ~0.5%. — [arXiv 2307.09617](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.09617)

### Inferences
- A VWAP-minus ASR or guaranteed-discount program gives the bank an incentive to buy more on days when price is below the running average and to slow/extend when price is high (the bank is short an average and has an early-termination option). That implies bank hedging flow that is heavier on weak days, the same direction as the Raymond James "be aggressive on weakness" guidance. This is a structural inference, not a measured intraday fact.
- During an ASR the buying on the tape is the **bank's** hedge, not issuer 10b-18 flow, so the issuer's Item 703 table shows the up-front share delivery, not when the market purchases happened.
- Tender offers (fixed-price or Dutch auction) are announced, dated events with a published price/range; they do not create hidden intraday flow and are not what a trader is trying to detect on the tape.

### Gaps
- No reliable, current percentage split of buyback dollars across OMR / 10b5-1 / ASR / tender / private block was found. The "95% open market" figure is weakly sourced; ASR share of dollars (often quoted by banks as high-single to low-double digits) could not be sourced.
- Could not open the Cravath (July 2025) memo, the Guzman guide, or any bank algo documentation to get exact descriptions of grid/matrix 10b5-1 schedules or percent-of-volume settings.
- Recalled, unverified: banks hedging an ASR generally agree by contract to trade "as if" Rule 10b-18 applied to their cover purchases (standard ASR confirmation language). Not confirmed in a snippet.

## 2. Rule 10b-18 conditions (17 CFR 240.10b-18)

### Takeaway
10b-18 is a voluntary, day-by-day safe harbor from manipulation liability with four conditions (one broker, time, price, volume); it mechanically keeps compliant issuer flow off the opening print, out of the last 10 (or 30) minutes, at or below the bid/last independent trade, and under 25% of four-week ADTV. Missing any one condition loses the safe harbor for all that day's purchases, but purchases outside it are not automatically unlawful.

### Cited Findings
- **[D]** Structure: four conditions (manner, timing, price, volume) applied on a daily basis; "failure to meet any one of the four conditions" disqualifies all of the issuer's Rule 10b-18 purchases from the safe harbor for that day. — [SEC 10b-18 FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/marketreg/r10b18faq0504.htm); [Cravath memo](https://www.cravath.com/a/web/iyzUHjtbyLYCG9UtcuXW7L/auAstB/structuring-share-repurchases-rule-10b-18-and-rule-10b5-1.pdf)
- **[D]** One broker: purchases must be made through only one broker or dealer on any single day (does not apply to unsolicited purchases). — [17 CFR 240.10b-18 (govinfo, 2024 CFR)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title17-vol4/pdf/CFR-2024-title17-vol4-sec240-10b-18.pdf); [TheCorporateCounsel.net, May 1 2024](https://www.thecorporatecounsel.net/blog/2024/05/the-10-minutes-that-matter-most.html)
- **[D]** Time: a 10b-18 purchase may not be "the opening (regular way) purchase reported in the consolidated system", and may not be effected during the 10 minutes before the scheduled close of the primary trading session (in the principal market and in the market where the purchase is effected) for a security with "an ADTV value of $1 million or more and a public float value of $150 million or more"; for all other securities the excluded window is the last 30 minutes. — [17 CFR 240.10b-18 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.10b-18); [SEC comment letter restating the rule, 2010](https://www.sec.gov/comments/s7-04-10/s70410-11.pdf); [Skadden, Rule 10b-18 safe harbors (Mar 2020)](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2020/03/trading-issues-arising-out-of-market-disruptions/rule_10b_18_safe_harbors.pdf)
- **[D]** Price: the purchase price must not exceed "the highest independent bid or the last independent transaction price, whichever is higher," quoted or reported in the consolidated system at the time of the purchase. — [17 CFR 240.10b-18 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.10b-18); [TheCorporateCounsel.net](https://www.thecorporatecounsel.net/blog/2024/05/the-10-minutes-that-matter-most.html)
- **[D]** Volume: total 10b-18 purchases on any single day must not exceed 25% of the security's ADTV, where ADTV is "the average daily trading volume reported for the security during the four calendar weeks preceding the week in which the Rule 10b-18 purchase is to be effected." — [17 CFR 240.10b-18 (govinfo)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title17-vol4/pdf/CFR-2024-title17-vol4-sec240-10b-18.pdf); [CRS, Stock Buybacks](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11393)
- **[D]** Block exception: once each week, in lieu of the 25% limit, the issuer may make one block purchase, provided it makes no other 10b-18 purchases that day (and the block is excluded from the ADTV calculation). — [Cravath memo](https://www.cravath.com/a/web/iyzUHjtbyLYCG9UtcuXW7L/auAstB/structuring-share-repurchases-rule-10b-18-and-rule-10b5-1.pdf); [17 CFR 240.10b-18 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.10b-18)
- **[D]** The ADTV/public-float test for the 10-minute window and the current volume/price mechanics come from the 2003 amendments (Release 33-8335, effective Dec 2003). — [SEC Release 33-8335](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/33-8335.htm); [Federal Register, Nov 17 2003](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2003/11/17/03-28593/purchases-of-certain-equity-securities-by-the-issuer-and-others)
- **[D]** The safe harbor is not available for repurchases that, although in technical compliance, are part of a plan or scheme to evade the federal securities laws. — [SEC Release 33-8335](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/33-8335.htm)
- **[D]** Compliance is not observable from public data and was imperfect in the one study with trade-level data: in Cook, Krigman & Leach's sample, "although the level of compliance is relatively high, only two firms in our sample consistently comply for all repurchases" (1990s data, companion paper, Journal of Business 2003). — [Cook, Krigman, Leach, J. Business 2003 (RePEc)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ucp:jnlbus:v:76:y:2003:i:2:p:289-316)
- **[D]** Context for the last-10-minutes exclusion: nearly 10% of all US share volume traded in the closing auction in a recent period (2024 commentary). — [TheCorporateCounsel.net, "The 10 minutes that matter most"](https://www.thecorporatecounsel.net/blog/2024/05/the-10-minutes-that-matter-most.html)

### Inferences
- A safe-harbor-compliant issuer order cannot lift an offer that is above the last independent print/highest independent bid, cannot be the opening print and cannot trade 3:50-4:00 pm ET (large caps) or 3:30-4:00 pm (ADTV < $1M or float < $150M). So issuer flow, by construction, is passive-to-neutral, does not initiate upticks to new highs, and is absent from the closing auction. A stock ramping into the close or gapping on the open print is not being moved by 10b-18 flow.
- The 25% of ADTV number is a ceiling, not typical usage. For mega caps the actual program is a low-single-digit percent of volume (see section 3).
- The once-a-week block exception means an occasional large negotiated print from an issuer is possible without other issuer buying that day.

### Gaps
- Rule text could not be opened at eCFR/Cornell/govinfo; quoted phrases are those visible in snippets or restated by SEC/law-firm sources. Verify wording against eCFR before quoting verbatim in a final report.
- Recalled, unverified: (i) "block" is defined as $200,000+ in value, or 5,000+ shares worth $50,000+, or 20+ round lots totalling 150%+ of daily volume, and excludes shares the broker accumulated for the purpose of selling to the issuer; (ii) paragraph (c) relaxes the rule in the session after a market-wide trading suspension (volume limit rises to 100% of ADTV and the time-of-purchase condition is lifted); (iii) paragraph (d) says no presumption of violation arises for purchases outside the safe harbor; (iv) after-hours purchases are permitted at prices not above the lower of the closing price and the current independent bid/last sale. None of these four could be confirmed in a snippet.
- No evidence found of any amendment to Rule 10b-18's four conditions since 2003 (a 2010 proposal to tweak it was never adopted - the 2010 comment letter above refers to that proposal). Status "unchanged as of late 2026" is my inference from the absence of contrary results, not a confirmed fact.

## 3. How big is buyback flow (S&P 500 totals, top repurchasers, share of volume, concentration, excise tax)

### Takeaway
S&P 500 buybacks run about $1.0-1.1 trillion a year (roughly $4 billion per trading day), with about half of the dollars coming from 20 companies; at market level that is on the order of 0.5% of daily US equity dollar volume, so it is a persistent bid, not a dominant one. The 1% excise tax has not changed behaviour.

### Inferences

JPMorgan at ~$27.6 billion/yr is ~$110 million/day, roughly 4-5% of a ~$2.5 billion ADV. So for the heaviest repurchasers the program is a low-single-digit percent of daily volume on average, far below the 25% ADTV cap, but it is concentrated in open-window days and (by design) in the passive side of the book, so its share of *bid-side passive* liquidity on a given day can be much larger than its share of total volume.
- Flow is very concentrated: ~half the index dollars sit in 20 names, with IT, Communication Services and Financials the dominant sectors. For most of the other ~480 names buyback flow is small relative to volume.

## 4. Timing: authorization vs purchases, completion, blackout windows, 10b5-1 during blackouts, blackout return evidence, seasonality

### Takeaway
An authorization is an option, not a schedule: completion is partial and slow, and actual buying clusters in the weeks after earnings because company-imposed blackout policies (not SEC rules) shut discretionary buying from roughly two to three weeks before quarter-end until one to three days after earnings. Flow drops but does not stop in blackouts (10b5-1 plans keep running), and the evidence that the market is weaker during blackouts is thin to negative.

### Cited Findings
- **[D]** Open-market programs do not commit the firm to buy; completion is partial. Sources summarizing Stephens & Weisbach (1998) and later work put average completion around 50% after one year / median below 50%; 24% of US OMR programs announced 1985-2012 involved no actual buybacks in the fiscal year of announcement. Non-US medians are lower (Canada 28.6%, UK 37%). — [CFA Institute Research Foundation literature review, 2022](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/-/media/documents/book/rf-lit-review/2022/rflr-stock-buybacks.pdf)
- **[D]** 10b5-1 preset plans complete more and faster than plain OMRs. — [Bonaimé, Harford, Moore (Management Science 2020)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3322)
- **[D]** Hillert, Maug, Obernberger (JFE 2016; 50,204 repurchase-months, US, 2004-2010) is cited for the finding that firms front-load buyback programs to reduce price risk (secondary citation). — [JFQA "Repurchases for price impact" citing Hillert et al.](https://jfqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/23978_Repurchases_for_Price_Impact.pdf); [Tinbergen listing](https://tinbergen.nl/publication/166343/stock-repurchases-and-liquidity)
- **[D]** Academic blackout definition and effect: Dittmann, Li, Obernberger, Zheng (Journal of Accounting and Economics 80(1), 2025; US sample 2006-2019) define the blackout as running from 20 days before fiscal quarter-end until three days after the following earnings announcement; repurchase timing is "largely determined by the corporate calendar". If a blackout covers half of a month, repurchase intensity falls by 0.073% of shares (= 50% x 0.1463), "almost half of the average" monthly repurchase intensity in their sample. — [ECGI working paper](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/2025-06/equity-based-compensation-and-the-timing-of-share-repurchases.pdf); [JAE listing (RePEc)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jaecon:v:80:y:2025:i:1:s0165410125000345); [Tinbergen DP](https://papers.tinbergen.nl/21101.pdf)
- **[D]** Blackouts are a matter of company insider-trading policy (to avoid trading on material non-public information), not an SEC blackout rule; issuer buybacks are subject to Rule 10b-5 like any purchase. — [Marketplace, Sept 23 2021](https://marketplace.org/2021/09/23/do-insider-trading-rules-apply-to-corporate-stock-buybacks); [247WallSt, Dec 2018](https://247wallst.com/technology-3/2018/12/22/apple-buybacks-blackout-period/)
- **[P]** Desk rule of thumb (Goldman, 2015): "most companies are precluded from engaging in open-market stock repurchases during the five weeks before releasing earnings." — [Ritholtz reprint of Bloomberg, Oct 2015](https://ritholtz.com/2015/10/buyback-burst-seen-as-salvation-from-goldmans-sp-500-decline-2)
- **[U]** Goldman desk commentary (Bloomberg-syndicated; article date unreliable, text appears to be 2022): buyback volumes "usually fall about 35%" once a blackout begins. A glossary page claims Goldman documented executions falling by over 50% in the late-2018 blackout. — [The Wealth Advisor reprint](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/goldman-buyback-desk-was-flooded-orders-during-stock-rout); [Convex glossary](https://convextrade.com/glossary/equity-buyback-blackout-period)
- **[U]** Open-window estimates from desks (methods differ: % of companies vs % of index weight): Goldman, early 2026 - about 59% of S&P 500 companies back in open windows, ~40% having exited blackout since mid-January; Goldman, May 2026 - about 40% of corporates in the open window, expected open until June 12; Citadel Securities - 24% of S&P 500 index weight outside blackout on Oct 12, ~55% by Oct 31, ~85% by Nov 9 (year not confirmed); a blog claims ~465 companies (93%) in blackout as of July 6 2026. — [Advisor Perspectives/Bloomberg, May 4 2026](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2026/05/04/ultimate-dip-buyers-stocks-strong-buybacks-surge); [Cardano Capital substack](https://cardanocapital.substack.com/p/buyback-blackouts-93-of-the-s-and); [Investing.com analysis](https://uk.investing.com/analysis/sp-500-technical-reset-clears-the-path-for-fundamentals-200626943)
- **[D]** 10b5-1 as the blackout workaround: firms "constrained by blackout windows" are more likely to adopt preset plans, which provide "an expanded repurchase window." Issuer plans have no mandatory cooling-off. — [Bonaimé, Harford, Moore](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3322); [Skadden, Dec 2022](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2022/12/sec-amends-rules-for-rule-10b51-trading-plans-and-adds-new-disclosure-requirement)
- **[P/D]** Is the market weaker in blackouts? State Street Global Advisors, "Buyback Blackout Periods Do Not Negatively Impact Performance": heavy-buyback firms (buyback index vs equal-weight S&P 500) showed no negative relative returns in any blackout period measured. Alpha Architect (Jan 2019) reached the same conclusion, calling the supposed effect noise. — [SSGA paper](https://www.ssga.com/library-content/pdfs/etf/us/b27-buyback-blackout-periods-do-not-negatively-impact-performance.pdf); [Alpha Architect, Jan 18 2019](https://alphaarchitect.com/2019/01/buyback-blackout-periods-do-not-negatively-impact-market-performance/)
- **[D]** Bargeron, Bonaimé, Thomas (JFQA 2017): returns between program authorization and completion announcements are indistinguishable from zero; long-run post-buyback excess returns come mainly from announcement returns at subsequent authorizations and takeover bids. — [JFQA 2017](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/3A0AA39EF30BB41483794352CDB40BAC/S0022109017000084a.pdf/timing_and_source_of_longrun_returns_following_repurchases.pdf)
- **[P]** Seasonality (Goldman, two vintages that disagree on rank): 2015 Kostin note (2007 and 2009-2014 averages) - November busiest at ~13% of annual S&P 500 buyback spending; Q4 30% of annual outlays vs Q1 18%, Q2 25%, Q3 26%. 2025 Goldman flows note - November is the second-heaviest month at 10.1% of annual executions, behind August. — [Ritholtz/Bloomberg, Oct 2015](https://ritholtz.com/2015/10/buyback-burst-seen-as-salvation-from-goldmans-sp-500-decline-2); [ETF Trends, 2015](https://www.etftrends.com/2015/10/stock-repurchase-season-buyback-etfs-to-consider/); [Tiger Brokers summary of 2025 Goldman flows note](https://www.itiger.com/news/1155391250)
- **[P]** Buyback desks see order flow surge in sell-offs: Goldman's buyback desk "was flooded with orders during stock rout"; Bloomberg 2026 calls corporates the "ultimate dip-buyers". — [The Wealth Advisor/Bloomberg](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/goldman-buyback-desk-was-flooded-orders-during-stock-rout); [Bloomberg, May 4 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-04/-ultimate-dip-buyers-of-stocks-remain-strong-as-buybacks-surge)

### Inferences
- Combining the academic window (quarter-end minus ~20 days to earnings plus ~3 days) with desk comments: for a calendar-quarter large cap reporting in the third or fourth week after quarter-end, discretionary buying is off from roughly mid-month of the last month of the quarter until 1-3 trading days after its print. The heaviest issuer buying in a given stock is therefore most likely in the ~5-7 weeks starting a couple of days after its earnings date. This is a per-stock calendar, so index-level "blackout" talk is a staggered aggregate.
- "Blackout = buyers gone" is overstated: desk estimates are a 30-35% fall in executions, not 100%, because 10b5-1 plans continue. Measured return effects of blackouts are not distinguishable from zero in the two published tests found.
- The Aug/Nov peaks line up with post-earnings open windows for calendar-quarter reporters (Q2 results late July, Q3 results late October); Q4 is heaviest on the older data.

### Gaps
- No snippet confirmed the often-quoted practitioner version "blackout starts ~2 weeks before quarter end and ends 1-2 days after earnings"; the documented academic definition found is 20 days before quarter-end to 3 days after earnings, and Goldman's older shorthand is "five weeks before earnings." Individual company policies vary and are disclosed (since FY2023/24 10-Ks) as Exhibit 19 insider trading policies - not reviewed here.
- Recalled, unverified: Stephens & Weisbach (1998) found firms acquire 74-82% of announced shares within three years. The CFA review's "below 50%" framing (found) and this figure differ by horizon/measure; verify before using either.
- Goldman's own blackout-return analyses (Kostin) and the primary Goldman desk notes are behind login and were not found; all Goldman numbers here are second-hand.
- No source found giving week-of-quarter execution intensity directly from Item 703 monthly data beyond the Dittmann et al. coefficient.

## 5. Intraday / daily behaviour: when issuers buy, bargain prices, volatility, price support, spreads

### Takeaway
The documented evidence is at monthly (US) or daily (Hong Kong, France, Japan) frequency: issuers on average pay a little less than the period's average price (about 2% on average, more for small/infrequent repurchasers, little or nothing for large frequent S&P 500 names), buy more after price declines, and their buying is associated with tighter spreads, lower idiosyncratic volatility and price support in down markets. I found no study documenting the time-of-day distribution of US issuer purchases.

### Cited Findings
- **[D]** Dittmar & Field, "Can managers time the market? Evidence using repurchase price data" (JFE 115(2), 2015; uses the post-2004 monthly average-price disclosures): firms repurchase at a significantly lower price than the average market price in all sample years; the average firm pays about 2% less than the average investor; infrequent repurchasers pay roughly 6-8% less and earn significantly higher returns up to three years after actual repurchases; firms buying when insiders also buy and firms with low prior returns also pay less. — [JFE listing (RePEc)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v115y2015i2p261-282.html); [University of Michigan release](https://news.umich.edu/?p=22236); [CFA Digest summary](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2015/06/can-managers-time-the-market-evidence-using-repurchase-price-data-digest-summary)
- **[D]** Ben-Rephael, Oded, Wohl, "Do Firms Buy Their Stock at Bargain Prices? Evidence from Actual Stock Repurchase Disclosures" (Review of Finance 18(4), 2014, pp. 1299-1340): using monthly repurchase data, firms repurchase at prices significantly below average market prices; the discount is negatively related to firm size and positively related to market-to-book (small and growth firms get the bargain; large firms much less). — [Review of Finance listing (RePEc)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/revfin/v18y2014i4p1299-1340..html); [Bar-Ilan record](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/do-firms-buy-their-stock-at-bargain-prices-evidence-from-actual-s/)
- **[D]** Cook, Krigman, Leach, "On the Timing and Execution of Open Market Repurchases" (RFS 17, 2004, pp. 463-498; 64 firms that voluntarily supplied trade-level repurchase data): execution styles range from immediate intense buying to delayed and smoothed buying; NYSE firms on average beat naive-accumulation benchmarks, Nasdaq firms do not; **no clear evidence** that repurchases are timed to coincide with, precede or follow information-release days; repurchase trading "contributes to market liquidity by narrowing bid-ask spreads and attenuating the price impact of order imbalances on days when repurchase trades are completed." — [RFS listing (RePEc)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v17y2004i2p463-498.html); [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=251854)
- **[D]** Hillert, Maug, Obernberger, "Stock repurchases and liquidity" (JFE 119(1), 2016, pp. 186-209; 50,204 US repurchase-months, 2004-2010): with instrumental variables, repurchases "unequivocally improve liquidity"; earlier contrary results were confounded by endogenous controls; liquidity also shapes how firms execute programs. — [Erasmus record](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/stock-repurchases-and-liquidity/); [Tinbergen listing](https://tinbergen.nl/publication/166343/stock-repurchases-and-liquidity)
- **[D]** Busch & Obernberger, "Actual Share Repurchases, Price Efficiency, and the Information Content of Stock Prices" (RFS 30(1), 2017, pp. 324-362): repurchases make prices more efficient and reduce idiosyncratic risk; effects are "primarily driven by repurchases in down markets"; interpretation - firms provide "price support at fundamental values"; no evidence that managers use repurchases to manipulate prices when selling stock or exercising options. — [Harvard Law Forum summary, Nov 16 2016](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2016/11/16/actual-share-repurchases-price-efficiency-and-the-information-content-of-stock-prices/); [RFS listing (RePEc)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:30:y:2017:i:1:p:324-362.)
- **[D]** Hong Kong daily disclosures - Brockman & Chung, "Managerial timing and corporate liquidity" (JFE 61(3), 2001, pp. 417-448): about half of managers beat a naive accumulation strategy; bid-ask spreads **widen** and depth falls on repurchase days (adverse-selection reading: others detect an informed buyer and withdraw). Results are consistent with either timing on private information or price support. — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222513004_Managerial_timing_and_corporate_liquidity_evidence_from_actual_share_repurchases); [CFA Institute RF literature review](https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/-/media/documents/book/rf-lit-review/2022/rflr-stock-buybacks.pdf)
- **[D]** France vs Hong Kong (Journal of Banking & Finance, "Actual share repurchases, timing and liquidity"): repurchases "are large enough to stabilize prices, but do not lead to a higher share price"; the two markets give broadly similar results despite different disclosure regimes. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426606002135)
- **[U]** A US student thesis (Erasmus, supervised by Obernberger) reports NYSE firms pushing prices above intrinsic value in positive-news periods - not peer reviewed; contradicts the published "no manipulation" finding. — [Croes MSc thesis](https://thesis.eur.nl/pub/50349/MscThesis_Croes_410090lc_Final.pdf)
- **[D]** Before the 2004 Item 703 change (effective with the 2003 amendments) US firms did not have to report any information on repurchase prices or actual amounts, which is why US evidence starts in 2004 and is monthly. — [Ben-Rephael, Oded, Wohl working paper (TAU)](https://en-coller.tau.ac.il/sites/coller-english.tau.ac.il/files/WP_4-2013_Ben%20Rephael_Oded_Wohl.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Issuers buy below VWAP / on down days" is supported in the weak, low-frequency form: lower-than-average monthly prices and more buying after declines and in down markets. It is not established at intraday resolution for US stocks. The mechanical reason is partly the rule itself (cannot pay above last independent print/highest bid) and partly price-contingent 10b5-1 grids.
- The bargain is smallest exactly where a large-cap intraday trader looks: big, frequent repurchasers (the Apple/Alphabet/JPMorgan class) run steady programs and get little or no discount to average price, implying near time-uniform, VWAP-like participation rather than opportunistic dip-buying.
- Liquidity evidence conflicts by market structure: US studies (Cook et al.; Hillert et al.) find tighter spreads / better liquidity; Hong Kong (Brockman & Chung) finds wider spreads on repurchase days. For US large caps the "buyback adds resting bid liquidity" reading is the better-supported one.
- "Price support" in the literature means lower idiosyncratic volatility and less downside in down markets on monthly data, not a visible intraday floor.

### Gaps
- **No US intraday evidence** on time-of-day of issuer purchases was found (no source using TAQ-matched issuer trades). The only US trade-level study (Cook, Krigman, Leach) uses 64 firms from the 1990s and the snippets say nothing about time of day.
- Japan: only the title of "Testing the managerial timing ability: Evidence from stock repurchases in Japan" surfaced ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S154461231000036X)); findings not seen. UK daily-RNS-based studies were not found.
- Could not open Ben-Rephael et al., Dittmar & Field or Hillert et al. full texts to pull exact discount magnitudes by size bucket, the within-quarter timing relative to earnings, or spread-change magnitudes.
- Recalled, unverified: Ben-Rephael et al. also report that repurchase activity is concentrated after earnings announcements, that actual repurchases are followed by positive abnormal returns for up to ~3 months, and that the companion S&P 500-only paper (Oded & Wohl 2011) finds large firms do not buy at a discount.
- "Dittmar & Field - Do firms buy their stock at bargain prices?" in the brief conflates two papers: that title is Ben-Rephael, Oded & Wohl (2014); Dittmar & Field's paper is "Can managers time the market? Evidence using repurchase price data" (2015).
- No Bonaimé et al. paper specifically on intraday/bargain-price execution was found; the Bonaimé papers surfaced concern 10b5-1 plans, long-run return sources, and employee-compensation dilution ([Critical Finance Review 2024](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/CFR-0145)).

## 6. Disclosure a trader can actually use; what can and cannot be known in real time

### Takeaway
Nothing about US issuer buying is disclosed in real time: the only mandatory data is the quarterly Item 703 table (monthly share totals and average price, published weeks after the quarter in the 10-Q/10-K). The SEC's 2023 rule that would have added daily data was vacated on Dec 19 2023 and has not been replaced as far as I could find. Real-time knowledge is limited to announced facts (authorizations, ASRs, tenders) and inference from the earnings calendar.

### Cited Findings
- **[D]** Current requirement: issuers report repurchases aggregated monthly under the pre-existing Item 703 of Regulation S-K, in Forms 10-Q and 10-K (the table gives, per month of the quarter, total shares purchased, average price paid, shares bought under publicly announced programs, and remaining authorization). — [Weil, Feb 26 2024](https://www.weil.com/-/media/mailings/2024/q1/sec-issuer-repurchase-disclosures_-be-sure-to-look-at-the-correct-rules-02-26-24.pdf); [SEC pre-amendment Item 703 text](https://sec.gov/files/corpfin/pre-amendment-item-703.pdf)
- **[D]** The Share Repurchase Disclosure Modernization rule (adopted May 2023) would have required quarterly tabular disclosure of **daily** repurchase activity (Exhibit 26 / Form F-SR), narrative disclosure of objectives, a checkbox on officer/director trading within four business days of a buyback announcement, and Item 408(d) disclosure of issuer 10b5-1 plan adoptions/terminations. — [KPMG summary](https://kpmg.com/us/en/frv/reference-library/2024/sec-share-repurchase-disclosure-rules.html); [Fenwick](https://fenwick.com/insights/publications/sec-amends-rules-for-disclosure-of-share-buybacks)
- **[D]** Vacatur timeline: Fifth Circuit held on Oct 31 2023 that the SEC acted arbitrarily and capriciously (Chamber of Commerce v. SEC, No. 23-60255); SEC stayed the rule Nov 22 2023; told the court Dec 1 2023 it could not cure the defects in 30 days; court vacated the rule Dec 19 2023. Disclosure "reverts" to the prior requirements; Item 408(d) was vacated too, while the rest of Item 408 (officer/director 10b5-1 disclosures) remains. SEC published technical amendments removing the vacated text (Release 34-99778; Federal Register Apr 8 2024). — [SEC announcement](https://sec.gov/newsroom/whats-new/further-announcement-regarding-share-repurchase-disclosure-modernization-rule); [SEC rule page S7-21-21](https://sec.gov/rules-regulations/2024/03/s7-21-21); [Federal Register, Apr 8 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-04-08/html/2024-06187.htm); [Dechert](https://www.dechert.com/knowledge/onpoint/2023/5/sec-adopts-share-repurchase-disclosure-rules-.html); [Jenner & Block](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/publications/client-alert-fifth-circuit-court-of-appeals-vacates-the-secs-share-repurchase-disclosure-modernization-rule-amendments)
- **[D]** Rule 10b-18 conformity "is unverifiable with public data." — [Cook, Krigman, Leach, J. Business 2003](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ucp:jnlbus:v:76:y:2003:i:2:p:289-316)
- **[D]** Since the 2022 10b5-1 amendments, companies file their insider trading policies as Exhibit 19 to the 10-K, which is where a company's own blackout-window definition can be read (examples: Sabre, Johnson Controls, Kulicke & Soffa exhibits). — [Sabre Exhibit 19](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1597033/000162828026008800/exhibit191sabrecorporation.htm); [Johnson Controls Exhibit 19](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000833444/000083344426000108/ex191202610-k.htm)
- **[D]** 10b5-1 repurchase plan agreements and ASR confirmations do appear as exhibits in some filings (e.g. plan agreements filed with 8-K/10-K), showing formula structure after the fact. — [Example 10b5-1 repurchase agreement exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1000230/000119312512399644/d414352dex101.htm); [Reliv 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000768710/000116923205002182/d63408_ex10-1.htm)

### Inferences
- What a trader **can** know in real time: (a) an authorization exists and its remaining size (press release/8-K, then each 10-Q); (b) whether an ASR is running and its notional/term (press release/8-K at launch, 10-Q notes at settlement); (c) the company's own blackout definition (Exhibit 19) and its earnings date, hence whether discretionary buying is plausibly open; (d) last quarter's monthly pace and average price (Item 703), which gives a run-rate as a percent of ADV.
- What a trader **cannot** know: whether the issuer is in the market today, the broker, the limit/grid levels, whether a 10b5-1 plan is active (issuer plan disclosure under Item 408(d) was vacated), or intraday timing. Any "the buyback is on the bid" claim on a given day is inference.
- Item 703 lag: month-1 purchases of a quarter are published roughly 2.5-4 months later; useful only for calibrating typical pace, not for same-day decisions.

### Gaps
- Whether the SEC re-proposed any buyback daily-disclosure rule in 2024-2026 was not found; the SEC rule page shows only technical amendments (page last updated May 20 2025 per search summary). Treat "no replacement rule as of Nov 2026" as probable but unconfirmed.
- Which 8-K item ASRs and authorizations are filed under (commonly Item 8.01/7.01 with a press release, or Item 1.01 if the ASR agreement is deemed material) could not be confirmed from a snippet - recalled practice only. There is no specific 8-K item mandating disclosure of an authorization or ASR.
- Commercial daily/weekly buyback trackers (Verity/InsiderScore, bank desk flow reports) were not surfaced; I found no public description of what they offer beyond aggregating announcements and Item 703 data.

## 7. Announcement effects (OMR, ASR, tender offers) and long-run drift

### Takeaway
Open-market buyback announcements have historically produced roughly a 2-3% short-window abnormal return, with tenders much larger (high single digits to low teens) and ASRs smaller (about 1%); announcement effects have shrunk since about 2001 as repurchasers became larger. The famous long-run drift (12.1% over four years in 1980-1990 data) is disputed for recent US samples.

### Cited Findings
- **[D]** Ikenberry, Lakonishok, Vermaelen (1995; open-market announcements 1980-1990): average four-year buy-and-hold abnormal return of 12.1% after the announcement (larger for value stocks). — [NBER w4965](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4965.ris); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:4965)
- **[D]** Typical announcement returns in the older literature: about 3% for open-market programs (ILV 1995; Grullon & Michaely 2002), 3-5% in some summaries; 8-15% for fixed-price and Dutch-auction tender offers. — [Springer, Review of Quantitative Finance and Accounting](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11156-010-0215-y); [FGV thesis citing ILV and Grullon-Michaely](https://gvpesquisa.fgv.br/sites/gvpesquisa.fgv.br/files/gabriel_alejandro_micheloud.pdf)
- **[D]** Decline over time: the median size of repurchasing firms has risen sharply and announcement returns have declined; abnormal-return magnitudes "significantly dropped after 2001", with five-day announcement abnormal returns significantly smaller in the two most recent subsamples (around 2% in the snippet). — [Springer RQFA](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11156-010-0215-y); [Lee et al., "Repurchases have changed" (KAIST, 2019)](https://www.business.kaist.ac.kr/faculty/inmoo/Articles/RepurchasesHaveChanged_June2019_final.pdf)
- **[D]** Long-run drift is contested: Fu & Huang (2015) find the long-horizon abnormal return has disappeared for recent repurchases; Peyer & Vermaelen (2009) "reject the hypothesis that the buyback anomalies ... have disappeared"; Manconi, Peyer, Vermaelen (2018) find long-run abnormal returns in 31 non-US countries; Bargeron, Bonaimé, Thomas (2017) find returns between authorization and completion indistinguishable from zero, with the long-run excess traced to later authorization announcements and takeover bids. — [Peyer & Vermaelen](https://tevgeniou.github.io/EquityRiskFactors/bibliography/BuybacksPV.pdf); [KAIST paper citing Fu & Huang](https://www.business.kaist.ac.kr/faculty/inmoo/Articles/RepurchasesHaveChanged_June2019_final.pdf); [JFQA 2017](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/3A0AA39EF30BB41483794352CDB40BAC/S0022109017000084a.pdf/timing_and_source_of_longrun_returns_following_repurchases.pdf); [ECGI, buybacks around the world](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/are_buybacks_good_for_longterm_shareholder_value_evidence_from_buybacks_around_the_world.pdf)
- **[D]** ASR announcements: positive average abnormal returns (Bargeron, Kulchania, Thomas, JFE 101(1), 2011); a Financial Analysts Journal study finds "small (about 1.2 percent) but positive and statistically significant" ASR announcement returns, smaller than for other repurchase methods. — [JFE listing (RePEc)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:101:y:2011:i:1:p:69-89); [Financial Analysts Journal](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.2469/faj.v66.n6.4)
- **[D]** Tender offers: Comment & Jarrell (Journal of Finance 46(4), 1991) - fixed-price self-tenders are a stronger signal than Dutch auctions and open-market programs; fixed-price announcement returns about 8% in one review's summary; Lie's later work finds the market responds more favourably to Dutch-auction than fixed-price offers in his sample, and Lie & McConnell (1998) find no difference in subsequent earnings improvement. — [Comment & Jarrell (RePEc)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v46y1991i4p1243-71.html); [Lie & McConnell](https://www.biz.uiowa.edu/faculty/elie/Lie_McConnell.pdf); [Purdue dissertation](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/dissertations/AAI9713548)
- **[P]** Goldman's "buyback aristocrats" basket (consistent large repurchasers) outperformed the equal-weight S&P 500 in 2025 year-to-date as of Oct 2025 (a factor/selection result, not an announcement effect). — [CNBC, Oct 21 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/21/goldman-sachs-says-these-buyback-aristocrats-have-outperformed.html)

### Inferences
- For a day trader the announcement effect is an event-day gap/drift on the headline (average low single digits for OMR authorizations at large caps, often bundled with earnings so not separable), not evidence that buying has started: purchases cannot begin in a blackout and authorizations are frequently announced with earnings.
- The average effect is smallest for exactly the large, habitual repurchasers that dominate dollar flow; surprise, size relative to market cap, and method (tender > OMR > ASR) drive the reaction.

### Gaps
- Recalled, unverified: ILV's initial announcement-window return of about 3.5% (3.54%); Comment & Jarrell's ~11% (fixed-price), ~8% (Dutch auction), ~2.3% (open market); Peyer & Vermaelen's ~2.4% announcement return and their 48-month abnormal return for 1991-2001. None were visible in snippets; the one snippet number for fixed-price tenders was "approximately 8%", which conflicts with my recalled 11% - check the original.

## 8. Practitioner claims about what a buyback looks like on the tape - and whether they are supported

### Takeaway
The rule-driven claims are solid (issuer flow does not chase, is not the opening print, is absent from the last 10 minutes and the closing auction, and is capped well below 25% of ADTV); the tape-reading claims (visible steady bid, support at round numbers or VWAP, identifiable footprint) have no documented support that I could find and are not observable given current disclosure.

### Cited Findings
- **[D]** "No chasing": a compliant purchase cannot be above the higher of the highest independent bid and the last independent transaction price - supported by rule text. — [17 CFR 240.10b-18 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.10b-18)
- **[D]** "Not at the open, not into the close": not the opening regular-way purchase; nothing in the last 10 minutes (ADTV value >= $1M and float >= $150M) or last 30 minutes (others); the closing auction (nearly 10% of US share volume in a recent period) is therefore excluded. — [TheCorporateCounsel.net, May 1 2024](https://www.thecorporatecounsel.net/blog/2024/05/the-10-minutes-that-matter-most.html); [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2020/03/trading-issues-arising-out-of-market-disruptions/rule_10b_18_safe_harbors.pdf)
- **[P]** "Spread through the day / VWAP-like": brokers often use VWAP-style or schedule-based execution for buybacks (Guzman); ASRs settle on average 10b-18 VWAP minus a discount (Mayer Brown). — [Guzman practitioner's guide](https://www.guzman.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Share-Buybacks-for-US-Corporations-Practitioners-Guide-v-LinkedIn.pdf); [Mayer Brown 2018](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/events/2018/07/west-legaledcenters-share-buybacks/files/presentation-materials/fileattachment/share-buybacks-7-24-18.pdf)
- **[P]** "Buys more when weak": broker advice to be aggressive on weakness with 10-20% flex around volume targets (Raymond James); 10b5-1 formulas keyed to market price (Tech Data); buyback desks flooded with orders in routs (Goldman via Bloomberg). — [Raymond James](https://www.raymondjames.com/-/media/rj/dotcom/files/corporations-and-institutions/equity-capital-markets/ces_share_repurchase.pdf); [Tech Data 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000790703/000119312511075076/d10k.htm); [The Wealth Advisor/Bloomberg](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/goldman-buyback-desk-was-flooded-orders-during-stock-rout)
- **[D]** "Provides support / dampens volatility": supported at monthly frequency - lower idiosyncratic risk and price support concentrated in down markets (Busch & Obernberger 2017); improved liquidity (Hillert et al. 2016); narrower spreads and less price impact from order imbalances on repurchase days (Cook et al. 2004). France: "large enough to stabilize prices, but do not lead to a higher share price." — [Harvard Law Forum on Busch & Obernberger](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2016/11/16/actual-share-repurchases-price-efficiency-and-the-information-content-of-stock-prices/); [Hillert et al.](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/stock-repurchases-and-liquidity/); [Cook et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v17y2004i2p463-498.html); [ScienceDirect, France](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426606002135)
- **[P/D]** "Blackouts remove the bid and the market sags": executions fall an estimated ~35% (desk claim, second-hand), but tests of returns find no negative blackout effect (SSGA; Alpha Architect). — [The Wealth Advisor/Bloomberg](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/goldman-buyback-desk-was-flooded-orders-during-stock-rout); [SSGA](https://www.ssga.com/library-content/pdfs/etf/us/b27-buyback-blackout-periods-do-not-negatively-impact-performance.pdf); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2019/01/buyback-blackout-periods-do-not-negatively-impact-market-performance/)
- **[D]** Brokers have timing discretion that can be used against a naive "average of daily VWAPs" benchmark, i.e. execution is not uniformly spread across days in VWAP-minus products. — [arXiv 2307.09617](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.09617)

### Inferences
- Realistic expectation for a large-cap day trader: when a heavy repurchaser is in an open window, some low-single-digit percent of the day's volume is a patient, price-capped buyer working between the open print and 3:50 pm ET, heavier on weak days. It will not defend a specific level on a given day in any way that can be verified, will not participate in a breakout, and is gone for the close. It is one more source of passive bid liquidity that slightly compresses downside volatility on average.
- Claim ratings: no chasing = documented (rule); absent at open print/last 10 min = documented (rule; compliance voluntary and historically high-but-imperfect); VWAP-like scheduling = practice; heavier on dips = practice plus low-frequency documented; price support/volatility dampening = documented at monthly horizon only; "steady visible bid", "support at round numbers", "support at VWAP", identifiable footprint on the tape = **unverified folklore** (no source found).
- Because the safe harbor is optional and unverifiable, an issuer or its broker could trade outside it (e.g. in the last 10 minutes); the 1990s trade-level sample shows deviations occurred. Do not treat the windows as absolute.
