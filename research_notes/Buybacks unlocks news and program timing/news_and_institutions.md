## 1. Headline / "news bot" trading: feeds, users, and how fast prices react

### Takeaway
Machine-readable news is a real, sold product aimed explicitly at automated and quant trading, and the documented reaction window for it is seconds (5 ms for macro numbers in index products; the first ~5 seconds of a 120-second window for company newswire items). For scheduled company news (earnings), most of the price adjustment happens in the after-hours/pre-market session through quote changes rather than trades, so by 9:30 the bulk of the information repricing has usually already happened.

### Cited Findings

**The feeds (what exists and who it is sold to)**
- [PRACTICE] Bloomberg describes Event-Driven Feeds as structured, machine-readable data "for automated trading"; a Bloomberg fact sheet says the feed is designed to help firms act on news "within milliseconds"; Bloomberg's own engineering account says the feeds are 8-11 ms faster on average than its previous products and use multicast distribution. No microsecond claim was found. — [Bloomberg EDF product page](https://www.bloomberg.com/professional/products/data/enterprise-catalog/event-driven-feeds/); [Bloomberg engineering story](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/stories/tackling-enterprise-customers-need-speed-real-time-meets-mandate/); [EDF textual news fact sheet](https://assets.bbhub.io/professional/sites/41/Fact-Sheet-EDF-Textual-News.pdf)
- [PRACTICE] Bloomberg's newer Real-Time News Feeds offer customisable "tickerized" subscriptions aimed at automated market-making, event-driven and quantitative trading. — [The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/bloomberg-enhances-real-time-news-feeds-offerings-with-customisable-capabilities-for-trading-and-risk-workflows/); [A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/bloomberg-transforms-real-time-news-into-structured-data-for-systematic-workflows/)
- [PRACTICE] LSEG Machine Readable News: its "fastest, lowest latency" tier is Headlines Direct, "designed specifically for event trading use cases"; MRN Real-Time News is the next tier; LSEG also sells analytics scoring sentiment, significance, relevance, novelty and readership. — [LSEG MRN page](https://lseg.com/en/data-analytics/financial-news-service/machine-readable-news)
- [PRACTICE] Dow Jones Elementized News Feed: an "ultra-low-latency" feed of economic and corporate news in discrete XML-tagged fields, marketed to proprietary traders and hedge fund managers; later expanded with corporate events/earnings-report fields and a Hong Kong co-location point. — [WatersTechnology launch report](https://www.waterstechnology.com/inside-market-data/news/1616422/dow-jones-launches-algo-news-feed); [WatersTechnology corporate events](https://www.waterstechnology.com/node/1933556); [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/17594/dow-jones-adds-company-news-to-machine-readable-xml-data-feed)
- [DOCUMENTED] RavenPack processes every Dow Jones Newswire article algorithmically and delivers article-level metrics (relevance, sentiment) to subscribers; this is the dataset used in the von Beschwitz-Keim-Massa study. — [Fed IFDP 1233 record](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/ifdp/first-to-quotreadquot-the-news-new-analytics-and-algorithmic-trading.htm); [RAPS 2020 record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rasset/v10y2020i1p122-178..html)
- [UNVERIFIED] A third-party commentary site says Bloomberg/Reuters/Refinitiv distribute news to subscribers within 100-300 ms of original publication; no methodology given and it does not match vendor framing. — [Frontier Ledger](https://frontierledger.ai/data-sourcing-alternative-data/real-time-news-feeds-latency-considerations-for-event-driven-trading)

**How fast prices react (studies)**
- [DOCUMENTED] Chordia, Green, Kottimukkalur (RFS 2018; SPY 2008-2014, E-mini July 2011-2014, 18 macro announcements): SPY and ES prices respond to macro surprises **within 5 milliseconds**; trading intensity rises more than 100-fold after release; profits to being fast are small (**about $19,000 per event in SPY, about $50,000 in ES**) and have not grown; order flow has become less informative, i.e. quotes reprice directly on the news rather than through trades. Buying the 2-second early peek at Consumer Sentiment did not show profits at slower traders' expense. NOTE: this is index products on macro data, not single stocks on company news. — [RFS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v31y2018i12p4650-4687..html); [working paper version](https://www.abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2016/annual-conference/investment-finance/38-do-high-frequency-traders-need-to-be-regulated-evidence-from-algorithmic-trading-on-macroeconomic-news.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED] von Beschwitz, Keim, Massa (RAPS 2020; sample described by Wharton as eight years ending 2012): RavenPack coverage speeds up both the price update and the volume response "in the first few seconds"; the reaction in the first 5 seconds relative to the total over 120 seconds is significantly larger for high-relevance articles; the authors read the pattern as algorithms causing an initial overreaction that slower traders correct; a citing paper adds that news analytics also reduce liquidity. — [RAPS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rasset/v10y2020i1p122-178..html); [VoxEU column](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/first-read-news-news-analytics-and-institutional-trading); [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/high-frequency-trading-profiting-news/)
- [DOCUMENTED] Groß-Klußmann & Hautsch (J. Empirical Finance 2011; London Stock Exchange stocks, 20-second VAR, Reuters news analytics): news arrivals produce measurable responses in returns, volatility, volume and spreads; only items flagged highly relevant matter; sentiment has some predictive power for the subsequent price trend, **but wider bid-ask spreads reduce the profitability of trading on it**. NOTE: UK stocks. — [JEF record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v18y2011i2p321-340.html); [working paper](https://publikationen.ub.uni-frankfurt.de/frontdoor/index/index/year/2010/docId/7389)
- [DOCUMENTED] Rogers, Skinner, Zechman, "Run EDGAR Run" (JAR 2017): paying subscribers to the SEC's Public Dissemination Service received filings before the public EDGAR posting; prices, volumes and spreads began moving roughly 15-30 seconds before public posting. A secondary source cites a 28 bp advantage over 81 seconds (not confirmed against the paper). The SEC subsequently removed the timing advantage. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2513350)
- [DOCUMENTED] Grégoire & Martineau (J. Accounting Research 2022): earnings surprises reach prices **mostly through quote changes, not trades**; after a positive surprise the ask moves quickly and the bid lags (reverse for negative); trade-based returns understate the speed and size of the reaction; 99.1% of earnings in their data are announced outside regular hours; median of only 16 after-hours trades after an announcement; cumulative returns during regular hours after the open are "mostly flat". — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3060094); [JAR](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1475-679X.12394)
- [DOCUMENTED, source level = thesis abstract] Martineau's UBC thesis: price discovery after earnings surprises mostly occurs after-hours and is "generally complete by 10 a.m."; 80% of the after-hours price response occurs on arrival of the first trades. Not confirmed to be in the published JAR paper. — [UBC thesis record](https://open.library.ubc.ca/cIRcle/collections/ubctheses/24/items/1.0348752)
- [DOCUMENTED] Christensen et al., "Warp speed price moves: jumps after earnings announcements" (JFE 2025): probability that an announcing firm's price jumps in the after-hours session exceeds 90%, versus 2.95% in regular sessions without announcements. — [JFE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X25000182); [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08962)
- [DOCUMENTED] Boudoukh, Feldman, Kogan, Richardson (RFS 2019): textually identified fundamental news explains **49.6% of overnight idiosyncratic volatility versus 12.4% during trading hours**; volatility on identified-news days is more than double other days; "complex" (multi-type) news days are 27% of overnight news days and 22% of trading-hours news days but explain 32.4% and 6.1% of idiosyncratic variance respectively. — [RFS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v32y2019i3p992-1033..html); [CFA Digest summary](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2019/08/dig-v49-n8-2)
- [DOCUMENTED] Jeon, McCurdy, Zhao (JFE 2022; ~21 million Factiva articles, 9,000+ firms): jump intensity and jump-size distributions depend on news flow and content, and the link has strengthened over recent decades; it is stronger for firms with more media visibility, analyst coverage and institutional ownership; analyst ratings, earnings, capital-structure changes, M&A and conference calls are the categories most associated with jumps; news frequency is the strongest single variable. — [JFE record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v145y2022i2p1-17.html); [Rotman summary](https://www-2.rotman.utoronto.ca/insightshub/finance-investing-accounting/news-stock-swings)
- [DOCUMENTED, old] Greene & Watts (1996), as cited in later work: for overnight earnings announcements the NYSE opening trade impounds most of the price response; daytime announcements spread the response over several trades. — [cited in Is the Opening Price Efficient?](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228261255_Is_the_Opening_Price_Efficient_Evidence_from_After-Hours_Earnings_Announcements)

### Inferences
- A working timeline consistent with the cited evidence (each row's support is in the findings above or in sections 2-5):

| Window | Who is plausibly dominant | Support |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 seconds after a machine-readable headline | Automated subscribers to low-latency feeds; market makers repricing quotes | Documented for macro news (5 ms) and Dow Jones/RavenPack items (first 5 s) |
| Seconds to minutes, after-hours | Quote repricing by liquidity providers on thin volume (median 16 trades) | Documented (Grégoire-Martineau) |
| Pre-market to ~10:00 | Residual price discovery completes; opening auction absorbs overnight imbalance | Documented at thesis level ("complete by 10 a.m."); Greene-Watts |
| Rest of Day 1 | Institutions reacting to news tone; elevated volume all day | Documented that institutions trade on tone right after first release (section 2); volume more than double normal (section 4) |
| Days 2-5 | Institutional follow-through; volume stays elevated about a week | Documented (Huang-Tan-Wermers 5-day return effect; Nasdaq volume) |
| Days 5-60 | Residual drift, concentrated where institutional attention was low and in small names; multi-day parent orders finishing | Documented but magnitude disputed (section 3) |

- The single-stock intraday edge from being "first to the headline" belongs to machines within seconds; nothing found supports a human discretionary edge in that window.
- Because regular-hours cumulative returns after earnings are "mostly flat" on average, the average Day 1 intraday directional edge from the earnings surprise itself is close to zero; any intraday trend on a given day is variance around that mean, not an average drift.

### Gaps
- No study was found that gives, for single-stock **unscheduled intraday** news (FDA, M&A, contract wins), the percentage of the total move completed at 1 s / 1 min / 30 min / close. The 5 s vs 120 s result is a ratio inside a two-minute window, not a share of the full-day move.
- No public evidence names which HFT or quant firms subscribe to which feeds. Vendor pages name customer types only.
- Benzinga and other press-release-parsing services: no independent latency or usage evidence found; not covered.
- Hu, Pan, Wang "Early peek advantage" could not be retrieved.
- Exact coefficients for von Beschwitz et al. and Chordia et al. were not seen; only abstract-level figures.

## 2. Institutional reaction speed: do long-only institutions trade Day 0 or later, with or against the news?

### Takeaway
The best institutional-trade evidence says institutions as a group trade in the direction of the news, starting right after the first release, with the largest share of trading in the post-announcement days rather than only on Day 0; the evidence that they anticipate news is mixed (yes in 2003-2005 NYSE order-flow data, no for unscheduled news in the Ancerno/newswire study). No source found gives a clean "X% trade on Day 0" split.

### Cited Findings
- [DOCUMENTED] Hendershott, Livdan, Schürhoff (JFE 2015; daily non-public institutional buy/sell data for ~1,700 NYSE stocks, 2003-2005, matched to Reuters news): institutional trading volume predicts the occurrence of news; institutional order flow predicts news sentiment, and returns on announcement days; conclusion is that institutions are informed about both content and timing of upcoming public news. — [JFE record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v117y2015i2p249-287.html); [Harvard Law forum post](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2015/10/27/are-institutions-informed-about-news)
- [DOCUMENTED] Huang, Tan, Wermers (RFS 2020; newswire releases matched to high-frequency institutional trades): institutions "chiefly trade on the tone of news directly after the earliest news release in a cluster"; they react quickly but do **not** predictively trade on unscheduled news; a one-standard-deviation increase in news-driven institutional trading is associated with an annualized abnormal return of 12.5 bp over the five days after the news, on top of the return predicted by tone. — [RFS record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:33:y:2020:i:10:p:4627-4675.); [Harvard Law forum summary](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2019/12/27/institutional-trading-around-corporate-news-evidence-from-textual-analysis/); [Waterloo summary](https://uwaterloo.ca/school-of-accounting-and-finance/corporate-news-and-institutional-trading)
- [DOCUMENTED] Ancerno study of earnings announcements, 2002-2010: institutional net trading is positively related to the earnings surprise before, during and after the announcement, with the **strongest relation in the post-announcement period**; the largest share of average daily institutional trading occurs after the announcement, followed by the immediate announcement window. — [Information discovery, interpretation and analysis by institutional investors around earnings announcements (JRFM)](https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm19040294); [working paper](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~akumas/Informationiscoveryinterpretationandanalysis4-2024.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED] A separate Ancerno-based paper finds average institutional trading is significantly higher in the direction of earnings news, read as consistent with market underreaction. — [Institutional trading, news, and accounting anomalies (JAE 2024)](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/JAE_2024.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED] Campbell, Ramadorai, Schwartz (JFE 2009; daily institutional flows inferred from TAQ matched to quarterly 13F): draft summary says institutional trading anticipates both earnings surprises and post-earnings drift. Only a draft-level summary was seen. — [JFE record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v92y2009i1p66-91.html); [Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/handle/1/2609649/Campbell_CaughtOnTape.pdf?sequence=2)
- [DOCUMENTED] Di Maggio, Franzoni, Kogan, Xing (NBER w31360): institutions unwind holdings before earnings announcements and re-establish them afterwards (avoiding idiosyncratic volatility); the authors link this pressure to the earnings-announcement premium. — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31360/w31360.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED] Ben-Rephael, Da, Israelsen (RFS 2017): abnormal institutional attention (AIA), built from Bloomberg terminal news search/reading, responds faster to major news than retail attention (Google search), leads it, and "facilitates permanent price adjustment"; drift after earnings announcements and analyst recommendation changes is driven by announcements institutions did not attend to. — [RFS record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:30:y:2017:i:9:p:3009-3047.); [working paper](https://www.abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2016/annual-conference/investment-finance/153-it-depends-on-where-you-search-a-comparison-of-institutional-and-retail-attention.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED] Irvine, Lipson, Puckett, "Tipping" (RFS 2007): abnormally high institutional volume and net buying begin about five days before analysts' initial buy recommendations are published; early buyers earn abnormal profits; the authors say the evidence is "consistent with tipping", not proof. Later work reports similar pre-upgrade buying; a Fed paper says tips occur 1-5 days before publication. — [RFS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v20y2007i3p741-768..html); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/2013/03/insider-trading-via-sell-side-analysts-welcome-to-the-jungle/)
- [DOCUMENTED, coverage caveat] Ancerno/Abel Noser data cover roughly 8% of CRSP dollar volume (1999-2011) and about 10% of institutional activity; other sources say up to 12%. All Ancerno results describe that client subset (largely pension plans and mutual fund managers). — [Hu et al., Institutional trading and Abel Noser data](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/80215/1/Hu_Institutional_Trading_Abel.pdf); [CFM/ANcerno paper](https://www.cfm.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/247-Slow-decay-of-impact-in-equity-markets.pdf)

### Inferences
- "With the news" is the supported answer for institutions in aggregate, both on and after the event day. Nothing found supports the folklore that long-only institutions systematically fade Day 1 gaps.
- "Institutions already positioned before the news" is supported for scheduled events and analyst initiations (Hendershott et al.; Campbell et al.; Irvine et al.) but not for unscheduled corporate news (Huang-Tan-Wermers). For a surprise FDA/M&A/contract headline, expect reaction, not anticipation.
- The post-announcement period carrying the largest share of institutional earnings-related trading is the main documented basis for expecting real Day 2+ institutional flow.
- High institutional attention on Day 0 implies a more complete Day 0 move and less drift; low attention implies more drift. For a widely watched large cap on a headline earnings day, the documented expectation is less follow-through, not more.

### Gaps
- No source gave the fraction of institutional event-related volume executed on Day 0 versus Days 1-5 versus later.
- No breakdown by institution type (hedge fund vs mutual fund vs pension) was retrieved; Ancerno under-represents hedge funds.
- AIA frequencies on earnings days and the drift magnitudes for high- vs low-attention announcements were not retrieved (paper not opened).
- Ancerno stopped supplying identifiable academic data in the mid-2010s; I found no post-2015 institutional trade-level evidence for the US.

## 3. PEAD and news drift; overnight vs intraday; the Day 1 path after an earnings gap

### Takeaway
PEAD is classic and well replicated historically, but its current size is disputed: Martineau (2022) says it has been absent in large stocks since 2006 and only recently disappeared in microcaps, while later papers and reviews say it persists at reduced size. For the intraday trader the firmer fact is that the earnings reaction is mostly an overnight event and regular-hours returns after the open are flat on average; I found no peer-reviewed large-sample study of gap-and-go versus gap-fade on earnings days.

### Cited Findings
- [DOCUMENTED] Martineau, "Rest in Peace Post-Earnings Announcement Drift" (Critical Finance Review 2022, vol. 11, pp. 613-646): prices now fully reflect earnings surprises on the announcement date; "for large stocks, PEAD have been non-existent since 2006 but has only disappeared recently for microcap stocks." — [CFR record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/now/jnlcfr/104.00000122.html); [published PDF](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED, via secondary summary] A 2025 UCLA Anderson Review brief reports that two accepted papers contradict Martineau and find PEAD "alive and well" (a secondary site names Dickerson, Julliard & Mueller in JFE and Hirshleifer, Peng & Wang in RFS), and that a Subrahmanyam working paper attributes the disagreement largely to research-design choices. I could not verify the papers' details. — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again)
- [DOCUMENTED] Hirshleifer, Peng, Wang (NBER w30860; RFS): earnings announcements from firms in more socially connected counties show stronger immediate price, volatility and volume reactions, weaker subsequent drift, but higher and more persistent volume. — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30860/w30860.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED, review article in a lower-tier outlet] A 2025 review concludes PEAD is persistent despite declining magnitude and scope. — [Review on PEAD](https://doi.org/10.54254/2754-1169/2025.22875)
- [UNVERIFIED] Non-peer-reviewed recent estimates conflict: one independent 2026 write-up reports the beat-minus-miss drift gap shrinking from 2.6 points (2023-24 announcements) to 0.5 points (2025-26); a Stockholm School of Economics thesis reports 60-day large-cap drift rising from 0.52% (2005-2020) to 1.99% after 2020. — [Truthsayer](https://www.truthsayer.com/insights/post-earnings-announcement-drift); [Quanterlab](https://quanterlab.substack.com/p/post-earnings-drift-in-the-point)
- [DOCUMENTED] Drift after earnings and after analyst recommendation changes is concentrated in announcements with low institutional attention. — [Ben-Rephael, Da, Israelsen RFS 2017](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:30:y:2017:i:9:p:3009-3047.)
- [DOCUMENTED] Overnight vs intraday: see section 1 (Grégoire-Martineau: regular-hours cumulative returns after the open "mostly flat"; Martineau thesis: discovery generally complete by 10 a.m.; Boudoukh et al.: identified news explains 49.6% of overnight vs 12.4% of trading-hours idiosyncratic volatility; Christensen et al.: after-hours jump probability above 90% on earnings). — [JAR](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1475-679X.12394); [RFS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v32y2019i3p992-1033..html); [JFE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X25000182)
- [DOCUMENTED] Wong: overnight returns are informative about earnings news, effects strongest on the first day after the announcement and more pronounced when news arrives after hours. — [The timing of information arrival and overnight returns](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0278425422001181)
- [UNVERIFIED, backtest, not peer-reviewed] Zarattini, Barbon, Aziz, "A Profitable Day Trading Strategy for the U.S. Equity Market" (SFI Research Paper 24-98; 7,000+ US stocks, Jan 2016-Dec 2023): a 5-minute opening-range breakout restricted to "Stocks in Play" (opening-range relative volume at least 100%, top 20 by relative volume, with price/liquidity/volatility floors) is reported at over 1,600% total net return, Sharpe 2.81, annualized alpha 36%; a QuantConnect recreation reported Sharpe about 2.4. This is the closest thing found to a large-sample test of opening-range continuation on news-volume days, but it is a backtest by authors with a commercial day-trading affiliation, not earnings-specific, and cost assumptions were not verified. — [SFI record](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2498.html); [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/intraday-trading-of-overactive-stocks-via-opening-range-breakout/); [QuantConnect replication](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)
- [UNVERIFIED, practitioner] A non-peer-reviewed study of 6,552 overnight gaps in SPY, QQQ, IWM, NVDA, TSLA, AAPL (2022-2026), **excluding earnings events**: gap-ups above 2% (n=327) had median intraday return +0.03% and same-sign continuation 50.5%; best continuation evidence fell between about 09:45 and 10:30. — [Vortex Capital "Gap Map"](https://www.vortexcapitalgroup.com/insights/the-gap-map-when-us-equity-gaps-continue-fade-and-mean-nothing)

### Inferences
- Reconciling the two documented facts (flat average regular-hours return after earnings; strong reported results for relative-volume opening-range breakouts): the average Day 1 intraday drift in the gap direction is about zero, but conditional intraday trends on high-relative-volume days may be exploitable in either direction. The first is peer-reviewed; the second is a backtest.
- "Gap continues" versus "gap fades" on earnings Day 1 is not settled by any large-sample peer-reviewed study found here. Treat both gap-and-go and gap-fade statistics circulating among traders as folklore until tested on own data.
- Where drift still exists it is most plausible in smaller, less-followed names with low institutional attention. That is the opposite of the liquid large-cap "in play" names where intraday desks concentrate.

### Gaps
- Bernard & Thomas (1989/1990) original magnitudes were not retrieved in this session; cite from the papers directly.
- Drift after guidance changes specifically: nothing retrieved.
- Day 2 continuation conditional on Day 1 close location (e.g. close near high): no study found.
- Martineau's exact magnitudes by size bucket and period, and the designs of the rebuttal papers, were not seen.
- No earnings-specific, peer-reviewed intraday path study (gap size vs open-to-close return, first 30 minutes vs rest of day) was found.

## 4. Volume and execution on news days

### Takeaway
Documented: volume on the session after earnings is well over double normal and stays elevated about a week. That a historical-curve VWAP mis-schedules on such days, and that desks switch to POV, arrival-price or liquidity-seeking logic, is standard practitioner reasoning and vendor design, but I found no broker TCA study quantifying it; the specific "30-40% of volume at the open" figure is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- [DOCUMENTED, exchange research] Nasdaq Economic Research (Phil Mackintosh): pre-earnings sessions run about 50% above normal volume; the session after results is "well over double normal", and trading "stays elevated for around a week"; volume rises more after a miss than a beat; the earnings release matters much more than the later filing date for returns and volume. — [Nasdaq, Earnings Announcements Sliced and Diced](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/earnings-announcements-sliced-and-diced)
- [PRACTICE] A VWAP algorithm forecasts the day's volume curve from historical averages and fills the parent order in proportion; a static VWAP fixes the schedule up front, a dynamic one updates as realized volume arrives; POV trades a fixed share of real-time volume (for example 10%) and so adapts to actual liquidity but has an unknown completion time. — [Execution algorithms overview](https://pnlpro.fit/encyclopedia/algorithmic-trading/execution-algorithms); [Arithmion intraday volume profiles](https://arithmion.com/learn/en/depth-execution/intraday-volume-profiles/)
- [UNVERIFIED] One educational source says that on a surprise earnings print the opening burst can be 30-40% of daily volume. Single source, no dataset; the same page has internal inconsistencies. — [Arithmion](https://arithmion.com/learn/en/depth-execution/intraday-volume-profiles/)
- [PRACTICE, vendor research] Bloomberg's "Quintet Volume Projection" paper (Markov et al.) addresses intraday volume forecasting for execution algorithms. I could not open it, so its treatment of special days is unconfirmed. — [arXiv 1904.01412](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.01412)
- [PRACTICE, survey] The TRADE's Algorithmic Trading Survey (long-only 2024): VWAP was the most-cited algo (79% of respondents) with dark liquidity seeking at 77%; 2020 hedge-fund tables show dark liquidity seeking about 70% and VWAP about 62%; VWAP TCA remains the most common performance metric, with implementation shortfall TCA and liquidity capture also common. PDF extractions were garbled in places. — [The TRADE long-only 2024](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Algo-survey.pdf); [The TRADE hedge funds 2020](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/TT-2020-Q2-Algo.pdf); [The TRADE hedge funds 2026](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Algo-Survey-HF-2026.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED] Nasdaq (Nov 2026 article on 23/5 trading): index benchmarks, regular-session VWAP calculations and day high/low will remain centered on the 9:30-16:00 session under proposed extended hours. — [Nasdaq, All About How 23/5 Trading Will Work](https://www.nasdaq.com/economic-institute/the-print/all-about-how-235-trading-will-work)
- [DOCUMENTED] On triple-witching days Nasdaq open auctions are about 10 times normal (about $28 billion larger). Included only to show special-day auction distortion is measured for expirations; no equivalent earnings-day auction figure was found. — [Nasdaq, The Powerful Impact of Triple Witching](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/the-powerful-impact-of-triple-witching-2021-06-10)

### Inferences
- Mechanically, if realized volume is front-loaded relative to the historical curve, a static VWAP schedule under-trades early and over-trades late relative to true VWAP. If price also trends in the news direction, a buyer on a good-news day ends up buying later at worse prices. This is logic, not a measured result from the sources found.
- Because VWAP remains the most-used algo and the most-used TCA benchmark in buy-side surveys, VWAP-benchmarked institutional flow is likely present on Day 1 in liquid names. Whether a given desk uses a curve-following VWAP or a participation/arrival-price strategy on an event day is not observable from public data.
- Volume staying elevated for about a week is consistent with (but does not prove) multi-day institutional repositioning after earnings.

### Gaps
- **No broker or TCA publication (Virtu/ITG, Goldman, JPM, Bernstein, Bloomberg) on event-day execution was retrieved.** Searches returned retail education pages. Kissell's textbook treatment of special-day volume profiles was not retrieved.
- No measured intraday volume curve for earnings days versus normal days (share of volume by half-hour) was found.
- No data on what share of institutional Day 1 flow uses VWAP vs POV vs IS vs liquidity-seeking.

## 5. How long institutional parent orders last, and what changes that

### Takeaway
Documented at summary level: Ancerno parent orders average 1.38 trading days and 1.74 brokers, durations and participation rates are heavy-tailed (power-law), and classic studies treat multi-day "packages" as the normal unit for large managers. The specific duration tables (share done in one day, share lasting four-plus days, duration by size or urgency) were not retrievable here, and no study found measures how long a fund takes to build or exit a position specifically after news.

### Cited Findings
- [DOCUMENTED] An Ancerno-based paper reports parent orders are on average split across **1.74 brokers and 1.38 trading days**. — ["Splitting and Shuffling: Institutional Trading Motives and Order Submissions Across Brokers"](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/ibSfSdEb)
- [DOCUMENTED] Bucci et al. (CFM; more than 8 million Ancerno metaorders of US institutional investors): participation rate and duration are both well approximated by truncated power-law distributions over several orders of magnitude; "metaorders are often executed over several days", producing long-range autocorrelation in order flow; impact decays slowly after completion. — [Slow decay of impact in equity markets (CFM)](https://www.cfm.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/247-Slow-decay-of-impact-in-equity-markets.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.05332)
- [DOCUMENTED] Chan & Lakonishok (J. Finance 1995; all trades of 37 large investment managers, July 1986-December 1988): analyze the whole sequence of trades ("package") as one order; impact and cost depend on firm capitalization, relative package size and, most importantly, the identity of the manager. Earlier 1993 JFE work on single trades found small average impact with a buy/sell asymmetry (secondary summary: +0.34% buys, -0.04% sells; about 1% for buy packages). — [JF record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:50:y:1995:i:4:p:1147-74); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6574)
- [DOCUMENTED] Keim & Madhavan (JFE 1995; equity transactions of 21 institutions of differing styles): data include the number of days needed to fill an order and order-type choice; trade duration and order type vary with investment style and trade motivation. — [JFE record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:37:y:1995:i:3:p:371-398); [Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/fnce_papers/383)
- [DOCUMENTED] Anand, Irvine, Puckett, Venkataraman (RFS 2012; Ancerno): institutional trading desks show persistent relative cost performance; a FINRA paper cites their average institutional trading cost of about 25 bp over 1999-2008. — [RFS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v25y2012i2p557-598.html); [FINRA OCE working paper](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/OCE_WP_jan2019.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED] Busse, Chordia, Jiang, Tang (Management Science 2021; Abel Noser trades matched to 583 funds in an earlier version): larger mutual funds trade less frequently and hold larger stocks to avoid higher trading costs. — [MS record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:67:y:2021:i:2:p:1227-1248)
- [DOCUMENTED] Frazzini, Israel, Moskowitz, "Trading Costs" (AQR live executions, about $1.7 trillion, 21 developed markets, Sept 1998-June 2016): trade size as a fraction of daily volume is the most important price-impact variable; average spread at arrival about 21.33 bp but AQR rarely paid even half the spread because it works passive limit orders; an earlier version reports market impact of about 11.21 bp for large-cap and 21.27 bp for small-cap trades, about 16 bp for the average long-only trade versus 10 bp long-short; actual costs are "an order of magnitude smaller than previous studies suggest". — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3229719); [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Trading-Costs); [Swedroe summary](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-trading-cost-truths); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/the-best-research-paper-ever-written-on-trading-costs/)
- [DOCUMENTED] van Kervel & Menkveld (J. Finance 2019; large institutional orders vs. large HFTs; data are Swedish/European, not US): HFTs initially lean against large institutional orders but eventually turn and trade with them for long-lasting, most-informed orders; institutional trading cost is 46% lower when HFTs lean against the order; institutions appear to trade off speed of profit capture against the risk of being detected. — [JF record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:74:y:2019:i:3:p:1091-1137); [Tinbergen working paper](https://papers.tinbergen.nl/17092.pdf)

### Inferences
- An average of 1.38 trading days with a power-law tail implies most parent orders by count finish within a day, while a minority of large orders run for many days and carry a disproportionate share of dollar volume. The count-based "typical order" and the dollar-weighted "typical flow" are different things; the multi-day tail is what matters for a trader hoping to lean on persistent flow.
- Drivers of duration supported by the cited work: order size relative to daily volume (Frazzini et al.; Chan-Lakonishok), manager identity and style (Chan-Lakonishok; Keim-Madhavan), and the detection/urgency trade-off (van Kervel-Menkveld). News days more than double available volume (section 4), so for a given size and participation cap an order completes faster on Day 1 than on a normal day. That last step is arithmetic, not a measured result.
- Patient, passive, low-participation execution of the AQR type is slow-signal flow and is unlikely to be the urgent flow behind a Day 1 trend. Fundamental managers reacting to a thesis change have faster alpha decay and more reason to be aggressive, but that is not measured in the sources found.
- "A fund takes days to weeks to build or exit a position after news" is a practitioner belief consistent with the heavy-tailed duration evidence and week-long elevated volume, but it is not directly documented by any source retrieved.

### Gaps
- Exact duration distributions were not retrievable. From my recollection (not verified this session, do not cite without checking): Chan & Lakonishok report that a majority of package dollar value is in packages lasting four or more days and only about a fifth completes within one day; Keim & Madhavan report mean order durations of under two days. Verify against the original tables.
- AQR's average trade size as percent of daily volume and trade horizon in days: not retrieved.
- Order duration conditional on news, earnings, volatility, or explicit PM urgency instructions: no study found.
- Time to complete a full position build/exit after a catalyst (as opposed to a single parent order): no study found; 13F data are quarterly and cannot resolve it.
- Ancerno evidence ends around 2014-2015; nothing found for the 2020s US market.

## 6. Analyst and index-driven follow-through; "Changing Fundamentals" frameworks

### Takeaway
Analyst recommendation changes have a documented drift lasting months in older data (larger and longer for downgrades), and institutions demonstrably trade ahead of some analyst reports. The SMB Capital "Changing Fundamentals" and "2nd Day" plays are attributed practice with no published statistical test found.

### Cited Findings
- [DOCUMENTED, old sample] Womack (J. Finance 1996; First Call recommendation changes 1989-1991, about 1,573 observations), as cited in later work: returns continue to drift for months in the direction of the initial reaction; mean post-event drift is modest and short-lived for buys (+2.4%) and larger for sells (-9.1%), lasting up to six months for downgrades. Seen only through secondary citations. — [The correlation between stock returns before and after analyst recommendation revisions](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/350482859_The_correlation_between_stock_returns_before_and_after_analyst_recommendation_revisions); [Bradshaw et al., Predictability of analyst stock recommendation revisions](https://www.bayes.citystgeorges.ac.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/681939/Flake_20220318.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED] Bradshaw et al.: market reactions to recommendation revisions are attenuated when the revision is more predictable, i.e. part of the move precedes publication. — [Bradshaw et al.](https://www.bayes.citystgeorges.ac.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/681939/Flake_20220318.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED] Drift after analyst recommendation changes is concentrated where institutional attention (Bloomberg AIA) is low. — [Ben-Rephael, Da, Israelsen RFS 2017](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:30:y:2017:i:9:p:3009-3047.)
- [DOCUMENTED] Institutional buying rises about five days ahead of analyst initiations with buy ratings ("Tipping"). — [Irvine, Lipson, Puckett RFS 2007](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v20y2007i3p741-768..html)
- [DOCUMENTED] Analyst ratings are among the news categories most associated with price jumps. — [Jeon, McCurdy, Zhao JFE 2022](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v145y2022i2p1-17.html)
- [PRACTICE] Mike Bellafiore (SMB Capital) names the "changing fundamentals" trade as his answer to what a new trader should learn first, describing a large shift in a company's fundamentals creating multiple trading opportunities, and says one training class had to put these trades at the top of its stock-selection list. — [MoneyShow article](https://www.moneyshow.com/articles/daytraders-42251)
- [PRACTICE] An SMB "PlayBook" presentation lists "2nd Day Earnings" under intraday fundamental plays; no rules or statistics appear in what was retrieved. — [SMB PlayBook slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/singapore-ifest-2013/25178389)
- [UNVERIFIED] A third-party page describes a "buy on 2nd day continuation or pullback" strategy loosely attributed to SMB; it is not an SMB source. — [forex.in.rs](https://www.forex.in.rs/smb-capital-strategy1)

### Inferences
- The documented chain that could support a multi-day "changing fundamentals" move is: institutions trade in the news direction with most trading after the announcement (section 2); volume stays elevated about a week (section 4); analyst revisions cluster after events and carry their own drift in older samples. Each link is documented separately; the combined Day 2/Day 3 continuation trade as traded by prop desks has not been tested in any source found.
- Pre-publication institutional buying ahead of analyst actions means the morning-after upgrade is partly priced before it prints. That argues against assuming an upgrade headline alone gives a fresh intraday edge.

### Gaps
- Altınkılıç & Hansen's intraday "piggybacking" evidence (that many analyst revisions follow news rather than create it) could not be retrieved; it is the main counter-view and should be read before relying on analyst-driven follow-through.
- Post-2010 evidence on recommendation and estimate-revision drift magnitudes: not retrieved.
- Price-target-change effects, the timeline of estimate revisions over the weeks after an event, 13F/ownership changes after catalysts, and index-driven follow-through: nothing retrieved.
- No statistical test of SMB's Changing Fundamentals or 2nd Day plays was found, by SMB or anyone else.

## 7. Which stocks get VWAP/TWAP-style algorithmic execution; do institutions trade sub-$300M low-float names?

### Takeaway
Buy-side surveys confirm VWAP and dark liquidity-seeking are the two most-used algo types overall, but I found no public breakdown of algo choice by market-cap segment. Evidence that institutions are light in micro-caps and that retail-style flow concentrates there is exchange research and industry commentary, not peer-reviewed size-decile data.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTICE, survey] The TRADE long-only survey 2024: 79% of respondents use VWAP, 77% dark liquidity seeking; hedge-fund surveys show growth in algo-wheel usage; no market-cap breakdown is given. — [The TRADE long-only 2024](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Algo-survey.pdf); [The TRADE hedge funds 2025](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Algo-Survey-HF-2025.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED, exchange research, 2019] Nasdaq (Mackintosh), using FINRA non-ATS TRF (off-exchange) volume as a retail proxy: retail activity concentrates in microcaps ("retail appear to love microcap stocks"), mid-caps are what retail trades least, and annual turnover falls sharply for microcaps, which the author reads as institutions being "underweight" them. No percentages were visible in the retrieved text. — [Nasdaq, What Types of Stocks Do Retail Investors Trade?](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/what-types-of-stocks-do-retail-investors-trade-2019-08-08)
- [UNVERIFIED] An industry guide says institutional holdings in micro-caps are typically under 20% versus roughly 70-80% for large caps, and that founders/insiders/private-placement holders often retain 30-50% or more of shares outstanding. No primary data cited. — [TitanFX micro-cap guide](https://research.titanfx.com/us-stock-trading/micro-cap)
- [PRACTICE] O'Shaughnessy Asset Management estimates about $72 billion of roughly $100 billion of market cap in its microcap universe is freely tradable, and treats that as a hard capacity limit for large investors. — [OSAM microcaps commentary](https://www.osam.com/Commentary/microcaps-factor-spreads-structural-biases-and-the-institutional-imperative)
- [DOCUMENTED, old] Keim (Wharton working paper, year-end 1996 data): institutional ownership rises with market cap in a nonlinear way. Decile figures not seen. — [The Changing Nature of Institutional Stock Investing](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/03.14.Keim_.pdf)
- [DOCUMENTED] Larger mutual funds deliberately hold bigger stocks and trade less often to avoid trading costs. — [Busse, Chordia, Jiang, Tang](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:67:y:2021:i:2:p:1227-1248)
- [DOCUMENTED] AQR's realized market impact is roughly twice as high in small caps as in large caps (about 21 bp vs 11 bp in an earlier version of the paper). — [Swedroe summary of Frazzini, Israel, Moskowitz](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-trading-cost-truths)
- [DOCUMENTED] The news-jump relationship is stronger in firms with more analyst coverage and institutional ownership. — [Jeon, McCurdy, Zhao JFE 2022](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v145y2022i2p1-17.html)

### Inferences
- The institutional-execution story in sections 2, 4 and 5 (VWAP-benchmarked parent orders, multi-day packages, news-direction institutional flow) rests on data dominated by stocks institutions actually hold. Applying it to sub-$300M low-float momentum names is not supported by anything retrieved; the available evidence points to those names being retail-heavy with low institutional presence.
- Schedule-based algos (VWAP/TWAP) need a reasonably stable volume curve and enough depth to hide in, which is a mid/large-cap property. For thin names the logical alternatives are participation-capped, liquidity-seeking or high-touch/block execution. This is standard practitioner reasoning, not a finding from the sources retrieved.
- A VWAP line on a low-float runner's chart is a retail and market-maker reference level; no retrieved evidence shows institutional VWAP algos operating in such names.

### Gaps
- No public data on algo strategy mix by market-cap or ADV bucket.
- No current (2020s) institutional ownership by size decile and no retail share of volume by size bucket with actual percentages. The Nasdaq 2019 chart values were not retrievable.
- No evidence on hedge-fund or small-cap-specialist participation in sub-$300M catalyst names specifically.
- Off-exchange share is an imperfect retail proxy; treat the Nasdaq inference accordingly.
