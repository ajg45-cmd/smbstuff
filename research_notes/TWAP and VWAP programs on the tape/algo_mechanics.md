## 1. How does a VWAP algo build its schedule, and how does TWAP differ?

### Takeaway
A VWAP algo pre-computes a schedule by applying the stock's historical intraday volume curve to the parent order between its start and end time, then works each time bucket with mostly passive limit orders, turning aggressive only when it falls behind; TWAP ignores volume entirely and trades by the clock (25% done after a quarter of the window, 50% after half). Broker documentation confirms the main user parameters: start/end time, max % of volume, limit price, whether to continue past end time, whether to take liquidity, and whether to join auctions.

### Cited Findings
- IBKR's best-efforts VWAP "seeks to achieve the Volume-Weighted Average price (VWAP), calculated from the time you submit the order to the close of the market" (i.e. the benchmark is interval VWAP from order arrival to close, not necessarily full-day VWAP) — [IBKR VWAP algo page](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/index.php?f=1124)
- IBKR VWAP lets the user set a maximum participation of up to 50% of ADV, and "The user can request the order to continue beyond its stated end time if unfilled at the end of the stated period" — [IBKR VWAP algo page](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/index.php?f=1124)
- IBKR TWS guide, VWAP "allow past end time" setting: "If checked, the algo will attempt completion by the specified end time, but will continue to work past the end time to execute any unfilled portion." — [IBKR TWS Users' Guide, VWAP](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/software/tws.bak/usersguidebook/algos/vwap.htm)
- IBKR TWAP: "The system trades based on the clock, i.e., on a 2-hour order the system is 25% done after 30 minutes, 50% done after an hour, etc. Unlike VWAP, TWAP (time weighted average price) does not speed up/slow down based on projected volume or price moves." (attribution approximate; returned from IBKR's third-party-algo descriptions) — [IBKR algo descriptions](https://interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/software/tws.bak/ibalgostop.htm)
- IBKR third-party algo descriptions include a volume-curve algo that "allows the user flexibility to control how much the strategy has to be ahead or behind the expected volume", and a TWAP variant that "allows the user flexibility to control how much leeway the model has to be off the expected fill rate" — this is broker-documented evidence that the ahead/behind tracking band is a user-settable parameter (attribution approximate) — [IBKR third-party algos](https://www.interactivebrokers.ie/es/index.php?f=4985)
- Jefferies VWAP (as offered through IBKR): "This strategy automatically manages transactions to achieve the all-day or intra-day VWAP through a proprietary algorithm"; has a trigger-price field — [IBKR guide, Jefferies VWAP](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/software/tws.bak/usersguidebook/algos/jeffvwap.htm)
- Jefferies TWAP: "This strategy spreads transactions evenly over the designated time period by slicing the total order quantity into smaller orders." Optional fields: start time, end time, a relative limit in basis points from arrival price, a volume limit (max % of volume), auction participation, trading session — [IBKR guide, Jefferies TWAP](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/software/tws.bak/usersguidebook/algos/jefftwap.htm)
- Jefferies algo menu on IBKR: VWAP, TWAP, Volume Participation, Seek, Strike, Buyback, Post. API "WoWMode" (would-if-well/"I would" style switch) values: "BLITZ", "DARKSeek", "Seek_Passive", "Seek_Active", "Seek_Aggressive", "Volume_10%", "Volume_20%", "Volume_30%", "VWAP_Day", "Patience" — i.e. when price reaches a user-set trigger level the order flips from its base strategy to one of these modes — [IBKR TWS API, Jefferies algos](https://interactivebrokers.github.io/tws-api/jefferies.html)
- Schedule construction (vendor documentation): each clip size "is determined according to the historical weekly and daily volume profile of the instrument" — [Deltix Ember VWAP algorithm docs](https://ember.deltixlab.com/docs/algos/vwap/)
- Worked example of bucketing: if 10% of daily volume historically trades 10:00-10:05, the algo routes 10% of the parent in that window (tutorial; illustrative number, not a real statistic) — [HF trading book, VWAP/TWAP/POV](https://hftradingbook.com/execution/vwap-twap-pov)
- Catch-up: the algo "may send more aggressive marketable orders if it is behind schedule"; with a max-participation cap, "even if the algorithm is behind schedule, it will not exceed the specified share of observed volume" (secondary/tutorial sources) — [ClearStreet on TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/chart/NVDA/8TssIcpc-What-Are-TWAP-and-VWAP-and-How-Do-They-Work/); [Deltix Ember docs](https://ember.deltixlab.com/docs/algos/vwap/)
- Static vs dynamic: when today's volume deviates substantially from the historical profile a static VWAP schedule is poorly calibrated; dynamic variants re-plan the remaining schedule as the session unfolds (blog-level source) — [Halley Tech substack](https://halleytech.substack.com/p/execution-algorithms-vwap-twap-pov-quant-research)
- A VWAP schedule "keeps your participation rate roughly constant all day" (because it trades in proportion to expected volume), whereas a fixed-rate POV tracks realized volume and leaves the finish time uncertain (blog-level source) — [HMA Quant substack](https://hmaquant.substack.com/p/vwap-twap-and-the-art-of-not-moving)
- Late-day skew of the US curve (informal, podcast speaker, not sourced to a dataset): "you can have half of the day's volume traded after 3:00 PM, 40% after 3:30 PM" on heavy days — [TD Securities "Bid Out" ep. 71](https://www.tdsecurities.com/ca/en/bid-out-episode-71). Treat as an anecdote about heavy-close days, not an average; it conflicts with the ~8-9% average closing-auction share in section 6.

### Inferences
- A full-day VWAP parent in a liquid stock is therefore heaviest 9:30-10:00 and 15:00-16:00 and lightest around midday, and its minute-by-minute participation rate is roughly flat: a 10%-of-ADV order shows up as ~10% of each interval's volume all day (follows from the "participation roughly constant" finding).
- Because limit price and max-%-volume caps override the schedule, a VWAP buy program visibly stops when price trades above its limit and then is behind schedule; when price comes back inside the limit it trades faster than the curve to catch up (follows from the documented limit/relative-limit, tracking-band and allow-past-end-time parameters). The exact catch-up rule is broker-proprietary.
- **UNVERIFIED RECALL (practitioner textbooks; Johnson "Algorithmic Trading & DMA", Kissell; not confirmed this session):**
  - Volume curves are typically built from roughly 20-30 trading days of history per stock (or a sector/liquidity-bucket composite for thin names), in bins of 1 to 15 minutes (5-minute and 15-minute bins are the common textbook examples), with separate curves for special days (index rebalance, options expiration/quad witching, half days, Fed days).
  - Within a bin the child-order logic is usually: post passively at the near touch first, reprice as the quote moves, and cross the spread only when the fill shortfall versus the schedule exceeds the tracking band or the bin is about to expire. Slice sizes and timing are randomized to defeat pattern detection.
  - "I would" price: a discretionary level at which the client is willing to complete the whole residual (in the dark or aggressively) regardless of schedule. "Must complete" / "force completion" makes the algo cross the spread or send the residual to the closing auction rather than finish short.
  - Desks choose TWAP over VWAP when the volume curve is unreliable or meaningless: illiquid/small-cap names with lumpy prints, newly listed stocks, pre/post-market sessions, half days, and short windows (e.g. 30-60 minutes) where a historical curve adds little.

### Gaps
- No primary broker spec was retrieved that states the numeric width of a VWAP tracking band (e.g. +/- X% of schedule) or the exact bin length; these are proprietary.
- Barry Johnson and Kissell texts were not accessible; the bin-size, look-back and passive/aggressive ladder details above are recall only.
- No sourced statement on when desks prefer TWAP over VWAP was found in a search extract.

## 2. How do POV and Implementation Shortfall algos behave, and what share of flow uses each algo type?

### Takeaway
POV/Inline reacts only to realized volume (trade X% of whatever prints, no fixed end time), while IS/Arrival Price algos front-load according to an urgency/risk-aversion setting and are benchmarked to the mid-quote at order arrival. In survey data VWAP, dark liquidity-seeking and %-of-volume are consistently the three most-used algo types; in The TRADE's 2026 hedge fund survey 83% of respondents used VWAP, 75% dark liquidity-seeking, 69% % volume, 47% TWAP, 43% single-stock IS.

### Cited Findings
- IBKR Percent of Volume: clients can set a value between 1-50% to control participation between stated start and end times; the algo may vary from the target within a client-set range — [IBKR Percent of Volume algo](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/trading/orders/percent-of-volume.php)
- IBKR algo training material: ADV is the reference used when choosing a participation rate; "You can use any value from .01% to 50%." — [IBKR Campus, Size Variant Percentage of Volume](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/trading-lessons/size-variant-percentage-of-volume-2/)
- IBKR offers POV variants whose rate changes with price, size remaining or time (Price Variant, Size Variant, Time Variant Percentage of Volume) — [IBKR Campus, Size Variant Percentage of Volume](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/trading-lessons/size-variant-percentage-of-volume-2/)
- IBKR Arrival Price: "Minimizes implementation shortfall against the arrival price"; benchmark is "the bid/ask midpoint at the time the order is submitted"; urgency/risk-aversion levels are Get Done, Aggressive, Neutral, Passive; "Get Done is more aggressive than the Aggressive selection. Neutral is slightly more aggressive than is Passive." — [IBKR Arrival Price algo](https://interactivebrokers.ie/en/trading/orders/arrival-price.php); [IBKR TWS API IB algos](https://interactivebrokers.github.io/tws-api/ibalgos.html)
- Almgren-Chriss framework: IS algos trade off market impact against timing risk via a risk-aversion parameter; the risk-neutral limit degenerates to TWAP (a straight-line schedule) and higher risk aversion front-loads the schedule (secondary summaries of Almgren & Chriss 2000) — [Halley Tech substack](https://halleytech.substack.com/p/execution-algorithms-vwap-twap-pov-quant-research); [VWAP research briefing](https://vwap.ashconvexity.workers.dev/)
- Contested point: a later analysis (cited in a patent filing) argues optimal schedules for risk-averse traders are less front-loaded than the original Almgren-Chriss solution suggests — [USPTO 8301548](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8301548)
- "Beating VWAP" can still be a bad execution versus arrival if the price drifted away all day; that is the core difference in what the two benchmarks reward (blog-level) — [Halley Tech substack](https://halleytech.substack.com/p/execution-algorithms-vwap-twap-pov-quant-research)
- The TRADE 2026 Algorithmic Trading Survey, hedge funds, "Types of algorithms used (% of responses)": VWAP 83.46%, dark liquidity seeking 74.80%, % volume 68.50%, TWAP 47.24%, implementation shortfall (single stock) 42.52% — [The TRADE Algo Survey HF 2026](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Algo-Survey-HF-2026.pdf)
- The TRADE hedge fund survey: implementation shortfall (basket) usage rose from 10.91% in 2021 to 24.67% in 2025; dark liquidity seeking was 74% in the 2024 hedge fund edition and declined in 2025 — [The TRADE Algo Survey HF 2025](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Algo-Survey-HF-2025.pdf); [The TRADE, Beyond the Data](https://www.thetradenews.com/beyond-the-data-hedge-funds-use-of-dark-liquidity-seeking-algos-dips-for-the-second-consecutive-year)
- Long-only 2025: "volume weighted average price (VWAP), dark liquidity seeking, and volume percentage emerged as the top three" algo types used — [Datos Insights summary of 2025 long-only survey](https://datos-insights.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/20250805_Global-Algorithmic-Trading-Survey-2025_Meeting-the-Demands-of-the-Long-Only_Report_Summary.pdf)
- Long-only 2022: % volume, dark liquidity seeking and VWAP were each used by roughly three out of four participants — [The TRADE Long-Only Algo Survey 2022](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Algorithmic-Trading-Survey-Long-Only-2022.pdf)
- Long-only 2026 survey sample: 52 long-only responses (58 in 2025); it contains an "Algorithm usage by value traded" table whose numbers I could not extract — [The TRADE Algo Survey LO 2026](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Algo-Survey-LO-2026-1.pdf)
- How algos are measured (TCA benchmark used): hedge funds 2025 — VWAP TCA 57%, implementation shortfall TCA 53%, liquidity capture 49%; long-only 2024 — VWAP TCA 34%, IS TCA 32%, liquidity capture 20% — [The TRADE Algo Survey HF 2025](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Algo-Survey-HF-2025.pdf); [The TRADE Long-Only Algo Survey 2024](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Algo-survey.pdf)

### Inferences
- The TRADE figures are "% of respondents who use this algo type at all", **not** share of volume or notional. No source found here gives the share of institutional share volume executed by VWAP vs POV vs IS. Do not present 83% as "83% of flow is VWAP".
- For tape reading: a POV program has no schedule, so it is invisible in quiet tape and gets bigger exactly when volume surges (it chases volume, including volume caused by other aggressors); an IS program is largest in its first minutes and decays; a VWAP program is U-shaped regardless of what price does.
- **UNVERIFIED RECALL:** IS algos are commonly "price adaptive": a buy order speeds up when price is favourable (below arrival, "in the money") in aggressive-in-the-money mode, or slows when price is favourable in passive-in-the-money mode; brokers expose this as a setting. Broker names for POV include "Inline", "Volume Inline", "Participate", "With Volume", "Follow".

## 3. Typical order sizes vs ADV, participation rates, durations, and behaviour at completion or limit

### Takeaway
Academic datasets of real institutional algo orders show that the typical parent is small and slow: average participation of roughly 2-5% of volume over its life, average duration ~84 minutes in one large sample, with child orders overwhelmingly limit orders (under 0.4% market orders). Broker caps are far wider (IBKR allows 0.01%-50%; Jefferies presets are 10/20/30%).

### Cited Findings
- NBER paper on institutional execution algorithms (2012-2016 data, 2.3 million parent orders generating over 300 million child orders): "The average parent order attempts to trade $287,000 over 84 minutes, equivalent to 4.80 percent of volume over the duration of the order." — [NBER conference paper](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f145256/f145256.pdf)
- Same sample: fewer than 0.40% of child orders are market orders; limit orders are 81.5% of child orders — [NBER conference paper](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f145256/f145256.pdf)
- Anand et al. (2011), Ancerno institutional database: average daily participation rate about 2.5%; van Kervel & Menkveld (2019), ~5,000 parent orders (Sweden): average trade size $2.2 million, participation rate 3.6% — [Management Science supplement](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/mnsc.2023.4871/suppl_file/mnsc.2023.4871.sm1.pdf)
- A separate study of 20,000+ parent orders: average order size roughly $1 million, about 1.8% of volume traded during the execution (attribution approximate) — [NBER conference paper f117181](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f117181/f117181.pdf)
- FINRA Office of the Chief Economist working paper, 330 million institutional orders: average size of the top-level order aggregated at broker-stock-day is 1,371 shares, larger in large caps — [FINRA OCE working paper Jan 2019](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/OCE_WP_jan2019.pdf)
- Broker-permitted participation ranges: IBKR POV 1-50% (0.01%-50% on variants); IBKR VWAP max up to 50% of ADV; Jefferies would-mode presets Volume_10%, Volume_20%, Volume_30% — [IBKR Percent of Volume](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/trading/orders/percent-of-volume.php); [IBKR VWAP](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/index.php?f=1124); [IBKR TWS API, Jefferies](https://interactivebrokers.github.io/tws-api/jefferies.html)
- At end time with residual: IBKR VWAP stops unless "allow past end time" is set, in which case it "will continue to work past the end time to execute any unfilled portion" — [IBKR TWS guide, VWAP](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/software/tws.bak/usersguidebook/algos/vwap.htm)
- IBKR Close Price algo: "Minimizes market impact by intelligently working orders into the close." (i.e. part of a close-targeted order trades in the continuous market before 16:00 rather than all in the auction) — [IBKR algo descriptions](https://interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/software/tws.bak/ibalgostop.htm)

### Inferences
- The "5-20% of volume" range commonly quoted on desks is the range of *settings traders choose for meaningful orders*; the measured average across all algo parents is lower (about 2-5%) because the population is dominated by small orders. Both statements are consistent.
- A parent order at 5% participation needs volume equal to 20x its size to complete: an order of 5% of ADV at 5% POV takes the whole day; 1% of ADV at 10% POV completes in about 10% of the day's volume (arithmetic, not a sourced statistic).
- When a program completes, its share of interval volume simply disappears: the mechanical tell is a drop in volume and loss of the persistent bid (or offer) with no price trigger. When it hits its limit price it stops lifting but its passive child orders may remain resting at or below the limit, which is why program limits often act as intraday support/resistance until the order is amended or cancelled (reasoned inference, not a sourced fact).
- **UNVERIFIED RECALL:** common desk conventions are that orders under ~1-2% of ADV are low-touch/DMA-or-simple-algo, 5-10% of ADV is a standard full-day VWAP/POV candidate, and above ~20-30% of ADV the order is usually worked over multiple days, crossed as a block or done as a risk trade. Common instructions are full day (9:30-16:00, with or without the opening and closing auctions), "10:00 to close" (skipping the volatile open), and "into the close" (last 30-60 minutes plus MOC).

### Gaps
- No sourced distribution of order duration by algo type, or of the share of parents that run the full day versus a sub-interval.
- No sourced convention for ADV-size thresholds; the numbers in the recall bullet are practitioner lore.
- NYSE MOC/LOC cutoff (15:50 ET) and Nasdaq MOC cutoff (15:55 ET) were not confirmed from a primary source in this session (recall only).

## 4. What does the buy side use VWAP as: benchmark, guaranteed/principal VWAP, and PM behaviour around VWAP

### Takeaway
VWAP is both an execution benchmark (full-day or interval) and a contract price: in a guaranteed (principal) VWAP trade the broker commits to fill the client at the day's VWAP and bears the slippage risk itself, charging a commission/premium for that risk; in agency VWAP nothing is guaranteed. Roughly a third to more than half of buy-side desks name VWAP as the TCA benchmark for their algos.

### Cited Findings
- Madhavan (2002), "VWAP Strategies": VWAP is a benchmark that can span a full day or part of one; its comparison table lists guaranteed principal VWAP as low commission and guaranteed execution but with significant exposure to adverse price moves, whereas agency VWAP is not guaranteed — [Madhavan, Transaction Performance, Spring 2002](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)
- Guaranteed VWAP: a broker can promise execution at the day's VWAP and then work the order for its own account, earning commission and carrying the execution risk — [Wikipedia, VWAP](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/VWAP)
- Academic pricing of guaranteed VWAP: the premium the broker needs depends on order size, liquidity and the broker's risk aversion (Guéant & Royer) — [VWAP execution and guaranteed VWAP](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1306.2832)
- Baldauf, Frei & Mollner (Management Science 2022): give conditions under which guaranteed-VWAP contracts are optimal in a principal-agent setting; market-on-close contracts generally cannot be optimal (because the dealer can move the closing price he is paid on) — [Kellogg summary](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2022/principal-trading-arrangements-when-are-common-contracts-optimal)
- TCA benchmark usage: hedge funds (2025) VWAP TCA 57% vs implementation shortfall TCA 53%; long-only (2024) VWAP TCA 34% vs IS TCA 32% — [The TRADE HF 2025](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Algo-Survey-HF-2025.pdf); [The TRADE LO 2024](https://www.thetradenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Algo-survey.pdf)
- IBKR's VWAP is benchmarked to VWAP "from the time you submit the order to the close" — confirmation that interval VWAP, anchored at order arrival, is a standard variant — [IBKR VWAP](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/index.php?f=1124)

### Inferences
- A broker who has sold a guaranteed VWAP has every incentive to execute exactly on the volume curve (any deviation is his own P&L risk), so guaranteed-VWAP flow is the most rigidly curve-shaped flow on the tape (inference from the contract structure).
- A buyer measured against VWAP is "beating the benchmark" on any share bought below the running VWAP and "losing" on any share bought above it. That creates a mechanical preference to be more passive/patient above VWAP and more willing to buy at or below it (and the mirror image for sellers), which is the usual explanation for VWAP acting as an intraday magnet/support in a program-driven stock. This is a reasoned inference and common desk lore, **not** a sourced empirical finding.
- **UNVERIFIED RECALL:** Berkowitz, Logue & Noser (1988), "The Total Cost of Transactions on the NYSE" (Journal of Finance), is the paper that introduced VWAP as a benchmark, arguing that a trade better than the day's volume-weighted average has less-than-average market impact. Known criticisms (Harris, Madhavan): VWAP can be gamed (a trader can spread any order out and match VWAP while incurring large opportunity cost), is distorted when the order is itself a large share of the day's volume (the order becomes the benchmark), and ignores the decision price.

### Gaps
- Berkowitz/Logue/Noser could not be confirmed from a search extract.
- No empirical source was found on PM/trader behaviour conditional on price being above or below VWAP.
- Madhavan's 2002 estimate of VWAP's share of institutional trading was not in the extract retrieved.

## 5. Corporate buybacks: Rule 10b-18, 10b5-1 plans, ASRs, blackout windows

### Takeaway
Rule 10b-18 is a voluntary, day-by-day safe harbor with four conditions (one broker, timing, price, volume): no opening print, no purchases in the last 10 minutes for liquid names, no bidding above the market, and at most 25% of four-week ADTV per day; those constraints force buyback execution into a passive, capped, middle-of-the-day participation pattern, which is why brokers ship dedicated "Buyback" algos. ASRs are priced at the average daily VWAP over the term minus a negotiated discount, so the bank's hedge is itself a multi-week VWAP program.

### Cited Findings
- "To come within the safe harbor, an issuer's repurchases must satisfy (on a daily basis) each of Rule 10b-18's four conditions"; failure to meet any one removes all of that day's repurchases from the safe harbor — [SEC Division of Trading and Markets 10b-18 FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/marketreg/r10b18faq0504.htm)
- The four conditions are manner, timing, price and volume; no presumption of manipulation arises merely because purchases fall outside the conditions (paragraph (d)) — [SEC 10b-18 FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/marketreg/r10b18faq0504.htm)
- Manner and timing as summarized by counsel: purchases "must be executed with a single broker or dealer on any single day"; a purchase "is not the opening trade of the day nor made in the last 10 minutes of the trading day" — [Goodwin, Launching a Share Repurchase Program](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/resource/off-the-shelf/pcap-launching-share)
- The 10-minute-before-close cut-off applies to securities with ADTV value of at least $1 million and public float of at least $150 million (wording in the search extract was garbled; the thresholds are as stated) — [FindLaw, SEC Adopts Issuer Stock Repurchase Amendments](https://corporate.findlaw.com/finance/sec-adopts-issuer-stock-repurchase-amendments.html)
- Volume: daily purchases may not exceed 25% of ADTV, measured over the four calendar weeks preceding the week of the purchase; since the 2003 amendments block trades are no longer excluded from the volume limit, with a once-a-week block alternative ("once each week, in lieu of purchasing under the 25 percent of ADTV limit for that day...") — [eCFR 17 CFR 240.10b-18](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-240/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFRbda83517ce4377f/section-240.10b-18); [FindLaw summary](https://corporate.findlaw.com/finance/sec-adopts-issuer-stock-repurchase-amendments.html)
- Price: bids/purchases may not exceed the highest independent bid or the last independent transaction price, whichever is higher (confirmed only in summary form by the search tool, not from quoted rule text) — [eCFR 17 CFR 240.10b-18](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-240/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFRbda83517ce4377f/section-240.10b-18)
- The 2003 adopting release states the safe harbor does not cover opening bids and purchases, nor bids and purchases near or at the close — [SEC Release 33-8335](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/33-8335.pdf)
- A stricter volume cap ("the lesser of 25% of the security's four-week ADTV or the issuer's average daily Rule 10b-18 purchases during the three full calendar months") appears in the rule; see note in Inferences on where it applies — [Cornell LII 17 CFR 240.10b-18](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.10b-18)
- Broker implementation: Jefferies lists a dedicated "Buyback" algo, and its other algos carry an "IsBuyBack" flag: "Indicates that the algo should engage SEC Rule 10b-18 restrictions for Buy Back in US instruments" (cannot be changed once set) — [IBKR TWS API, Jefferies algos](https://interactivebrokers.github.io/tws-api/jefferies.html)
- 10b5-1 and blackouts: repurchases can run daily during an open window under a 10b-18 engagement, or "over a period of time (which may be during a blackout period) if documented as a written trading agreement under a Rule 10b5-1 plan" — [Goodwin, Launching a Share Repurchase Program](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/resource/off-the-shelf/pcap-launching-share)
- The SEC's December 2022 10b5-1 amendments imposed cooling-off periods on directors/officers (later of 90 days or two business days after the 10-Q/10-K, capped at 120 days) and 30 days for other persons, but **not** on issuers: the SEC did not mandate a cooling-off period for issuer buyback plans — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/sec-adopts-major-changes-insider-transactions); [Kirkland](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2022/12/public-company-updates-from-the-sec)
- Blackout practice (treasury peer survey, anecdotal): one company's blackout "starts 15 days prior to quarter-end and ends after earnings release" and it runs its buyback through a 10b5-1 plan during that period; another executes repurchases "systematically throughout the year, entirely through 10b5-1 plans" — [NeuGroup, Talking Shop](https://connect.neugroup.com/public/blogs/talking-shop-how-long-is-your-10b5-1-buyback-cooling-off-period)
- Many companies apply the same blackout period to corporate repurchases that they apply to insiders (older source, 1999) — [Journal of Accountancy](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/1999/may/mccarthy.html)
- Academic finding: buyback timing is driven by the corporate calendar because many firms treat the period before the earnings announcement as a blackout — [AEA 2023 conference paper](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2023/program/paper/TayB6iDB)
- ASR pricing: "The aggregate number of shares repurchased in an ASR is determined by reference to the volume-weighted average price (VWAP) per share over the duration of the ASR (which is typically three- to six-months), less a negotiated discount to VWAP." Dealers offer the discount in exchange for, among other things, the right to accelerate the maturity of the ASR — [Wilson Sonsini](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/accelerated-share-repurchase-asr-programs-to-be-impacted-by-proposed-changes-to-rule-10b5-1-and-share-repurchase-disclosure-rules.html)
- ASR as an execution problem: the bank buys the shares in the market over a variable window that cannot extend past maturity; the problem combines optimal execution with exotic (Bermudan/Asian-style) option pricing — [Guéant, Pu, Royer (arXiv 1312.5617)](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1312.5617)

### Inferences
- Combined effect on the tape: a 10b-18 buyback cannot be the opening print, cannot buy in the last 10 minutes (30 for less liquid names), cannot lift offers above the last independent print/bid (so it is effectively a passive or at-the-last buyer that never initiates an uptick), and cannot exceed 25% of four-week ADTV. In practice that is a capped-participation, passive VWAP/POV program running from just after the open to 15:50 ET that stops at its daily share cap, and it is absent from the closing auction (inference from the four conditions; the closing auction falls inside the excluded last-10-minutes window).
- The "lesser of 25% of ADTV or the three-month average daily purchases" wording belongs, to my recollection, to the merger-period carve-out in the rule's definition of "Rule 10b-18 purchase" (ordinary-course purchases allowed during a merger/acquisition period), not to the general volume condition. **UNVERIFIED** in this session.
- Because an issuer-plan cooling-off was not adopted, a company can keep buying through its insider blackout via a pre-set 10b5-1 instruction grid (e.g. buy more at lower prices). Buyback flow therefore does not vanish in blackout windows; discretionary open-market buying does, 10b5-1 grid buying continues.
- An ASR dealer is short VWAP-minus-discount over the averaging window: it borrows and delivers shares up front, then covers in the market. Buying on the daily volume curve every day hedges the averaging exposure, so ASR covering looks like a steady daily VWAP buy program, typically also run within 10b-18-style limits.
- **UNVERIFIED RECALL (rule text I could not open this session):** the rule's timing condition excludes "the opening (regular way) purchase reported in the consolidated system", purchases within the last 10 minutes before the scheduled close of the primary trading session for securities with ADTV value >= $1 million and public float >= $150 million, and within the last 30 minutes for all other securities; the once-a-week block may be done only if no other 10b-18 purchases are made that day and the block is excluded from the ADTV calculation; after a market-wide trading suspension the volume limit is raised to 100% of ADTV and timing conditions are relaxed. Also from recall: the SEC's 2023 share-repurchase disclosure modernization rule (daily repurchase data) was vacated by the Fifth Circuit in December 2023, and a 1% federal excise tax on net buybacks has applied since 2023.

## 6. Child-order mechanics: slicing, venues, posting vs taking, and lit vs off-exchange prints

### Takeaway
Algo child orders are overwhelmingly small limit orders (81.5% limit, <0.4% market in a 300-million-child-order sample), routed across lit books, hidden/midpoint order types and dark pools. Off-exchange (TRF-reported) volume was 50.6% of consolidated volume in 2025 and about 50% in Q3 2026, but it drops sharply in the final minutes, and the closing auction itself is about 8-9% of volume on a normal day and 20-34% on rebalance/expiry days.

### Cited Findings
- Child-order type mix: fewer than 0.40% of child orders are market orders; 81.5% are limit orders (2012-2016 institutional algo sample) — [NBER conference paper](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f145256/f145256.pdf)
- Scale of slicing: 2.3 million parent orders generated over 300 million child orders (about 130 child orders per parent on average, computed from those two figures) — [NBER conference paper](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f145256/f145256.pdf)
- Dark/liquidity-seeking algo description (IBKR-listed third-party algo): "Liquidity seeking dark strategy with the ability to dynamically slide between targeted levels with a single numeric input in an effort to minimize market impact. Ability to access major dark pools and hidden liquidity at lit venues. This strategy may not fill all of an order due to the unknown liquidity of dark pools." (attribution approximate) — [IBKR third-party algos](https://www.interactivebrokers.ie/es/index.php?f=4985)
- Jefferies liquidity-seeking modes are tiered by aggression: Seek_Passive, Seek_Active, Seek_Aggressive, DARKSeek, BLITZ, Patience; a separate "Post" algo exists for passive posting — [IBKR TWS API, Jefferies algos](https://interactivebrokers.github.io/tws-api/jefferies.html)
- Off-exchange share: TRF off-exchange share reached 50.6% of total consolidated volume in 2025, up 361 bps year on year — [Cboe 2025 U.S. Equities Year in Review](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/2025-u-s-equities-year-in-review)
- September 2026: TRF 50.6% of ADV, averaging 50.4% over Q3 2026 — [Cboe September volume briefing](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/market-metrics-that-matter-u-s-cash-equities-september-volume-briefing)
- Different definition: Rosenblatt reports 56.02% of volume executed "off-board" in August 2026 (broader measure, not comparable with Cboe's TRF figure) — [Rosenblatt US volumes August 2026](https://www.rblt.com/market-structure-reports/us-securities-volumes-august-2026)
- In 2025 off-exchange volume exceeded exchange volume in all but three months; Liquidnet says this reversed in 2026 with activity moving back toward lit venues (conflicts with Cboe's ~50.4-50.6% for Q3 2026; likely month-selection or definitional differences) — [Liquidnet Liquidity Landscape Q2 2026](https://www.liquidnet.com/expert-insights/iliquidity-landscape-us-edition-q2-2026-market-structure-outlook)
- 2025 was the year off-exchange share rose above 50%; "less than a third of all volume intraday comes from lit venues at the National Best Bid and Offer (NBBO)" — [Nasdaq, "It's Darkest in the Middle of the Day"](https://nasdaq.com/articles/its-darkest-middle-day)
- "Off-exchange trading, which now accounts for around 50% of overall market volume, falls off considerably in the final minutes" of the day; IEX handles 10% of exchange volume in the final 10 minutes in single stocks priced $1 and above — [IEX, Closing Time](https://www.iex.io/article/closing-time-you-dont-have-to-go-home-but-you-might-want-to-trade-here-end-of-day-trading)
- Closing auction size: about $50 billion notional per day in 2024, "roughly 9% of total daily trading volume", rising to about 20% on index-rebalance or options-expiration days; over 34% of notional on 2024 Russell reconstitution day — [BMLL, Into the Close](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/into-the-close-unpacking-u-s-closing-auction-dynamics-and-the-impact-of-the-russell-reconstitution)
- Closing share 8.04% of total equity volume in August 2026; 17.44% of closing volume occurred off-exchange (i.e. broker/ATS guaranteed-close facilities printing at the official close) — [Rosenblatt US volumes August 2026](https://www.rblt.com/market-structure-reports/us-securities-volumes-august-2026)
- Closing auction was 6.6% of total US volume in March 2026 — [Liquidnet Liquidity Landscape Q2 2026](https://www.liquidnet.com/expert-insights/iliquidity-landscape-us-edition-q2-2026-market-structure-outlook)
- Conflicting higher figure: SSGA, citing Instinet data for December 2025, puts the US closing auction at 15.39% of daily volume (Developed Europe 34.82%) — likely a different denominator (e.g. lit/exchange volume or a specific index universe) and a quad-witching/rebalance month — [SSGA, How Passive Investing Is Reshaping Microstructure](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/institutional/insights/how-passive-investing-reshaping-microstructure)
- Long-run trend: closing auction volume grew from about 4% of ADV at the start of the sample to a peak of about 10% of ADV at the end (JFQA paper) — [JFQA, Price Impact in Closing Auctions](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/price-impact-in-closing-auctions-opening-auctions-and-continuous-markets-a-benchmark-for-cost-of-trading-on-anomalies/0F72910A79C5B42CF6E85F55164CE846)
- Closing auction volume in September 2026 was up 20% year over year, with outsized gains at NYSE — [Cboe September volume briefing](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/market-metrics-that-matter-u-s-cash-equities-september-volume-briefing)

### Inferences
- An institutional program is therefore only partly visible on the lit tape: dark-pool, midpoint and broker-internalized fills print to the TRF (exchange code "D" on most platforms) with no displayed quote beforehand, while the passive posted portion shows up as a persistent refreshing bid/offer on lit books. The Nasdaq title and the IEX note together imply the dark share is highest midday and lowest into the close, so the lit tape under-represents programs most at midday and is most informative in the last 10-15 minutes.
- The ~50% off-exchange figure is not mostly institutional algo flow: it combines retail wholesaler internalization, ATS/dark pools and broker single-dealer platforms. No split was retrieved in this session, so the share of *algo* volume that prints off-exchange cannot be stated from these sources.
- **UNVERIFIED RECALL:** child orders are typically sized near the displayed quote size (often 100 shares or less; odd lots are now a majority of trades in many high-priced stocks, per SEC/exchange odd-lot statistics), with randomized size and inter-arrival time as anti-gaming; many algos use minimum-quantity conditions in dark pools to avoid being pinged; ATS/dark pools account for roughly 10-13% of consolidated volume with wholesalers/single-dealer platforms making up most of the rest of off-exchange volume; smart order routers check dark/midpoint venues first and take displayed liquidity last.
