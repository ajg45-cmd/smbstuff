## 1. The intraday volume curve and the growth of the closing auction

### Takeaway
The close is now the single largest liquidity event of the day: the closing auction alone is roughly 10% of consolidated US volume (9.74% in Dec 2025 on one measure, 15.39% on another), up from about 3% in 2010 and 7.5% in 2018, and the growth is tied to index/ETF ownership and close-benchmarked rebalancing. I could not find a single authoritative 2023-2026 table giving first-30-min / midday / last-30-min shares for S&P 500 vs Russell 2000 names; the numbers in circulation for those buckets are blog-level and conflict.

### Cited Findings
- **[STRONG]** Closing auctions were 7.5% of daily US dollar volume in 2018, up from 3.1% in 2010; the fraction of daily volume executed 9:30 am-3:30 pm fell over the same period (volume migrated toward the close). — [Bogousslavsky & Muravyev, AEA draft](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7); published as [J. Financial Markets 66 (2023)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v66y2023ics1386418123000502.html)
- **[STRONG]** In the same study the last five minutes of continuous trading (3:55-4:00 pm) account for 6.96% of total daily volume. — [Bogousslavsky & Muravyev, June 2021 draft](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- **[STRONG]** Closing volume is strongly associated with ETF ownership and institutional rebalancing; auction volume permanently rises relative to intraday volume after a stock is added to the S&P 500 (and falls after deletion). — [Bogousslavsky & Muravyev, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418123000502)
- **[MODERATE]** Closing auction share reached 9.74% of total US equity volume in December 2025; 22.81% of "closing volume" printed off-exchange (broker guaranteed-close/internalized MOC). — [Rosenblatt (RBLT), US Securities Volumes 2025 Year-in-Review](https://www.rblt.com/market-structure-reports/us-securities-volumes-2025-year-in-review)
- **[MODERATE]** Instinet data as of 31 Dec 2025 (via State Street) put the US closing auction at 15.39% of daily volume (Developed Europe 34.82%, Asia ex-Japan 16.78%), and the "last 2 hours" excluding the auction at about 33.85% of US volume. Taken together that implies roughly half of US volume trades after 2:00 pm on that dataset. — [SSGA, "Closing time: How passive investing is reshaping equity market microstructure"](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/institutional/insights/how-passive-investing-reshaping-microstructure)
- **Conflict flag:** the 9.74% (Rosenblatt) and 15.39% (Instinet/SSGA) figures are for the same month and differ because of denominator/universe definitions (e.g. total consolidated volume including off-exchange vs. a narrower universe). A 2025 Interactive Brokers podcast says "about 5%" on a typical day, which is lower than both. Use ~10% of consolidated volume as the working figure and treat 15% as an index-universe figure. — [Rosenblatt](https://www.rblt.com/market-structure-reports/us-securities-volumes-2025-year-in-review); [SSGA](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/institutional/insights/how-passive-investing-reshaping-microstructure); [IBKR podcast](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/podcasts/ibkr-podcasts/is-your-stock-about-to-russell-up/)
- **[MODERATE]** Nasdaq Economic Research (Mackintosh, June 2021): an average of 21.2% of total volume traded in the final hour, peaking above 30% in March 2021. — [Nasdaq, "M-ELO: The Final Hour of Trading"](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/m-elo%3A-the-final-hour-of-trading-2021-06-22)
- **[MODERATE, dated]** On Nasdaq's own book, the close was almost 16% of Nasdaq's volume and the open about 4%; about a fifth of the exchange's volume is in the two auctions. (Exchange-level share, not share of consolidated volume.) — [Nasdaq, "What should we do when an ETF doesn't close"](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/what-should-we-do-when-an-etf-doesnt-close-2019-10-10)
- **[MODERATE]** Cboe reported the closing auction at about 4.5% of total market volume in one October (older article; shows how low the figure was before the recent run-up on a total-volume denominator). — [Cboe Insights](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/the-impact-closing-auctions-have-on-volumes/)
- **[MODERATE]** NYSE closing-auction volume has set records each quarter; NYSE traded a record 605.5 million shares per day at the close in Q1 2026. — [NYSE, "Behind the Record Volumes"](https://www.nyse.com/research/insights/behind-the-record-volumes-a-hidden-opportunity)
- **[MODERATE]** Off-exchange (TRF) trading was 50.6% of consolidated volume in 2025, the first full year above half. — [Cboe, 2025 U.S. Equities Year in Review](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/2025-u-s-equities-year-in-review)
- **[MODERATE]** Why the close grew: index funds and ETFs strike NAV at the closing price, so trading anywhere other than the auction creates tracking error. — [SSGA](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/institutional/insights/how-passive-investing-reshaping-microstructure)
- **[MODERATE]** On index-event days the auction share jumps: about 15% of volume in the close on MSCI rebalances, about 20% on S&P and Nasdaq-100 rebalances, over 25% on Russell reconstitution day (one practitioner estimate). — [IBKR podcast](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/podcasts/ibkr-podcasts/is-your-stock-about-to-russell-up/)
- **[WEAK, conflicting]** Blog-level figures for the first 30 minutes range from 11-13% of daily volume to 20-25% to 25-30%, and 15-20% for the last 30 minutes. None gives methodology; they are not reliable. — [vantatrading.io](https://www.vantatrading.io/blog/trading-the-us-stock-market); [arithmion.com](https://arithmion.com/learn/en/depth-execution/intraday-volume-profiles/); [journalplus.co](https://journalplus.co/blog/time-of-day-trading-analysis/)

### Inferences
- The shape is better described as a "J" or reverse-L than a U: the Instinet split (about 34% in the last two continuous hours plus about 15% in the auction) leaves roughly half the day's volume for 9:30 am-2:00 pm, of which the first 30-60 minutes takes a disproportionate slice.
- Because about 23% of closing volume is matched off-exchange at the official close, the exchange auction print understates true MOC interest, and the published imbalance is only the residual that reached the primary exchange.
- A VWAP algo following a historical curve must back-load: a "10:00 to close" VWAP order that participates in the auction will do a double-digit percentage of its shares in the final print, which is why afternoon participation rates ramp even without new information.

### Gaps
- No verified 2023-2026 breakdown of first-30-min / midday / last-30-min volume shares, and nothing split S&P 500 vs Russell 2000. Best sources (Nasdaq Economic Research intraday exhibits, Goldman/JPM market-structure notes, Bloomberg Intelligence, BestEx Research) were either not surfaced by search or sit behind paywalls.
- The 2024 Bloomberg piece "Stocks trade for 390 minutes a day. Increasingly only 10 matter" (BestEx Research data, reportedly about a third of S&P 500 volume in the last 10 minutes including the auction) could not be opened; the "one third" figure is unverified here.
- No small-cap-specific closing share found. Background expectation (unverified): closing auction share is higher in index-heavy small caps and on rebalance days.

## 2. Program start/stop times and intraday momentum evidence

### Takeaway
The academic evidence for an intraday clock is strongest for two things: (a) return patterns recur at the same half-hour on subsequent days for up to 40 days, consistent with institutions repeating the same schedule day after day, and (b) the market's return from prior close through 10:00 am predicts the 3:30-4:00 pm return, with the best-supported mechanism being gamma and leveraged-ETF hedging late in the day. Specific desk conventions ("algos start 9:35-10:00", "VWAP 10:00 to close", "Europe leaves at 11:30", "2:00-3:00 pickup") are practitioner lore that I could not source to a rigorous study.

### Cited Findings
- **[STRONG]** Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka (J. Finance 2010): with the day split into 13 half-hour intervals, a stock's return in a given interval positively predicts its return in the same interval on subsequent days (lags 13, 26, 39...), lasting about 40 trading days. Volume and order imbalance show the same periodicity but do not fully explain it; the effect is strongest in, but not limited to, the first and last half hours, and is not driven by size or S&P 500 membership. The authors suggest traders time rebalancing around these patterns. — [Kellogg summary](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2010/intraday-patterns-in-the-cross-section-of-stock-returns/); [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.3535.pdf)
- **[STRONG]** Same paper: outside the same-time-of-day lags, short-horizon returns reverse, driven by temporary liquidity imbalances lasting less than an hour and bid-ask bounce. — [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.3535.pdf)
- **[STRONG]** Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, "Market intraday momentum" (JFE 129, 2018): in SPY data 1993-2013, the first half-hour return, measured from the previous day's close (so it includes the overnight gap), predicts the last half-hour return. Predictive R-squared about 1.6% in-sample and about 1.2% out-of-sample (working-paper figures). — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/)
- **[STRONG]** Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens, "Hedging demand and market intraday momentum" (JFE 142, 2021): intraday momentum (rest-of-day return predicting the last 30 minutes) appears across more than 60 futures from 1974 to 2020 and is linked to gamma hedging by option market makers and leveraged-ETF rebalancing; dealers who are net short gamma must trade in the direction of the day's move. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:377-403); [author PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/hot-topic-does-gamma-hedging-actually-affect-stock-prices/)
- **[MODERATE]** A follow-on paper states that this hedging starts around 3:30 pm each day (secondary source, not the original paper's own stated window). — [EFMA 2021 paper](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2021-Leeds/papers/EFMA%202021_stage-2049_question-Full%20Paper_id-237.pdf)
- **[MODERATE]** Barbon, Beckmeyer, Buraschi & Moerke (SFI WP 22-40): leveraged-ETF rebalancing and option delta-hedging are two distinct end-of-day liquidity demands producing end-of-day momentum and mean reversion in single-stock returns. Leveraged-ETF flows are mechanical and predictable, attract more liquidity provision, have shorter-lived price impact, and their effect has declined significantly over time; gamma effects persist through the sample. Earlier draft: the leveraged-ETF channel was economically larger, and the result is non-informational. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3925725); [SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/n-22-40-liquidity-provision-to-leveraged-etfs-and-equity-options-rebalancing-flows-evidence-from-end-of-day-stock-prices)
- **[STRONG]** Bogousslavsky, "The cross-section of intraday and overnight returns" (JFE 141, 2021): overnight margin requirements are higher and stock-lending fees are charged only on positions held overnight, so arbitrageurs cut positions before the close. A mispricing factor earns positive returns through the day but performs poorly at the end of the day, and the pattern strengthens in the second half of the sample. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:141:y:2021:i:1:p:172-194)
- **[MODERATE]** Earlier versions of that paper report that small and illiquid stocks earn high average returns in the last 30 minutes, and that "gradual" anomalies have large negative returns in the last half hour and overnight. (Thesis/working-paper wording; not confirmed in the published text.) — [EPFL thesis](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/229415/files/EPFL_TH7785.pdf?version=1); [Bocconi-hosted WP](https://finance.unibocconi.eu/sites/default/files/files/media/attachments/The%2520Cross-Section%2520of%2520Intraday%2520and%2520Overnight%2520Returns_21Dec1620170112151545.pdf)
- **[MODERATE]** Nasdaq final-hour data (21.2% of volume on average in the last hour, 2021) confirms the 3:00-4:00 pm acceleration in aggregate. — [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/m-elo%3A-the-final-hour-of-trading-2021-06-22)

### Inferences
- Heston-Korajczyk-Sadka is the cleanest academic footprint of multi-day scheduled programs: if a name is bid in the 10:00-10:30 or 3:30-4:00 bucket today, the conditional expectation for the same bucket tomorrow is slightly positive. The effect is statistically robust but small per interval; it is a tilt, not a setup by itself.
- The Gao et al. signal is small (R-squared about 1.6%) and is partly a gap signal, because the "first half hour" includes the overnight move. It is strongest on high-volatility, high-volume, and macro-news days per the paper's summaries; treat it as a trend-day filter (large open move + short-gamma regime => last-30-min continuation more likely), not a standalone edge.
- Two opposing end-of-day forces coexist: hedging flows push with the day's move (momentum) from about 3:30 pm, while arbitrageurs' de-risking (Bogousslavsky) and auction liquidity demand produce price pressure that reverts overnight. Direction into the close is therefore more reliable at the index level than in an individual name.
- Conventional desk clock (not sourced, see Gaps): opening auction 9:30; many schedule-based algos held out of the first 5-30 minutes because of wide spreads and unstable volume curves; midday trough roughly 12:00-2:00; ramp from 2:00-3:00; MOC decisions crystallise 3:45-3:50.

### Gaps
- No rigorous public source found for "algos commonly start 9:35-10:00" or "VWAP 10:00-to-close" as standard instructions; these are broker-desk conventions. Treat as **[WEAK/FOLKLORE]** until a broker TCA study is located.
- No quantified US-equity evidence found for a European-close (11:30 am ET) effect on US single-stock volume or flow. **[FOLKLORE]** as far as this research goes.
- No source quantifying the "lunch lull" or "2:00-3:00 pickup" as a percent of volume by half hour for 2023-2026.
- Could not confirm from the Baltussen et al. PDF the exact definition of the predicted window or the economic magnitude; the original should be read directly ([PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)).

## 3. NYSE and Nasdaq closing auction mechanics (2025-2026) and price behaviour around imbalances

### Takeaway
Two clocks matter: NYSE is a 3:50 pm market (MOC/LOC cutoff and regulatory imbalance at 3:50, D Orders live until 3:59:50), Nasdaq is a 3:55 pm market (MOC cutoff and NOII start at 3:55, early indicator from 3:50, LOC until 3:58). Auction price dislocations are small on average (about 8 bps) and the peer-reviewed evidence is that they revert about half shortly after the close and fully overnight, which argues against carrying a position overnight purely because of an imbalance-driven closing print.

### Cited Findings

**NYSE (listed names)**
- **[STRONG]** 3:50 pm: cutoff for MOC/LOC order entry and systemic publication of the MOC/LOC Regulatory Imbalance. After 3:50 only offsetting MOC/LOC (opposite side to a published regulatory imbalance) and Closing Offset (CO) orders are accepted, up to 4:00 pm. — [NYSE Closing Process Fact Sheet](https://www.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/NYSE_Auctions_Closing_Process_Fact_Sheet.pdf); [NYSE, "Offsetting a Regulatory Closing Imbalance"](https://nyse.com/data-insights/offsetting-a-regulatory-closing-imbalance)
- **[STRONG]** 3:50-3:58 pm: MOC/LOC/CO orders may be cancelled only for documented legitimate error. After 3:58 pm no cancels (except under Rule 7.35B(j)(2)(B)). — [NYSE Closing Process Fact Sheet](https://www.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/NYSE_Auctions_Closing_Process_Fact_Sheet.pdf)
- **[STRONG]** 3:55 pm: closing imbalance information begins to include Closing D Orders at their discretionary price range (before 3:55 the feed understates floor-broker interest). — [NYSE Closing Auction Insider's Guide](https://nyse.com/article/nyse-closing-auction-insiders-guide)
- **[STRONG]** 3:59:50 pm: D Orders can no longer be entered or modified. 4:00 pm: closing auction process begins. — [NYSE, "Closing Auction: Timing Shifts and Marketability Trends"](https://www.nyse.com/research/insights/nyse-closing-auction-timing-shifts-and-marketability-trends); [NYSE Insider's Guide](https://nyse.com/article/nyse-closing-auction-insiders-guide)
- **[STRONG, history]** The NYSE cutoff was moved to 3:50 pm (from 3:45 pm) under a rule change approved by the SEC in early 2019. — [Federal Register, 6 Feb 2019](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2019/02/06/2019-01175/self-regulatory-organizations-new-york-stock-exchange-llc-order-approving-a-proposed-rule-change-to)
- **[MODERATE, recent change in behaviour]** Between August 2024 and September 2025, the share of NYSE closing-auction orders submitted in the final 10 seconds of trading rose from 4.45% to 13.4%: auction interest is arriving later, via D Orders, so the 3:50 imbalance is a progressively less complete picture. — [NYSE, "Timing Shifts and Marketability Trends"](https://www.nyse.com/research/insights/nyse-closing-auction-timing-shifts-and-marketability-trends)
- **[MODERATE]** NYSE also publishes a "Significant Imbalance" designation and markets it as a signal for offsetting liquidity. — [NYSE data insights](https://www.nyse.com/data-insights/the-nyse-significant-imbalance-enhanced-trading-opportunities-at-the-nyse-closing-auction)

**Nasdaq (listed names)**
- **[STRONG]** 3:50 pm: Early Order Imbalance Indicator (EOII) disseminated every 10 seconds until the NOII begins. — [Federal Register, 24 Feb 2022 SEC order](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/02/24/2022-03876/self-regulatory-organizations-the-nasdaq-stock-market-llc-order-approving-proposed-rule-change-to)
- **[STRONG]** 3:55 pm: NOII (Net Order Imbalance Indicator) dissemination begins; Nasdaq stops accepting entry, cancellation or modification of MOC orders. LOC orders may be entered until 3:58 pm (late LOCs are subject to pricing constraints). — [Nasdaq Opening/Closing Cross quick guide](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/technicalsupport/specifications/TradingProducts/openclosequickguide.pdf)
- **[STRONG, history]** Nasdaq moved the Closing Cross cutoff and NOII start from 3:50 pm to 3:55 pm in October 2018; a 2019 change permitted LOC entry until immediately before 3:55 pm. — [Federal Register, 18 Mar 2019](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2019/03/18/2019-04945/self-regulatory-organizations-the-nasdaq-stock-market-llc-notice-of-filing-and-immediate)
- **Conflict flag:** older Nasdaq FAQ PDFs still on nasdaqtrader.com show a 3:50 pm cutoff and NOII start; they predate the October 2018 change. — [Nasdaq Closing Cross FAQ (outdated)](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/productsservices/Trading/ClosingCrossfaq.pdf)

**How price responds to imbalances, and whether it reverts**
- **[MODERATE, 2019 data]** Nasdaq Economic Research: closing prices move an average of about 5.5 bps immediately after the information in the NOII announcement; the final ten minutes are "pretty stable" relative to average intraday volatility; liquidity providers who offset imbalances are being paid for risk carried into the close. — [Nasdaq, "How much does the MOC imbalance matter?"](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/how-much-does-the-moc-imbalance-matter-2019-09-27)
- **[STRONG]** Bogousslavsky & Muravyev: the average absolute deviation of the auction price (from the pre-close quote midpoint) is 8.1 bps; for 1% of stock-days it exceeds 0.63%. — [AEA draft](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7)
- **[STRONG]** Those closing deviations revert by half shortly after the close (after-hours) and fully overnight; the reversal is complete for large stocks and "mostly" for small stocks, and survives a half-spread adjustment. Results hold under both NYSE and Nasdaq auction designs. — [Bogousslavsky & Muravyev, J. Financial Markets](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v66y2023ics1386418123000502.html); [Wharton Jacobs Levy copy](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Bogousslavsky.pdf)
- **[STRONG]** By contrast, price moves of similar size in the last five minutes of continuous trading reverse overnight about five times less: late continuous-session moves carry more information than the auction print itself. — [AEA draft](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7)
- **[MODERATE]** Interpretation: the fast reversal indicates imperfect liquidity provision in the auction and largely uninformed (index/ETF/rebalance) auction flow; some of the reversal is compensation to liquidity providers for overnight risk. — [Bloomberg, "Wall Street goes slightly crazy each weekday afternoon"](https://www.bloombergquint.com/markets/noise-for-sale-in-giant-u-s-auctions-used-to-end-stock-sessions); [AEA draft](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7)
- **[MODERATE]** Floor-broker/D-Order role at the NYSE close is examined in an NBER working paper ("Vestigial Tails? Floor Brokers at the Close in Modern Electronic Markets"). — [NBER](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f145281.pdf)

**24-hour trading (flag: changing now)**
- **[STRONG]** The SEC approved Nasdaq's extended-hours proposal for NMS stocks on 10 April 2026, following earlier approvals for 24X and NYSE Arca (22-hour weekday schedule). — [Arnold & Porter via Columbia CLS Blue Sky Blog](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2026/04/29/arnold-porter-discusses-sec-approval-of-nasdaq-proposal-to-expand-trading-hours/)
- **[MODERATE, single source]** Nasdaq's expanded schedule was expected to begin Sunday 6 December 2026, conditional on further rule approvals and SIP readiness; NSCC planned to move to a 24x5 schedule by 28 June 2026. — [Arnold & Porter](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2026/04/29/arnold-porter-discusses-sec-approval-of-nasdaq-proposal-to-expand-trading-hours/)

### Inferences
- Practical clock: 3:50 = first hard information (NYSE regulatory imbalance; Nasdaq EOII). 3:55 = second (Nasdaq NOII, MOC lock; NYSE feed starts showing D Orders). 3:58 = last cancel/LOC windows shut. 3:59:50 = NYSE D-Order lock. With NYSE D-Order interest increasingly arriving in the last 10 seconds, a 3:50 NYSE imbalance can be offset or flipped late.
- A published buy imbalance means more MOC/marketable-LOC buy interest than sell at the reference price; the stock tends to tick toward the imbalance side as liquidity providers demand a concession, then the concession is given back after the close. The average size (5-8 bps) is smaller than a typical spread-plus-slippage round trip for a retail-sized prop account in most names, so the edge is in the tails (the 1% of stock-days > 63 bps) and on index-event days.
- For overnight holds: a strong last five minutes of continuous trading is more informative than a strong auction print. A stock that closes on an auction spike should be expected, on average, to open back near its 3:59 level.
- 24-hour trading does not remove the 4:00 pm close as the NAV/benchmark fix; nothing found suggests cutoff times change with the extended-hours launch, but that was not confirmed.

### Gaps
- Could not read the current NYSE Rule 7.35B or Nasdaq Rule 4754 text directly (nyse.com and sec.gov unreachable). Details I believe from background but did not verify in-session: the frequency of NYSE closing imbalance updates between 3:50 and 4:00; the exact Nasdaq late-LOC price constraint (3:55-3:58); whether Nasdaq NOII updates every second after 3:55; NYSE Arca/Cboe auction specifics.
- A 2025 Nasdaq rule filing (SEC Release 34-102336) surfaced in search but could not be opened; its content (possibly a closing cross change) is unknown.
- No recent (2023-2026) public study of return conditional on imbalance size/sign from 3:50 to 4:00, and none on whether D-Order late arrival has changed the predictive value of the 3:50 imbalance.
- Status of the 6 December 2026 Nasdaq extended-hours launch as of today (23 Nov 2026) was not confirmed.

## 4. Rule 10b-18 timing restrictions and buyback flow

### Takeaway
Safe-harbor buyback flow is structurally absent from the opening print and from the last 10 minutes (liquid names) or last 30 minutes (everything else), so corporate bids live in the body of the day and vanish by 3:50 pm (or 3:30 pm); blackout windows around earnings are company policy, not SEC rule, and the evidence that they move prices is weak.

### Cited Findings
- **[STRONG]** Under Rule 10b-18's timing condition, issuers whose stock has ADTV value of $1 million or more and public float value of $150 million or more may not bid for or purchase during the last 10 minutes before the scheduled close of the primary trading session; all other issuers are barred during the last 30 minutes. — [SEC Release 33-8335 (2003 amendments), via Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2003/11/17/03-28593/purchases-of-certain-equity-securities-by-the-issuer-and-others); rule text at [17 CFR 240.10b-18 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.10b-18)
- **[STRONG]** A safe-harbor purchase may not be the opening transaction; the SEC considered and did not adopt letting liquid issuers participate in the open. — [Skadden, Rule 10b-18 safe harbors](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2020/03/trading-issues-arising-out-of-market-disruptions/rule_10b_18_safe_harbors.pdf); [SEC Release 33-8335](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2003/11/17/03-28593/purchases-of-certain-equity-securities-by-the-issuer-and-others)
- **[STRONG]** ADTV for the rule is the average daily trading volume in the four calendar weeks preceding the week of the purchase. Failing any one of the four conditions (manner, time, price, volume) removes all of that day's repurchases from the safe harbor; the safe harbor is unavailable for manipulative conduct even with technical compliance. — [17 CFR 240.10b-18, govinfo 2024 edition](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title17-vol4/pdf/CFR-2024-title17-vol4-sec240-10b-18.pdf)
- **[MODERATE]** Blackouts typically start a few weeks before the earnings report and end a few days after; academic work on trading windows finds the window usually reopens about two to three trading days after the release, and many firms treat fiscal quarter-end to announcement as the blackout. — [Guay, Kim & Tsui, "Determinants of insider trading windows"](https://accounting.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Guay_Kim_Tsui_Determinants-of-Insider-Trading-Windows_4_22_21.pdf); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2019/01/buyback-blackout-periods-do-not-negatively-impact-market-performance/)
- **[MODERATE]** Trades under a 10b5-1 plan adopted in an open window can execute during a blackout; plan trades before and after earnings are comparable in number. — [Sen working paper](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~rsen/RikSen_JobMktPaper-2.pdf)
- **[WEAK-to-MODERATE]** A practitioner study found no significant return difference for heavy-buyback firms during blackout periods; i.e. "buyback blackout = market weakness" is not supported. — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2019/01/buyback-blackout-periods-do-not-negatively-impact-market-performance/)
- **[MODERATE]** Buyback programs and actual repurchases cluster right after earnings announcements because of the corporate calendar. — [ECGI working paper, "The corporate calendar and the timing of share repurchases and equity compensation"](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/thecorporatecalendarandthetimingofsharerepurchasesandequitybasedcompensation.pdf)

### Inferences
- A corporate buyback algo is a patient, passive, price-capped buyer from shortly after the open until 3:50 pm (large/liquid) or 3:30 pm (small/illiquid). If a persistent bid in a name disappears right at 3:50, buyback is a candidate explanation; the closing auction is run without it.
- Because the price condition caps safe-harbor purchases at the highest independent bid or last independent trade (see Gaps for verification status), buyback flow supports dips and does not chase; it dampens intraday downside rather than driving breakouts.
- Buyback flow density is highest in the weeks after earnings and lowest in the roughly 2-6 weeks before, but 10b5-1 plans keep a meaningful share running through blackouts.

Verify against [17 CFR 240.10b-18](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.10b-18) before publishing.

## 5. Calendar effects on program flow: documented vs folklore

### Takeaway
Best documented: index reconstitution closes (enormous, scheduled, auction-concentrated), month-end institutional liquidity/rebalancing pressure with subsequent reversal, and end-of-day hedging flows. Poorly documented or folklore at trader level: precise "first-day-of-month inflow" trades, window dressing as a tradable price effect, and a clean T+1 effect on intraday flow.

### Cited Findings
- **[STRONG]** Russell reconstitution is the largest scheduled close of the year. 2025: Nasdaq Closing Cross executed 2,506,428,416 shares, a then-record $102.455 billion, in 0.871 seconds; LSEG reported $102.5 billion on Nasdaq and $114.7 billion on NYSE at that close. — [Nasdaq release via NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/record-notional-value-shares-traded-nasdaq-closing-cross-during-2025-russell); [LSEG/FTSE Russell](https://www.lseg.com/en/insights/ftse-russell/what-you-need-to-know-about-this-years-russell-recon1)
- **[MODERATE, verify]** June 2026 reconstitution: Nasdaq reported 4,594,880,616 shares, $334.027 billion in the Closing Cross (release dated 26 June 2026). This is more than triple 2025's figure; it is reported by Nasdaq's own release and a trade publication but I did not read the release in full, so confirm before quoting. — [Nasdaq release via Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/36754045/record-trading-volume-on-the-nasdaq-closing-cross-during-the-june-2026-russell-us-indexes-reconstitution); [TradeInformer](https://tradeinformer.com/institutional-trading/nasdaq-closing-cross-handles-record-334-billion-during-russell-rebalance)
- **[MODERATE]** Auction share of the day's volume on index events: about 15% (MSCI), about 20% (S&P quarterly and Nasdaq-100), over 25% (Russell reconstitution). — [IBKR podcast](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/podcasts/ibkr-podcasts/is-your-stock-about-to-russell-up/)
- **[STRONG]** S&P 500 additions permanently shift a stock's volume toward the closing auction. — [Bogousslavsky & Muravyev](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418123000502)
- **[STRONG]** Month-end liquidity effect ("Dash for Cash", Etula, Rinne, Suominen & Vaittinen, RFS 2020): institutions' month-end cash needs depress returns before month-end, followed by a rebound. Returns over T-8 to T-4 are negatively correlated (about -0.54 in one reported specification) with returns over T-3 to T-1. Mechanism under the then-T+3 cycle: pension funds had to sell by the close of T-4 to have cash by T-1. — [RFS](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/1/75/5494694); [working paper](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/21145/1/Dash4Cash.pdf)
- **[MODERATE]** Rebalancing by balanced/target-allocation funds is predictable from equity-vs-bond weight drift: threshold and calendar (month-end) rebalancing signals are each followed by equity returns lower by about 16-17 bps. (The search extract did not show the author list; consistent with Harvey, Mazzoleni & Melone, "The unintended consequences of rebalancing" - verify.) — [AFA paper listing](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=144452)
- **[STRONG]** Option expiration/hedging: the gamma-hedging channel for end-of-day moves is documented across 60+ futures markets, and the option-gamma effect persists through recent samples while the leveraged-ETF effect has decayed. — [Baltussen et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:377-403); [Barbon et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3925725)
- **[STRONG, procedural]** NYSE issues special quarterly-expiration closing procedures memos for triple-witching days (auction handling is formalised for those dates). — [NYSE Quarterly Expiration memo](https://www.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/markets/nyse/rule-interpretations/2022/Q3_-_Quarterly_Expiration_RM_-_Final.pdf)

### Inferences
- Ranking by evidence quality for an intraday trader: (1) index events (Russell June recon, S&P quarterly rebalances on the third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, MSCI semi-annual/quarterly reviews): **well documented**, flow is in the auction, pre-positioned by arbitrageurs, and the print reverts. (2) Month-end/quarter-end rebalancing: **documented in aggregate**, direction depends on the month's relative equity/bond performance (equities up strongly on the month => pension selling into month-end), effect size tens of basis points at the index level. (3) Gamma/opex effects on the last 30 minutes: **documented**, regime-dependent on dealer positioning. (4) Turn-of-month inflows, window dressing, "smart money trades the last hour": **folklore-to-weak** as stated; I found no rigorous recent source in this session.
- T+1 (US moved to T+1 on 28 May 2024, background knowledge) compresses the Dash-for-Cash mechanics: cash-raising sales that had to happen by T-4 under T+3 can now happen as late as T-2 for cash on T-1, so any pre-month-end selling window should have moved closer to month-end. This is an inference; no post-T+1 study was found.

## 6. Overnight vs intraday returns, multi-day institutional orders, and close strength

### Takeaway
It is well established that different investor clienteles dominate the overnight and intraday windows and that many return patterns earn their premium in only one of them (often with the opposite sign in the other); about 30% of institutional parent orders span more than one day (average about 1.4 days), and same-time-of-day continuation for up to 40 days is the best public evidence that multi-day programs leave a repeatable footprint. I found no rigorous public study showing that "closing in the top of the range on high volume" by itself predicts next-day continuation.

### Cited Findings
- **[STRONG]** Lou, Polk & Skouras, "A tug of war" (JFE 134, 2019): across 14 trading strategies, profits accrue entirely overnight or entirely intraday, typically with opposite signs in the two windows; interpreted as a tug of war between overnight and intraday investor clienteles. Firm-level overnight returns continue into overnight returns and intraday into intraday, with cross-period reversal. — [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/87481); [author PDF](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:134:y:2019:i:1:p:192-213)
- **[STRONG]** Hendershott, Livdan & Rösch, "Asset pricing: A tale of night and day" (JFE 138, 2020; sample 1990-2014): stock returns are positively related to beta overnight and negatively related to beta during the trading day, in the US and internationally, for both beta-sorted portfolios and individual stocks. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:138:y:2020:i:3:p:635-662); [Univ. at Buffalo](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/asset-pricing-a-tale-of-night-and-day/)
- **[MODERATE, working paper]** Lou, Polk & Skouras follow-up ("The day destroys the night, night extends the day", 2026 WP): overnight and intraday market returns respond in opposite directions to past market returns; past smoothed intraday market returns strongly positively forecast subsequent overnight returns; splitting returns this way improves equity-premium forecasts. — [FMG DP961](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2026-07/DP961.pdf)
- **[STRONG]** Bogousslavsky (JFE 2021): overnight margin and lending-fee frictions make arbitrageurs reduce positions into the close, so mispricing worsens at the end of the day and overnight; a mispricing factor does well intraday and poorly at day-end. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:141:y:2021:i:1:p:172-194)
- **[STRONG]** Closing-auction price deviations fully revert overnight (large stocks) — a direct, measured negative relation between the auction's price pressure and the overnight return. — [Bogousslavsky & Muravyev](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v66y2023ics1386418123000502.html)
- **[MODERATE]** Institutional parent orders reconstructed from ANcerno/Abel Noser tickets: multi-day orders are about 30% of parent orders; the average parent order lasts about 1.38 trading days and is split across about 1.74 brokers. (Papers stitch tickets following Anand, Irvine, Puckett & Venkataraman 2012.) — [AEA 2021 working paper, "Splitting and Shuffling"](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/ibSfSdEb); [FMA conference paper](https://www.fmaconferences.org/Taipei/Papers/202.pdf)
- **[MODERATE]** ANcerno covers roughly 750 institutions and about 48 million tickets and does not itself link tickets into parent orders; persistence in institutional trading-desk cost/skill is documented with it. — [Anand, Irvine, Puckett & Venkataraman (RFS 2012)](https://cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/people.smu.edu/dist/6/414/files/2020/07/Performance-of-Institutional-Trading-Desks-An-Analysis-of-Presistence-in-Trading-Costs-2012.pdf); [Hu, Jo, Wang & Xie survey of Abel Noser data](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/80215/1/Hu_Institutional_Trading_Abel.pdf)
- **[MODERATE]** In a separate proprietary dataset of active managers, most orders execute over one or two days and in one or two transactions; Chan & Lakonishok (1995) analysed about 115,000 institutional "packages". — [Di Mascio, Lines & Naik, "Alpha Decay"](https://www.top1000funds.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/SSRN-id2580551.pdf)
- **[STRONG]** Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka: same-half-hour return continuation at daily lags for about 40 trading days, with matching periodicity in volume and order imbalance — consistent with institutions repeating a trading schedule over many days. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.3535.pdf)

### Inferences
- If about 30% of parent orders (and a much larger share of institutional dollar volume, since big orders are the ones that get split across days — an inference, not a cited number) run multi-day, then a name with persistent one-sided VWAP/TWAP-like participation today has a materially elevated chance of seeing the same side tomorrow, most visibly at the same time of day (Heston et al.).
- Combining the papers gives a usable overnight rule set: (a) a close driven by the *auction print* (MOC imbalance, index flow) tends to mean-revert by the next open; (b) a close driven by the *last 30 minutes of continuous trading at the index level* (gamma hedging) is momentum into the bell with reversal risk afterward; (c) continuation from a multi-day institutional program shows up mostly *intraday* the next day (intraday-to-intraday continuation in Lou-Polk-Skouras; same-time-of-day in Heston et al.), not necessarily in the overnight gap. A "strong close => gap up" expectation is not what this literature supports; "strong close on program buying => more intraday buying tomorrow at the same clock times" is closer to it.
- Overnight-vs-intraday clientele evidence implies the overnight leg is dominated by a different (more retail/news-driven) crowd than the intraday institutional execution flow; holding overnight is a bet on the overnight clientele and news, not on the continuation of the day's algo.
