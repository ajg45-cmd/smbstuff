## Key question 1: The three core setups and how he screens

### Takeaway
All three setups are momentum setups: a breakout from a tight consolidation after a large prior advance, an episodic pivot (a surprise-driven gap of 10% or more on heavy volume, ideally in a neglected stock), and a parabolic short (with a rarer parabolic long bounce after a crash). His screen ranks the market for the top 1-2% of performers over 1, 3 and 6 months and favours high-ADR names.

### Cited Findings
**Breakout / high tight flag**
- Prior move of roughly 30-100%+ over the past 1-3 months, then a consolidation with higher lows and tightening range, then range expansion out of it. Consolidation is usually 2 weeks to 2 months. PRIMARY via PDF copy of his post — [pdfcoffee copy of "3 Timeless Setups"](https://pdfcoffee.com/3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions-qullamaggie-pdf-free.html); [qullamaggie.com original](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- "The strongest ones find support on the 10-day, strong ones on the 20 and the slower ones on the 50-day (moving) average." SECONDARY community notes on his streams — [Qullamaggie's Laws of Swing (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/655331063/Qullamaggie-s-Laws-of-Swing)
- In bullish markets breakouts "can give you 10-20x+ your initial risk if you're good at setup selection." SECONDARY summary of his post — [Ankur Patel on X](https://x.com/AnkurPatel59/status/2011390083492970660)
- He describes risk/reward as roughly 20-30x for breakouts versus 5-10x for parabolic shorts. SECONDARY — [Breakouts Happen](https://breakoutshappen.com/stock-news/how-to-trade-like-qullamaggie-setups-strategy-and-screener)

**Screening**
- Scans for the top 1-2% of stocks ranked by performance over 1, 3 and 6 months. SECONDARY — [Snappchart](https://www.snappchart.app/blog/beginner-playbook/qullamaggie-trading-strategy); [Breakouts Happen](https://breakoutshappen.com/stock-news/how-to-trade-like-qullamaggie-setups-strategy-and-screener)
- Conflict: interview-based notes list a longer set of windows, top 2% over 1, 3, 6, 12 and 18 months. SECONDARY — [Trading Resource Hub, Chat With Traders notes part 1](https://tradingresourcehub.substack.com/p/interview-qullamaggie-chat-with-traders-part1)
- One secondary source says he filters for ADR above 5%. SECONDARY, not traced to him — [Snappchart](https://www.snappchart.app/blog/beginner-playbook/qullamaggie-trading-strategy)
- He looks at earnings and revenue growth and estimates through MarketSmith; the biggest moves often come from earnings-driven growth names. SECONDARY stream notes — [Retail Traders Repository, streams 61-65](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/qullamaggie-stream-61-65-review)

**Parabolic short and parabolic long**
- Short candidates: large caps up 50-100%+ in a few days or weeks; small caps up 300-1000%+. PRIMARY via mirror — [qullamaggie.net mirror](https://qullamaggie.net/3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/). One reviewer notes these are his examples, not fixed cutoffs — [EBC](https://www.ebc.com/forex/qullamaggie-strategy-3-trading-setups)
- Stock up 3-5+ days in a row; entry on break of the opening range low (1- or 5-minute) or first red 5-minute candle, or a failed bounce into VWAP; stop at high of day; target is the 10- and 20-day moving averages, "that's where these stocks usually bounce." Mixed PRIMARY/SECONDARY — [Stonks Capital](https://stonkscapital.substack.com/p/systemizing-kullamagis-parabolic); [qullamaggie.net mirror](https://qullamaggie.net/3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- Parabolic long: after a stock falls 50-60%+ in a few days, a counter-rally of 50-100% over a few days can follow; he calls these uncommon. PRIMARY via mirror — [qullamaggie.net mirror](https://qullamaggie.net/3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)

(Episodic pivot criteria are under Key question 4.)

### Inferences
- A large-cap stock that is about 22% below its high and has just dropped about 10% in two days matches none of the three patterns on its face. It is not in the top 1-2% of 1/3/6-month performers, it is not consolidating tightly after a 30-100% advance, and a 10% two-day drop is far short of the 50-60%+ collapse he associates with a parabolic long bounce.

### Gaps
- Exact ADR threshold in his own words was not found. The "ADR above 5%" figure is from a third-party guide.
- Exact volume requirement for breakouts (as opposed to episodic pivots) was not found in any result.
- From memory (unverified in this session, no source retrieved): his post describes the consolidation as "surfing" the rising 10- and 20-day moving averages. Check against the original.

## Key question 2: Entry, stop and position sizing

### Takeaway
He buys the break of the opening range high, puts the stop at the low of the day, will not accept a stop wider than about one ADR/ATR, and sizes from the stop distance so that the loss is roughly 0.25-1% of the account, with positions mostly 10-20% of the account and never above 30% overnight.

### Cited Findings
- "Enter opening range highs. ORH can be the highs of the first 1-, 5-, or 60-minute candle." PRIMARY — [qullamaggie.com, 3 Timeless Setups](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- "The stop is at the lows of the day." PRIMARY — [qullamaggie.com, 3 Timeless Setups](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- Stop "not wider than ATR/ADR"; another source words it as no wider than 1x ADR or ATR, and says that if the low of day is more than one ADR away the trade is skipped. SECONDARY — [Snappchart](https://www.snappchart.app/blog/beginner-playbook/qullamaggie-trading-strategy); [Breakout planner tool](https://sharepredictions.com/tools/qullamaggie-breakout-planner/)
- "If price-change on day is more than the Average True Range (ATR), skip it." (do not chase an already extended day). SECONDARY community notes — [Laws of Swing (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/892710267/Qullamaggie-s-Laws-of-Swing)
- Most positions are 10-20% of the account; risk on most trades is usually 0.25-1%. PRIMARY as reported by search summary of his blog — [qullamaggie.com, 3 Timeless Setups](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- He does not believe you should ever have more than 30% of the account overnight in any single stock or ETF. PRIMARY as reported by search summary — [qullamaggie.com, 3 Timeless Setups](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- FAQ wording quoted second-hand: risk and size depend on "the liquidity of the stock and conviction on the trade." SECONDARY quoting PRIMARY — [Timothy Sykes profile](https://www.timothysykes.com/blog/qullamaggie/)
- He buys his full position in one go, has no cap on the number of positions, and has held upwards of 30 at once. SECONDARY interview notes — [Trading Resource Hub, CWT notes part 2](https://tradingresourcehub.substack.com/p/interview-qullamaggie-chat-with-traders-part2)
- Conflict on EP stops: his blog says low of day; community notes say "If EP, calculate stop from the low of opening candle." — [qullamaggie.com](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/); [Laws of Swing (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/892710267/Qullamaggie-s-Laws-of-Swing)

### Inferences
- His sizing is defined by a price stop on the stock: shares are calculated from the distance to the low of day so that a stop-out costs 0.25-1% of the account. A long option held without a stock-price stop has its full premium at risk, so under his framework the premium paid is the risk figure to compare against the 0.25-1% range.
- His entry requires the stock to be going up on the day (through an opening range high). Buying into a two-day decline before any upside trigger is the opposite sequence.

### Gaps
- The FAQ page itself could not be opened, so the exact FAQ sizing language is second-hand.

## Key question 3: Exit and trailing rules

### Takeaway
The stated rule is to sell a third to a half after 3-5 days, move the stop to breakeven, and trail the rest with the 10-day (fast stocks) or 20-day (slower stocks) moving average, exiting on the first close below it and ignoring intraday breaches. For episodic pivots he says to build your own sell rules and mentions the 20- or 50-day.

### Cited Findings
- "You should sell 1/3 to 1/2 of the position after 3-5 days, and then move the stop to break even. The rest of the position should be trailed with the 10- or the 20-day moving average. Depends on how fast the stock is." PRIMARY quoted by a third party — [AsymTrading on X](https://x.com/AsymTrading/status/1706716710735085833?lang=en); source post [qullamaggie.com](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- "If a beginner, stick to the 10-day. You wait for the first CLOSE below the 10-day." PRIMARY as reported by search summary — [qullamaggie.com, 3 Timeless Setups](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- He usually uses the 10-day for faster-moving stocks and the 20-day for slower-moving stocks. SECONDARY transcript-based — [Trading Resource Hub, CWT transcript](https://tradingresourcehub.substack.com/p/qullamaggie-chat-with-traders-reimagined-transcript)
- He ignores intraday violations of the moving average and exits only if the stock looks set to close below it, checking 10-15 minutes before the close: "wait for the close for your trailing stop, and have that stop where you'll get out no matter what." SECONDARY transcript-based — [Trading Resource Hub, CWT transcript](https://tradingresourcehub.substack.com/p/qullamaggie-chat-with-traders-reimagined-transcript)
- Community notes give the same sequence: sell 1/3 to 1/2 after 3-5 days, move stop to breakeven, sell the rest once it closes below the 10-day. SECONDARY — [Laws of Swing (pdfcoffee)](https://pdfcoffee.com/qullamaggiex27s-laws-of-swing-pdf-free.html)
- For episodic pivots: "create your own sell rules"; "you can trail these with the 20- or 50-day moving average, or whatever you find to work the best." PRIMARY as reported by search summary — [qullamaggie.com, 3 Timeless Setups](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- A commentator's gloss, not his statement: the better the market, the less to take off and the later; in a poor market take more off sooner. SECONDARY interpretation — [AsymTrading on X](https://x.com/AsymTrading/status/1706716710735085833?lang=en)
- Parabolic shorts are covered into the 10- and 20-day moving averages. PRIMARY via mirror — [qullamaggie.net mirror](https://qullamaggie.net/3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- Market-level exit: one source says that if the 10-day turns down while he is heavily long, he sizes down and cuts margin without waiting for individual stops. SECONDARY — [Breakouts Happen](https://breakoutshappen.com/stock-news/how-to-trade-like-qullamaggie-setups-strategy-and-screener)

### Inferences
- His trail only functions for a position that is above a rising 10- or 20-day average. A stock bought after a sharp two-day drop is most likely already below those averages, so the trailing rule gives no hold signal at entry; it would only become usable after the stock reclaims and holds above the averages.
- His exits are defined on the stock's daily closes. An option holder who wants to follow him would apply the close-below-the-average test to the underlying stock, not to the option price. This is an inference; no statement from him on managing options this way was found.
- The initial exit in his system is the low-of-day stop on day one, which caps the loss at a small fraction of the account before any trailing begins.

### Gaps
- No explicit rule was found for trimming a long that becomes extended far above its averages, other than the 3-5 day partial sale and his separate parabolic-short setup.
- No statement found on when he prefers the 50-day for breakouts; the 50-day appears only in the EP passage and in the "slower ones" note.
- The blog page was not opened, so whether the 1/3-1/2 and 3-5 day rule is written under breakouts only or also under EPs could not be confirmed.

## Key question 4: Episodic pivots and anticipated events versus surprises

### Takeaway
An episodic pivot is defined by surprise: unexpected good news in a neglected stock that has not rallied in the prior 3-6 months, confirmed by a 10%+ gap on volume large enough to trade a normal day's volume in the first 15-30 minutes. He buys after the market reacts (opening range high on the gap day), not before the news.

### Cited Findings
- "When unexpected good news hits a stock, especially if that stock is neglected, that can trigger multi-month, and multi-year moves." PRIMARY — [qullamaggie.com, 3 Timeless Setups](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- Requirements listed: "Gap up 10%+" and "Big volume"; volume should show in premarket or at the open, and the best examples often trade their average daily volume in the first 15-30 minutes. PRIMARY as reported by search summary — [qullamaggie.com, 3 Timeless Setups](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- For earnings EPs: "There has to be big growth numbers, preferably mid/high or even triple digit EPS and revenue growth and a significant beat to analyst expectations." PRIMARY — [qullamaggie.com, 3 Timeless Setups](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- "It's best if the stock has not rallied over the past 3-6 months." A big earlier move makes the gap less of a surprise. PRIMARY as reported by search summary — [qullamaggie.com, 3 Timeless Setups](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- "A significant earnings surprise will lead to a rally, if the surprise is not priced in to current price." Attributed by the search summary to a Qullamaggie-branded site; qullamaggie.net is a mirror whose authorship I could not confirm — [qullamaggie.net, 2023-12-26](https://qullamaggie.net/2023/12/26/)
- Three-element framing (neglect, catalyst, gap with volume); the more neglect, the bigger the possible swing. This framing originates with Pradeep Bonde, who coined the term. SECONDARY — [Tradingsim](https://tradingsim.com/blog/episodic-pivot-power-earnings-gap-buyable-gap-up-explained/); [Pradeep Bonde notes](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/pradeep-bonde-episodic-pivots)
- Catalysts described in secondary write-ups: earnings surprises, product launches, major news. SECONDARY — [FinancialWisdomTV](https://www.financialwisdomtv.com/post/the-episodic-pivot-strategy-qullamaggie-s-high-momentum-setup-explained)
- A stock that has already rallied 100% in the prior three months is no longer a surprise when it gaps. SECONDARY figure, not his — [Qullamaggie Compass](https://qullamaggiecompass.com/episodic-pivot)
- Entry is the opening range high on the gap day. PRIMARY — [qullamaggie.com, 3 Timeless Setups](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- Independent backtest: the EP showed a small real edge (about +0.15 to +0.20R before costs) in small and mid caps; the daily-bar breakout showed none. SECONDARY, a third party's test — [Quant for Free](https://quant4free.com/analysis/qullamaggie-strategy-stress-test/)

### Inferences
- A scheduled, heavily publicised product launch is the opposite of "unexpected good news." Under his definition the launch date itself is not the catalyst; a qualifying EP would be a later surprise (for example sales or guidance far above expectations) that produces a 10%+ gap on outsized volume, and his entry would come on that gap day.
- A position opened the day before a known event has no gap, no volume confirmation and no opening range high, so it cannot be classified as an EP at the time of purchase.
- The "not rallied in the past 3-6 months" preference is about the stock being ignored and basing. A stock that is falling sharply into the event is weak, which is different from neglected and flat; whether he would treat a pre-event sell-off as favourable "neglect" is not addressed in any source found.

### Gaps
- No direct statement from him on product launches as a category of catalyst was found; "product launches" appears only in third-party lists.
- No direct statement found on widely anticipated scheduled events specifically. The surprise requirement is the closest evidence.

## Key question 5: Stated views on specific practices

### Takeaway
He trades stock, not options, and says he does not need more leverage than broker margin. He avoids longs when the 10- and 20-day averages are falling, holds through earnings only with a profit cushion and after a partial sale, and no statement was found supporting purchases ahead of a known catalyst.

### Cited Findings
**Options and leverage**
- In a stream transcript he says he trades regular stocks and sometimes triple-leveraged ETFs, that options "are not for me," and that he does not need extra leverage. PRIMARY transcript on a third-party upload — [All Qullamaggie stream transcripts (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/748079660/All-Qullamaggie-streams-transcripts)
- He tried options and found them not suited to him; the note-taker speculates he had less edge there. Regular broker margin (about 400% intraday, 100% overnight) was described as more than sufficient. SECONDARY stream notes; the "less edge" reason is the note-taker's guess — [Retail Traders Repository, Oct 2019 streams](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/kristjan-kullamagi-qullamaggie-stream-d8c)

**Earnings**
- "I only hold through earnings if I have a good profit on the stock and have sold a partial for profit already." PRIMARY tweet — [Qullamaggie on X](https://x.com/Qullamaggie/status/1587130234574356485)
- In a 2019 stream he said he was not going to hold through earnings; a 2020 review says he sometimes holds when in the money and expecting a good report. SECONDARY stream notes showing his practice varied — [Retail Traders Repository, streams 61-65](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/qullamaggie-stream-61-65-review)

**Downtrends and moving averages**
- Avoid long trades when the 10-day moving average is sloping down, when a stock has broken below its 20-day, or when both are sloping down. SECONDARY compilation of his "Swing School" videos — [Swing School compilation summary](https://videohighlight.com/v/WFfGHO25oYY?mediaType=youtube&language=en&summaryType=default&summaryId=b7joQocBsSRa8p-RVClQ&aiFormatted=false)

**Market regime**
- Market filter: 10-day moving average above the 20-day and both rising (on QQQ or the index) means take long setups; below means stand aside or cut size. SECONDARY, consistent across several write-ups — [Breakouts Happen](https://breakoutshappen.com/stock-news/how-to-trade-like-qullamaggie-setups-strategy-and-screener); [Snappchart](https://www.snappchart.app/blog/beginner-playbook/qullamaggie-trading-strategy)
- He often says it is fine to sit out and not trade. SECONDARY — [Wall Street Trader Substack](https://wallstreettrader.substack.com/p/qullamaggies-trading-playbook-speed)
- One profile says that in a later period he ignored his own 10/20-day filter and kept taking breakouts and EPs through a downtrending market for months. SECONDARY, single source — [Complete Traders Edge](https://completetradersedge.com/kristjan-kullamagi-qullamaggie/)

### Inferences
- The option position conflicts with his stated practice on instrument alone. His leverage comes from margin on stock with a tight price stop, which carries no time decay and no strike to clear.
- Buying a stock that has just broken down about 10% in two days falls inside the "avoid longs when the stock has broken below its 20-day" guidance, assuming the stock is below its 10- and 20-day averages (to be confirmed by whoever holds the chart data).
- His earnings rule shows a general principle: he carries event risk only with a profit cushion and a partial already sold. A new, full position opened the day before a binary event has neither.

### Gaps
- No statement from him on time decay, LEAPS, strikes or out-of-the-money calls was found. The only evidence is the general "options are not for me."
- No direct statement on averaging down was found. His published process (full position at once, hard stop at low of day) leaves no room for it, but that is inference.
- No direct statement on large caps versus high-ADR names was found beyond the ADR screen and the large-cap parabolic threshold. Third-party guides say he favours high-ADR stocks.
- No direct statement on buying before a known catalyst was found.
- The "Swing School" avoidance rules come from an auto-generated video summary, the weakest source type here.
