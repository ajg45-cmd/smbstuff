Which exit keeps you in the trend?

Entry is a VWAP continuation, taken by hand. The exit is the part being automated — and the part actually being studied.

Given a VWAP continuation entry, which exit logic keeps a trend trader in the trend — and in what universe is that advantage significant?

That is a better-posed question than "does this setup have an edge," and it is worth saying why. Comparing exits on the same trades is a paired design: the entry noise that swamps an absolute-EV study cancels in the difference between two rules on the same trade. A paired comparison resolves an effect roughly an order of magnitude smaller than two independent samples of the same size — so most of the sample-size problem disappears.

The one caveat that has to stay in view

Exit rankings are conditional on the entry having positive expectancy. If entry EV ≤ 0, the optimal exit degenerates to "exit immediately," and a leaderboard of trailing rules is ranking ways to lose more slowly. So entry EV > 0 still has to be established — but it comes free from the same dataset, and it is a low bar, not the study.

Section one — the given

The entry, held fixed

A 15-min (or 5-min) bar that reaches session VWAP and closes back in the direction of the trend, inside the window, in a name that is already trending. Held constant so the exits are what vary.

The touch is judged minute by minute

VWAP moves inside the bar. Testing a low made at 11:03 against the line as it stood at 11:15 invents signals that never existed. The check runs against the concurrent VWAP, minute by minute.

Wick through, or come close

It does not have to print the line. Trading through and holding counts; so does coming within 0.10 × daily ATR. Both are logged separately so the difference stays testable.

The fill is the close, never the touch

You only know the bar closed strong once it has closed. Entering at the touch price is the single biggest source of fake edge in reversal-bar studies.

Trading the first signal dissolves the clustering problem

A trending name touches VWAP three or four times a session, and those signals share a catalyst and overlapping forward paths — one observation wearing several hats. Taking only the first gives one observation per name per day, which is what the statistics want anyway. The later ones are still logged, so "is the second one worth taking?" stays answerable instead of assumed.

Count these before committing

5M shares by 11:00 is a genuinely restrictive bar. It selects the true Stocks In Play — which is the intent — but it could also leave you with very few signals. Count how many survive it in Phase 0 before locking it in. Cumulative dollars is logged alongside so the swap costs nothing.

Section three — the crossed axis

5-minute and 15-minute are two questions, not one

The entry frame and the exit frame are separate choices, and they cross.

The off-diagonal cells are the interesting ones, and the bottom-right is the hypothesis your problem statement implies: enter on structure, hold on structure, and do not trail tighter than the frame you entered on.

A trailing rule running on a faster frame than the entry exits on noise the entry timeframe does not even register as a pullback. That is the mechanical reason a 5-minute EMA9 trail shakes a 15-minute trend trader out — and it is what your current hot key does.

Same rule, same trades, different frame 0.94R  vs  2.28R EMA9 trail on 5-min bars against EMA9 on 15-min bars, scored on identical entries. The tighter frame cut the trend in most of them.

Read that number correctly

It comes from hand-built synthetic sessions in the test suite, built to exercise the machinery. It demonstrates the mechanism; it is not evidence about your names or your market. It is the first thing the real dataset should be asked about — not a result to act on.

Section four — the rule set

Fifty rules, one engine

Every rule runs through the same engine, so the only difference between two rows of the leaderboard is the rule itself. Within-bar ordering is deliberately pessimistic: adverse levels are assumed hit before favorable ones, because on a 5- or 15-minute bar you cannot know the sequence, and the optimistic assumption is how backtests manufacture edge.

The benchmark is the point

hold_to_close — initial stop, hold to the bell, do nothing else — is scored on every trade. A trailing rule that cannot beat it is costing money to feel busy. Traders almost never check this, and it is the cheapest way for this study to pay for itself.

Section five — interpretation

Reading the leaderboard honestly

Each rule reports mean R, median, win rate, p90, percentage of available MFE captured, and the paired difference against the benchmark with a cluster-bootstrapped interval.

  1. Rank on the paired difference, not on mean R

    Mean R is dominated by which trades happened to land in the sample. The difference against the benchmark is not — that is the whole advantage of scoring every rule on identical entries.

  2. Pick the plateau, not the peak

    If EMA9 wins while EMA5 and EMA21 are poor, that is a fitted parameter. If all three beat the benchmark and 9 is best, that is a real effect with a best setting.

  3. Fifty rules is fifty tests

    The top row of a leaderboard is the maximum of fifty noisy numbers and is biased upward by construction. Apply Benjamini–Hochberg, log every variant tried, and take the winner's honest edge from a held-out period — never from the row that won.

  4. Cluster by day, not by trade

    Signals sharing a name and a day share a catalyst and overlapping paths. Treating them as independent shrinks every interval by roughly the square root of signals per day — which is how a study convinces itself of an edge that is not there.

The bar to clear

The winner beats the benchmark in most slices, and its advantage does not reverse in any of them. A rule that is +0.4R in strong trends and −0.4R in weak ones is not an exit rule — it is a trend filter you have not written down yet.

On the volume hypothesis

Kept, demoted. Both the raw ratio and the clock-normalized one are logged on every signal, but volume dry-up is now a conditioning variable — one of the slices above — rather than the headline. The RVOL-relative version stays parked until there is a reason to build it.

The reason to keep normalizing at all: volume falls from 10:30 to 12:00 on every stock every day, so raw dry-up will look present in most of the sample. As a slice that only matters if it turns out to separate the exits — which is now something the report answers rather than assumes.

Go / no-go

Keeping it to the bar you set — prove it, then improve it. Three things, all required:

  • The entry is worth trading at all. Mean end-of-day R > 0, cluster-bootstrapped interval excluding zero, after costs.
  • The exit work paid for itself. The best rule beats hold-to-close by a margin whose paired interval excludes zero, holds in most slices, and sits on a parameter plateau.
  • It survives the honesty checks. Holds on a period held out from the rule search, and does not vanish once fifty tests are corrected for.

Anything short of all three is a lead, not a result.

Section seven — inputs

What this actually needs

The good news: the exit lab needs almost no exotic data. Tell me which of these KTG already exposes and I'll write the loader against it — I can't inspect its interfaces from here.

Minimum to start — nothing else required

  1. 1-minute OHLCV including premarket

    For your in-play names, as much history as you can get. 5-min and 15-min are resampled from it — no separate feed.

  2. Daily OHLCV for ATR

    Trivial from anywhere.

  3. A symbol/date list of names that were in play

    For a first pass this can just be the names you traded or watched. The exit question does not need a clean universe definition to produce a first answer.

Verify this before trusting any of it

Confirm the computed VWAP matches the line on your screen. Session VWAP differs between platforms on two choices — whether it starts at 9:30 or includes premarket, and which venues' prints are included. If the backtest's VWAP is not the line you were looking at, every touch is measured against the wrong level and the study answers a question you never asked.

Section eight — remaining

Still open

The ten blocking questions are answered. These are the ones the data now has to settle, roughly in the order they matter.

Immediate

  1. How many signals survive the 5M-share filter? If it is a handful a month, the threshold moves before anything else does.
  2. Is entry EV > 0 at all? Everything downstream is conditional on it.
  3. Does the computed VWAP match KTG's line, tick for tick, on a sample of days you remember?
  4. Which of the three minimum data inputs does KTG already give you, and how far back?

The exit question

  1. Does any trailing rule beat hold-to-close after costs?
  2. Does the winner sit on a plateau, or is it a lone spike?
  3. Does the entry-frame / exit-frame pairing behave as the 2×2 predicts?
  4. What fraction of the available MFE does the winner capture — and does a hybrid (scale at 1R, trail the rest) beat a pure rule?
  5. Does the best exit differ between longs and shorts? Prior: yes, and by more than you would expect.
  6. Is there a time of day after which every rule underperforms holding?

Once there is a result

  1. Does the second signal of the day deserve to be traded, or only the first?
  2. Does the window want widening past 12:00, and in which direction?
  3. Does the volume slice separate the exits, or was it never load-bearing?
  4. Does the winning rule survive on a period held out from the search?
  5. What does the rule cost in slippage at your real size, and does it still win after that?

Then, and only then

  1. Port the winner into the hot key, replacing the fixed EMA9-on-5-min trail.
  2. Start the forward paper log — the only genuinely out-of-sample data you will ever have. It costs nothing today and is the most credible evidence you own in six months.
  3. Re-run the whole leaderboard on forward data at 3 and 6 months. If forward expectancy is under half the backtest, the backtest was fitted.
