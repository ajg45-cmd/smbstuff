The midday reset

A research plan for one question: is the second leg of a buy program a real, tradeable edge — or a story we tell about charts we already know worked?

A day-1 earnings name that gapped, held the gap, and cleared the premarket high in the first hour; that then makes an orderly first pullback into a reference level between 10:30 and 12:00, on volume that is drier than the midday clock alone would explain; and that then resumes with a volume expansion through the pullback high.

You are buying the second leg of an accumulation program at the point where the day's supply has been demonstrated absent. Risk is the pullback low. Size is a function of how many confirmations stack — the largest single upgrade being that today is also a breakout from a multi-month range.

Section one — mechanism

Why it should work

A large buyer does not fill in one push. They work an order in legs — pausing when price gets away from them, resuming when it comes back to their level.

The pause is the signal

Price drifting on falling volume means the buyer stepped off the bid — not that a seller arrived. A pullback where volume expands is distribution, and you do not want it.

The first hour clears supply

Gap-fill sellers and overnight holders transact 9:30–10:30. Clearing the premarket high is the evidence they were absorbed — which is why a VWAP pullback below PMH is a different, worse trade.

Midday flow is programmatic

Retail and news reaction concentrate early. What is left at 11:15 skews toward schedule-driven institutional orders — exactly the participant the setup tries to detect.

Section two — the reframe

Your hypothesis has a hole in it

Read this before writing any code

Volume declines from 10:30 to 12:00 on every stock, every day. That is the daily volume trough, not a signal. If you measure raw declining volume on the pullback, you will confirm your hypothesis on most of the sample and learn nothing at all.

The hypothesis is only testable once volume is normalized against the clock.

Build a baseline intraday volume curve — for each symbol, the median volume per minute-of-day over the trailing 20 sessions. Then the variable that carries the hypothesis is the ratio of what happened to what the clock alone predicts.

Raw ratio pvr_raw Mean volume/min during the pullback ÷ mean volume/min during the impulse. Nearly always < 1. Tells you it is lunchtime.
What the clock predicts pvr_expected The same ratio computed from the baseline curve alone — the decline you would get from a flat, featureless day.
The actual variable — H1 nPVR = raw ÷ expected Below 1 means drier than the clock explains. That is the real signal. Around 1 means nothing happened that the time of day does not already account for.

And one confound that will fool you even after normalizing

nPVR is mechanically correlated with pullback depth and duration — a shallow three-bar pullback has low volume almost by construction. Test the hypothesis on the residual after regressing out depth, duration and time of day, or within depth buckets. Skip this and you will discover that shallow pullbacks work, and publish it to yourself as a volume finding.

Pre-registered — do not edit after seeing results

ElementCommitment
H1Bottom nPVR tercile produces a mean MFE_60 at least 0.30R higher than the top tercile, controlling for depth and duration.
TestPermutation, 10,000 shuffles. Significance at p < 0.05.
Falsified ifSpread < 0.15R, or p > 0.05, or the spread vanishes under the depth/duration control.
Everything elseExploratory. Generates the hypothesis you pre-register next — it does not get to be a conclusion from this dataset.

Section three — definition

Rules a scanner can run

Written so code picks the candidates, not you. Every threshold is a prior to be swept, not a finding.

The six stages

  1. Universe

    Day-1 earnings, gap ≥ 3%, price ≥ $5, 20-day ADDV ≥ $50M. Long only for v1.

  2. Open quality

    An Open Strength Score from four components. Hard gate: a 15-min bar closed above the premarket high, and no 15-min close below VWAP.

  3. Leg structure

    ATR-scaled zigzag on 15-min bars. Impulse ≥ 1 ATR; pullback retraces 20–70% over 2–6 bars, holding a higher low.

  4. Reset level

    Holds within 0.35 ATR of VWAP, PMH, opening-range high, prior-day high, the 15-min EMA9, or a round number. Record which — level type is a predictor, not decoration.

  5. Trigger

    Break of the last down-closing pullback bar's high, before 12:00, on ≥1.5× the pullback's mean volume. Dry-up without expansion is a non-event.

  6. Risk

    Stop 0.10 ATR below the pullback low. Skip if R > 2.5% — a stop that wide means you are chasing, not at the level.

Three timeframes, not one

TierFrameJob
ContextDailyRange expansion, ATR, float rotation
Structure15-minLegs, pullback, trigger
Execution1-minVWAP, volume ratios, the MFE/MAE path

Why not 30-min: the window is only three 30-minute bars. Three bars cannot express impulse → pullback → trigger. 15-min gives six, the minimum viable resolution.

But make it a tested parameter. Run everything at 5 / 10 / 15 / 20 / 30-min. The requirement is not that 15 wins — it is that the edge forms a plateau across neighbours. An edge that exists at 15 and vanishes at 10 and 20 is a fitting artifact.

Your liquidity filter is a look-ahead

"5 million traded on the day" is not knowable at 10:30. Split it into three point-in-time filters:

  • Pre-open: 20-day ADDV ≥ $50M
  • At trigger: cumulative session $ volume ≥ $25M
  • Capacity: median 1-min $ volume over the prior 15 min ≥ $300k

The third is the one that decides whether you get filled, and the one everyone omits.

No catalyst, it is a chart not a trade. PMH not cleared, there is supply above you. Volume only as dry as the clock explains, you have observed nothing. No expansion on the trigger, nobody showed up.

On RVOL — your instinct needs restating

You chose day-1 earnings because they guarantee RVOL. That means every name in the sample is 5–30× and the variable has no variance left to explain anything with. A null on RVOL here is range restriction, not evidence. Use float rotation instead — it is the actual mechanism behind "RVOL matters on low float," and it varies inside this population.

Section five — proof

Four Monte Carlos, not one

They answer four different questions. Running one and calling it "the Monte Carlo" is the standard mistake.

MC-1 · Block bootstrap

What does a normal bad stretch look like?

Resample realized R-multiples in blocks of 5–10 — not iid, because trades cluster by day, theme and regime, and iid flatters your drawdowns. Output: drawdown quantiles, longest losing streak, P(negative year).

This is the one that keeps you in the trade. On a synthetic 0.17R edge over 400 trades, the 90th-percentile losing streak runs 16 trades. Knowing that in advance is worth more to your P&L than the significance test.

MC-2 · Permutation

Is the signal real?

Hold the candidate set fixed, shuffle nPVR across it 10,000 times, recompute the tercile spread. p is the fraction of shuffles that beat what you observed. Robust to the fat-tailed return distribution in a way a t-test is not.

MC-3 · Random-entry control

Is the setup real, or just the population?

Null A: same symbol, same day, random entry minute in the window, same ATR-scaled stop. Null B: the same rules on random non-earnings days — that isolates how much is the catalyst.

The control everyone skips and the one most likely to kill the project. If the setup does not beat Null A, you have discovered that day-1 earnings gappers drift up — not that the reset works. Run it in Phase 1, not at the end.

MC-4 · Risk of ruin

What can you actually bet?

Sweep risk-per-trade from 0.25% to 3%. The size that maximizes the median outcome and the size where ruin risk becomes unacceptable are far apart — the gap is the answer. Then check whether probe/A/A+ at 15/30/80% of stop is justified or aspirational.

What the sample size honestly buys you

Effect to detectNeeded per group
Δ = 0.20R~880
Δ = 0.30R~390
Δ = 0.34R~300
Δ = 0.50R~140

Assuming per-trade σ ≈ 1.5R, at 80% power. If Phase 0 yields ~900 candidates, terciles give you 300 per group — enough to detect a large effect in one pre-registered hypothesis.

You cannot resolve an RVOL × float interaction at that size; interactions need roughly 4× the sample of main effects. Be prepared for that question to be unanswerable here, and to answer it with the forward log instead.

Apply costs before quoting any expectancy

Many intraday edges live entirely inside the cost model. Half the spread each way, impact proportional to your share of one minute's volume, plus commission. Report expectancy at 10%, 25% and 50% of a minute's volume — if it only survives at 10%, that is your capacity ceiling and it belongs in the headline, not a footnote.

Section six — discretion

The eye test, built so it means something

An eye test bolted on after you know the outcomes is not a filter — it is a way of laundering hindsight into rules. Built blind, it does something much better: it turns your discretion into a measured variable.

The protocol

  1. The scanner picks, you don't

    You never choose which charts to look at.

  2. Render truncated at the trigger bar

    Slice the dataframe before it reaches the plot — not just an axis limit. A visible axis extending past the trigger leaks the outcome, and you cannot un-see it.

  3. Grade before the reveal

    Cleanliness 1–5, level grade 1–10, take/pass, size band, one line of reasoning.

  4. Decoys, unlabeled

    20% near-misses that failed one filter, 10% random midday entries. Without them every chart on a pre-filtered list looks like a setup.

What it lets you answer

Question one Does your eye add anything? Fit mechanical features alone vs. mechanical + your grade; compare out-of-sample. If the grade adds nothing, you can automate with a clear conscience.
Question two — do this first Do you two mean the same thing by "clean"? Both grade the same 200 charts independently; compute Cohen's κ. Two evenings of work, and it may reveal you have been testing two different setups.
Question three What is your eye actually seeing? Regress the grade on the mechanical features. Whatever loads highest — bar overlap, widest down bar, level confluence — is your pattern recognition, written down and made computable.
Question four Are you calibrated? When you say A+, what is the realized hit rate versus A? If they match, your bands carry no information yet and sizing 80% is variance, not earned right.

Section seven — sequence

Six phases, each able to kill it cheaply

Phase 0 is the highest-value work here and the part most likely to be skipped in enthusiasm. Counting candidates and rendering charts, before any model exists, is what tells you whether there is a business.

PhaseDeliverableKill criterion
0Data pipeline, scanner, candidate table, chart images. No modeling.< 8 candidates/month, or capacity too small to matter
1H1 tested, plus MC-2 and MC-3Fails MC-3 Null A
2Exit surface — the EMA9 hot key scored against the alternatives—
3Enhancer scorecard fit, MC-1 and MC-4, sizing bands—
4Catalyst layer: implied move, guidance, theme breadth—
5Forward paper log reviewed at 3 and 6 monthsForward expectancy < half the backtest

Start the forward log on day one

It runs in parallel with everything else and costs nothing today. In six months it is the most credible evidence you will own — the only data that is genuinely out of sample.

This closes the loop on the hot key

Because the study logs the full forward path rather than one exit, Phase 2 scores EMA9-on-5-min against EMA9-on-15-min, the ATR-buffered variant, VWAP loss, trailing swing lows, fixed 2R and time stops — on the same trades. Every v2 upgrade you sketched is a parameter this dataset can settle instead of a guess.

Section eight — the list

Questions to answer

The first ten block everything downstream. Answer them in one sitting together and write the answers into the setup definition — the rest can be answered by the data, but only once these have made the questions well-posed.

Answer these first

  • "$5 million traded" — shares or dollars, and measured when?As written it is a look-ahead. Does the three-part replacement above match what you mean?
  • What is a "strong open," as a number you would accept before seeing the outcome?Held VWAP all hour? Closed the 10:30 bar in the top third? Pick the definition; let the sweep set the threshold.
  • Is clearing the premarket high a hard gate or an enhancer?As a gate it may halve the sample — which decides whether the study is powered at all.
  • Must the pullback hold VWAP, or may it wick through and reclaim?A wick-and-reclaim is a materially different and often better trade. You cannot pool them and then interpret the result.
  • Is 10:30–12:00 a hard window or the center of a distribution?Suggestion: collect wide, log trigger time as a feature, let the data draw the edges. Filtering first throws away the evidence you would need to set the filter.
  • "Second leg" — of what, exactly?Including the opening drive, or the first pullback after the PMH clear regardless of 9:30? This is ambiguous in every description of the setup.
  • Long only for v1?The short mirror has different borrow, squeeze and volume mechanics. Do not pool them.
  • What is your data budget?Premarket 1-min history across a 6-year universe is the binding constraint on the whole project. Answer before writing another line of code.
  • One trade at a time, or a portfolio?If three candidates trigger at 11:05 on the same theme, do you take all three? This changes MC-1, MC-4, and what "a trade" even means in the results.
  • What is the smallest edge you would actually trade?Write it down now. Decided afterward, it becomes whatever you happen to find.

A · Setup definition

  1. Must the impulse originate at the open, or can it start from a 10:00 base?
  2. The first pullback of the day, or the first after the PMH clear?
  3. Minimum bars in the pullback — is one 15-min down bar a reset?
  4. If it grinds sideways instead of pulling back, is that the same setup? A time-based reset may behave nothing like a price-based one.
  5. What invalidates it before the trigger, and after how many bars of chop do you stop watching?
  6. Trigger on a close above the pullback high, or a trade through it? Trade-through fills better, close confirms better.
  7. Is there a maximum extension from VWAP beyond which you are chasing?
  8. Trigger, stop, re-set, trigger again — one trade or two?

B · The volume hypothesis

  1. Do you accept that raw declining volume into midday is near-universal, so nPVR is the variable and not raw volume?
  2. Baseline from the stock's own 20 sessions, or a cross-sectional day-1 composite? Own history is cleaner but often unrepresentative because yesterday was a normal day.
  3. Dry-up relative to the impulse leg, or to the whole session so far?
  4. How much of what you call dry-up is really just shallow and short?
  5. Is the ideal signature low total volume, or collapsing down-volume with persistent up-volume? The latter needs trade-level data — a budget decision.
  6. Does expansion on the trigger matter more than dry-up on the pullback?
  7. Is there a level of dry-up that is too dry — where the name is simply dead?
  8. Volume in shares, dollars, or trade count? Trade count is least distorted by a single block.
  9. Exclude block prints and odd lots? One 500k print inside a quiet pullback wrecks the ratio — and may be the most informative event in the window.

C · Timeframe

  1. Does the edge form a plateau across 5/10/15/20/30-min, or a spike at one? A spike means you fit it.
  2. Should structure and trigger share a timeframe, or structure on 15-min and trigger on the 5-min break of it?
  3. Does the best timeframe scale with the stock's ADR?
  4. Is 30-min usable at all inside a 90-minute window?

D · RVOL, float, supply

  1. Do you accept that RVOL is range-restricted here, so a null is not evidence it does not matter?
  2. Is float rotation the variable you actually mean by "RVOL matters on low float"?
  3. Which float buckets — <20M / 20–75M / 75–300M / >300M?
  4. Is your float data point-in-time or a current snapshot applied to 2019? Almost certainly the latter — document it rather than pretend.
  5. Does short interest add anything beyond float, or is it collinear?
  6. Will you run a second study on non-earnings names to get the RVOL variance the question needs?
  7. Does a $2B name behave like a $200B one — should mega-caps be excluded as a different regime?

E · Range expansion and sizing

  1. Which range is "longer-term" — 20, 60, or 252 days?
  2. Is it the break that matters, or the tightness of the base it broke out of?
  3. Does expectancy scale with the timeframe of the range broken? If yes, that is your sizing rule — derived, not guessed.
  4. Is an all-time high its own category or the extreme of the same scale?
  5. How far above the range is too far — when is the expansion already spent?
  6. Must the size-up condition hold at the trigger, or does a later break still upgrade it?
  7. Do probe/A/A+ at 15/30/80% survive contact with the measured conditional expectancies?

F · Entry, stop, exit

  1. Stop below the pullback low or below the reset level? Different trades, different R.
  2. What is the widest R you will accept before the location is simply wrong?
  3. Does the best exit depend on the enhancer stack? An A+ range-expansion day should probably be held very differently from a probe.
  4. How does the EMA9 5-min hot key score against the alternatives on this candidate set?
  5. Should the exit switch timeframes once you are up X ATR — the 5-EMA failover? Test it, do not assume it.
  6. Is there a time stop? What does the MFE-versus-time curve look like — does it stop working at 13:00?
  7. Do you hold overnight when it is also a multi-month breakout? That is likely where the real money is.
  8. Does a 15:30 cutoff improve expectancy, or cut the tail that pays for everything?
  9. Scale out or all-or-nothing? Scaling smooths the curve and lowers expectancy — worth it at your size?

G · The eye test

  1. Will you commit to grading blind, with decoys, before seeing outcomes?
  2. How many charts can you realistically grade per week? That sets the pace of everything discretionary.
  3. Will you both grade the same 200 charts independently, so agreement can be measured?
  4. If your grade adds no out-of-sample power, will you accept that and automate?
  5. If it adds a lot, will you accept that the scanner's job is triage, not selection?

H · Statistics

  1. Will you actually keep a log of every threshold, feature and timeframe tried? Without it you cannot know how much you have overfit — and the count is always higher than memory suggests.
  2. Will you hold 2025 genuinely untouched until the end?
  3. What is the minimum candidate count below which you will not interpret a result? Suggest 30 per cell, no interaction claims under 300.
  4. Block bootstrap or iid for MC-1?
  5. Risk of ruin against a fixed account or a compounding one?
  6. What drawdown would make you stop trading it? Compare that to MC-1's distribution — if your pain threshold sits inside the edge's normal range, that is a sizing problem, not a signal problem.
  7. Are you willing to kill the project on MC-3 Null A?

I · Execution reality

  1. What is your actual fill quality on a 15-min break at 11:15 in a $60 stock — measured from your own fills, not assumed?
  2. What size can you take without moving it?
  3. Does the edge survive at 25% of a minute's volume, or only at 10%?
  4. Manual, alert-driven, or automated? The latency difference is worth real basis points at this timeframe.
  5. Does your existing hot key infrastructure support entry automation, or only exits?
  6. How many candidates can you monitor at once without degrading execution on any of them?

J · The catalyst layer

  1. Does gap size relative to the options-implied move beat gap size alone? The cleanest quantitative proxy for "the Street was surprised."
  2. Does the type of surprise matter — beat-and-raise vs. beat-only vs. revenue-beat-EPS-miss?
  3. Do analyst revisions in the 24h after the print add anything?
  4. Does theme breadth matter — how many names in the sector are also in play?
  5. Does it work on non-earnings day-1 catalysts (FDA, guidance, M&A), or is the earnings mechanism specific?
  6. Does the same reset work on Day 2 and Day 3 with decaying edge? If so, that is a natural sample-size multiplier.

K · Scope and process

  1. What is the one result that would make you trade this tomorrow?
  2. What is the one result that would make you drop it entirely?
  3. Who owns which half — data and code, versus chart grading and rules?
  4. What is the review cadence, and what gets written down each time?
  5. Are you prepared for the most likely outcome — a real but modest edge of 0.2–0.4R, highly conditional on two or three enhancers, needing the range-expansion filter to be worth sizing? That is what these studies usually find, and it is a good outcome, not a failure.
