TWAP · VWAP · buy and sell programs · your turns, trend days and low floats

You can't see the program. You can see which state the stock is in.

Execution programs are real, slow and mostly passive. The research proves they leave traces, and nothing in it shows anyone can identify one on a public tape in real time, size it, or know when it ends. What is documented is how one-sided flow behaves while it lasts and after it stops. Your own records rank the same transitions.

What you can trade around

  • One-sided flow that lasts minutes to hours is mostly one participant splitting an order, and it persists. DOCUMENTED
  • When the order finishes, about a third of its impact comes back by the same close. DOCUMENTED
  • About 30% of parent orders run past one day and tend to reappear at the same half-hour. DOCUMENTED
  • Closing-auction moves revert overnight. Late continuous-session moves revert about five times less. DOCUMENTED

What the public tape cannot tell you

  • Who is trading, or what kind of participant it is.
  • The total size of the order, or how much is left.
  • Whether it is one parent order or several unrelated ones.
  • Whether the benchmark is VWAP, TWAP, percent of volume or arrival price.
  • When it ends.
  • Whether a held level is one order or many.

    The state predicts the next leg, yet every mechanical long built on the hold lost, and adding back after a flush lost in every variant (−0.07R to −0.24R). Use it to stay in or size up a winner you already hold.

    20 of 33 best turns stopped you first

    Of the turns that closed beyond VWAP, 20 took out your stop before working. Split orders persist for hours, so the first flush low often breaks. Size has to leave room for a second entry.

    A flag in your checklist

    You ask for a stretch of 1.5 ATR or more. In the 95-turn sample, moves of 1 to 1.5 ATR won 75% (+0.26R, n=20) and moves above 1.5 ATR won 39% (−0.27R, n=23). The two measures differ, so recheck before changing the rule.
